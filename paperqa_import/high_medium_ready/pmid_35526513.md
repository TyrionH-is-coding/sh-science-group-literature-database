# ABSTRACT

Background: Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is an autoimmune disorder characterized by the presence of antiphospholipid antibodies (aPLs) and thrombotic events. The association of aPLs with thrombotic events depends on the number of positive tests. Besides the three classical tests to classify APS, phosphatidylserine/prothrombin complex autoantibodies (aPS/PT) are increasingly used to better define this condition. The aim of this systematic review was to evaluate the prevalence of aPS/PT in general and according to antiphospholipid antibody profiles in patients with APS.

Methods: A systematic search of PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library from January 1990 to September 2021 was carried out according to PRISMA guidelines. Proportions and $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using random-effects model. Publication biases were evaluated via visualization of funnel plots along with Egger's and Begg's tests.

Results: Twenty-one articles about the prevalence of aPS/PT in 1853 patients with APS were deemed eligible and analyzed according to the inclusion criteria. Pooled prevalence of aPS/PT IgG alone, IgM alone, and IgG/M were $50.0\%$ $45.0\%$ ,and $65.0\%$ , respectively. No significant publication bias was detected from funnel plots or Egger's and Begg's tests. When the prevalence of aPS/PT was calculated in homogeneous aPLs, a much higher rate of pooled prevalence of aPS/PT IgG/M in patients positive for Lupus Anticoagulant $(84.5\%)$ and in those with triple positivity $(83.4\%)$ was found.

Conclusions: These data show a high rate of aPS/PT positivity in patients with APS (especially in those positive for LAC) but further studies are needed to ascertain whether this test might be useful in the laboratory classification criteria of APS.

# 1. Introduction

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is an autoimmune disorder characterized by recurrent arterial and/or venous thrombotic events and recurrent fetal loss in the presence of persistently positive antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) [1-4].

Although the 2006 revised international laboratory criteria for APS are limited to lupus anticoagulant (LAC), anti-cardiolipin (aCL) and anti $\beta 2$ -glycoprotein I (a $\beta 2$ GPI) antibodies as part of the laboratory criteria, other tests such as anti-phosphatidylserine/prothrombin (aPS/PT) are of

growing interest [5]. In this study, we systematically reviewed all the published data on the prevalence of aPS/PT in APS, and conducted meta-analysis on the pooled APS population. In addition, the rate of aPS/PT in APS patients according to aPL profile was calculated.

After removal of duplicate entries automatically by EndNote (version X9, Clarivate Analytics) and manually, the eligibility of articles was assessed on title and abstract independently by two investigators (R.Z. and C.Y.C), and a senior investigator (V.P.). Studies were considered eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria:

1) Prospective or retrospective, cross-sectional or cohort study design;   
2) Diagnosis of APS fulfilled the laboratory and clinical criteria in the Sapporo or Sydney Consensus Statement;   
3) Available data about the prevalence of aPS/PT in patients with APS.

Studies were excluded if:

1) Reviews, case reports, or studies with a small sample size $(n < 10)$ ;  
2) Duplicated data from the same or overlapping patients' cohort.

A separate analysis on the prevalence of aPS/PT in APS patients positive for LAC or with double and triple positivity was conducted adding 2 studies in which patients were selected with this specific aPL profile.

# 2.2. Data extraction

R.Z. and C.Y.C. independently scored all included studies on a data extraction form, including author(s), year of publication, country, study design, population (primary-APS, or secondary-APS), sample size, age, gender, and assay used for aPS/PT detection, cut-off value, number of patients with positive aPS/PT, prevalence of aPS/PT in APS. To better report the pooled percentage of aPS/PT, we set three columns: IgG, IgM, and IgG/M. APS/PT IgG/M is the number of APS patients that were positive for at least one aPS/PT isotype. In case of multiple reports, we included the study in which the methods were reported in most detail. Differences in extraction were resolved by consensus or, if necessary, by a third researcher (V.P.).

# 3.8. Quality assessment

The quality of included studies was assessed by the AHRQ methodology with 8 items (Fig. 5). Half of included studies had an uncertain bias in patient selection as they did not report if patients were enrolled consecutively or not. There were no biases in performance and detection. Three studies [14-16] had a high risk in attrition as not all patients with APS received the testing for aPS/PT. Three studies [8,15,17] had a high risk of selective outcome reporting, as they only report the positivity of aPS/PT IgG/M without IgG alone and IgM alone.

# 3.10. Publication bias assessment

Funnel plots representing the prevalence of aPS/PT IgG, IgM, and IgG/M in APS were used to evaluate publication bias (Figs. 2-4). Here, we confirmed that APS patients with LAC positivity had a high prevalence of aPS/PT (76.4% for IgG, 70.5% for IgM, and 77.4% for IgG/M).

Although the evidence is solid enough to encourage routine testing for aPS/PT, this is still matter of debate. Data from at least two systematic reviews support the use of aPS/PT to improve the diagnostic accuracy in patients suspected for APS [19,20]. Amengual et al. proposed to consider aPS/PT IgG as an additional laboratory criterion for the classification of APS, since they found that the presence of aPS/PT IgG was associated with arterial and venous thrombosis and obstetric manifestations of APS in an international multicenter study [12].

As already demonstrated for LAC [21], the aPS/PT estimated risk is very close to that reported for deep-vein thrombosis in patients with and without SLE [22,23]. In our meta-analysis, aPS/PT were more frequent in LAC-positive patients $(77.4\%)$ than in LAC-negative patients, a result similar to that reported in the study by Fabris et al. $(56.1\%$ vs. $6.4\%$ respectively) [24]. We must underlie here that, in a nationwide survey conducted by our group, LAC was deemed as positive in many thrombosis laboratory but it resulted false positive in $24\%$ of cases when the same sample was tested in a reference laboratory [25]. In other words, aPS/ PT test may help in cases where anticoagulant drugs prevent a correct LAC interpretation [26]. Besides LAC, a significant correlation between a2GPI IgG and aPS/PT IgG antibodies was observed in APS associated with thrombosis [9]. aPS/PT and its combination with LAC and a2GPI antibodies has recently been identified to display the best diagnostic accuracy for both vascular and obstetric APS among 23 possible combinations of six aPL assays (LAC, aCLs, a2GTs, aPTs, aPS/ PTs, and anti-phosphatidylethanolamine antibodies (aPEs) [27]. Thus, it appears from these studies that aPS/PT IgG may have a comparable or even higher risk for clinical manifestations of APS compared to some criteria aPLs [28].

A pathogenic role of PS/PT in APS was hypothesized: a) activation of endothelial cells by aPS/PT binding PT in complex with phosphatidylserine inducing the production of procoagulant substances; b) increasing the concentration of PT on cells with phosphatidylserine on their surface, which could promote coagulation since antibody-bound PT can still be converted into thrombin [29]; c) an impairment in global fibrinolysis as aPS/PT antibodies cause denser and poorly lysable plasma fibrin clot formation in APS patients [30].

It is undeniable that more robust and reliable diagnostic tests for APS are needed. The use of aPS/PT and aβ2GPI analyses for APS evaluation is particularly appealing given the possible role of these molecules in disease pathogenesis. Testing for antibodies to the PS/PT complex offers comparable sensitivity with significant overlap with LAC. If optimized and standardized, it is likely the testing for aPS/PT would contribute to diagnosis and management of APS and related disorders.

# 4.1. Study limitations

Limitation are those intrinsic to all the metanalyses. In the present

study we compiled results of aPS/PT according to yes/no positivity but methods of determination and cut-off for positivity were different. Moreover, our starting point was the diagnosis of APS but there is still debate on laboratory tests for a definite diagnosis.
