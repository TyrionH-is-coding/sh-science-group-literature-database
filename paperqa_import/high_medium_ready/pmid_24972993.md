# ABSTRACT

Tolerogenic dendritic cells (tDCs) have the potential to control the outcome of autoimmunity by modulating the immune response. The aim of this study was to uncover the tolerance efficacy attributed to beta-2-glycoprotein-I ( $\beta_{2}$ GPI) tDCs or $\beta_{2}$ GPI domain-I (D-I) and domain-V (D-V)-tDCs in mice with antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). tDCs were pulsed with $\beta_{2}$ GPI or D-I or D-V derivatives. Our results revealed that $\beta_{2}$ GPI related tDCs phenotype includes CD80 $^{\text{high}}$ , CD86 $^{\text{high}}$ , CD40 $^{\text{high}}$ , MHC class II $^{\text{high}}$ . The miRNA profiling encompasses miRNA $23b^{\text{high}}$ , miRNA $142-3p^{\text{low}}$ and miRNA $221^{\text{low}}$ . In addition, the $\beta_{2}$ GPI related tDCs showed reduced secretion of IL-1 $\beta$ , IL-12 and IL-23. D-I tDCs treatment was more efficient than $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs in inducing of tolerance in APS mice, manifested by lowered titers of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies (Abs) and reduced percentage of fetal loss. Tolerance induction was accompanied by poor T cell response to $\beta_{2}$ GPI, high numbers of CD4 + CD25 + FOXP3 + T-regulatory cells (Treg), reduced levels of IFN $\gamma$ , IL-17 and increased expression of IL-10 and TGF $\beta$ . Tolerance was successfully transferred by Treg cells from the tolerized mice to $\beta_{2}$ GPI immunized mice. We conclude that predominantly D-I-tDCs and $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs have the potential to attenuate experimental APS by induction of Treg cells, reduction of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI Abs titers and increased expression of anti-inflammatory cytokines. We suggest that $\beta_{2}$ -GPI-D-I-tDCs may offer a novel approach for developing therapy for APS patients.

# 1. Introduction

The antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is characterized by arterial and venous thromboses with recurrent fetal loss in the presence of circulating anti-phospholipid Abs [1,2]. The disease may be systemic involving the central nervous system (CNS), heart, skin and more [3,4]. The main pathogenic antibodies (Abs) target membrane negatively charged phospholipids (cardiolipin, phosphatidylserine), via the $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I ( $\beta_{2}$ GPI) Abs [5,6]. Evidence from human studies and animal models has shown that these

autoantibodies are directly involved in the pathogenic process of APS: a) Passive transfer of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI Abs induced fetal loss in naïve mice or thrombosis in an ex-vivo model [7-11]; b) Active immunization with $\beta_{2}$ GPI induce experimental APS in mice which was prevented by oral tolerance with $\beta_{2}$ GPI feeding [12,13]; c) Exchanging heavy and light chains between pathogenic and non pathogenic anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI single-chain Fvs, revealed that the pathogenicity of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI Abs lies in the CDR of the heavy chain of the immunoglobulins [14]; d) Anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI Abs activate platelets [15]; Trigger endothelial cells to secrete tissue factor via TLR4 pathway NF- $\kappa$ B activation and TNF- $\alpha$ release by monocytes [7,10,16-21]. Circulating anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI Abs are heterogeneous populations of Abs which recognize different epitopes on the $\beta_{2}$ GPI molecule, located on all the five domains of the molecule [7,21-26]. APS patients have personal profiles of circulating anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI targeting different epitopes on the $\beta_{2}$ GPI molecule [7,22].

Accumulating evidence support the importance of the first domain of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule as the dominant epitope [21,24-26].

<!-- page 2 -->
Moreover, several studies have detected a specific population of Abs recognizing a cryptic epitope on domain I, comprising arginine 39 to arginine 43 [24,25]. In contrast to Abs recognizing other domains of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule, anti-domain I Abs were found to be highly associated with clinical symptoms, mainly with thrombosis [25-27].

Dendritic cells (DCs) are the main antigen presenting cells modulators of both immunity and immunological tolerance [28]. Thus, tolerogenic DCs (tDCs) were proposed as one of the potential immunologic strategies to induce antigen-specific tolerance on T cells. In vivo, T-cell peripheral tolerance can be generated and maintained by DCs, leading to the promotion of anergy, immune deviation, deletion of self-reactive lymphocytes, or generation of regulatory T cells [29]. In vitro studies have shown that DCs exposed to various suppressive agents such as IL-10, TGF- $\beta 1$ , vitamin D3 among others, modulated DCs function and induce DCs with ability to trigger tolerance in T cells, coining them "tolerogenic dendritic cells" (tDCs) [29].

Tolerance induction on self-reactive naive T cells is essential for prevention of autoimmune reactions. tDCs induce tolerance through the presentation of antigen with inadequate costimulation and cytokine production for effector T cell activation, resulting in T cell silencing or deletion or induction of regulatory T cells [30,31]. The therapeutic potential of tDCs in autoimmunity was well established for several autoimmune diseases; In-vitro studies and in animal models, exemplified the potential of tDCs to ameliorate autoimmune scenario in rheumatoid arthritis, type-I diabetes mellitus, multiple sclerosis, inflammatory bowel disease and immune thrombocytopenia [32-43]. Previously, we have shownin-vitro the effect of tDC induced by IL-10/TGFβ, loaded with $\beta_{2}$ GPI, on T cell response purified from 16 patients with primary APS [44]. $\beta_{2}$ GPI related tDCs attenuated $\mathrm{CD^{+}}$ T cell response to $\beta_{2}$ GPI, without affecting the proliferative response to an unrelated candidin [44].

Herein, we assess, in experimental APS mice, the mechanisms involved in tolerance induction by $\beta_{2}$ GPI pulsed tDCs, domain-I derivative pulsed tDCs and domain-V derivative pulsed tDCs. Moreover, we studied if the tolerance can be passively transferred by Treg cells from tDCs induced tolerized APS mice to naïve APS mice.

# 2. Materials and methods

# 2.1. Mice

Female BALB/c mice at 10-12 weeks and male at 14-16 weeks of age, were purchased from Harlan Israel. The mice were kept in individually ventilated cages. All experiments were approved and executed according to the protocols of the Ethical Committee of the Israeli Ministry of Health.

# 2.2. Generation of bone marrow derived DCs

Tolerogenic DCs were generated by treating bone marrow-derived DCs with dexamethasone and vitamin D3 during lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced maturation. Bone marrow cells (BMC) were isolated from the femurs and tibia of the mice and cultured in RPMI 1640 (Beit-Haemek, Israel) supplemented with $10\%$ fetal calf serum, 100 units/ml penicillin, $100~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ streptomycin, $2\mathrm{mM}$ of glutamine, non-essential amino acids, sodium pyruvate and $5\times 10^{-5}$ M 2-mercaptoethanol (Sigma-Aldrich) at $37^{\circ}C$ with $5\%$ $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ . DCs were generated as described previously [32,38]. Briefly, bone marrow was cultured for 10 days in medium containing $20~\mathrm{ng / ml}$ granulocyte-macrophage colony stimulating factor (GM-CSF; PeproTech). Cultures were refreshed on days 3, 6,

and 9 with medium supplemented with GM-CSF. On day 10, DCs were matured with LPS from E. Coli O111B4 (Sigma-Aldrich L-4391 St Louis, MO, USA) $1\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ for $18\mathrm{h}$ . Tolerogenic DCs were generated by adding dexamethasone $10^{-6}\mathrm{M}$ (Sigma-Aldrich) and vitamin D3 $10^{-10}\mathrm{M}$ (Sigma-Aldrich) during LPS-induced maturation. During maturation time the DCs were pulsed with $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ whole molecule $100\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ , Domain-I derivative $100\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ or domain-V derivative (EMBI: GDKVSFFCKNKEKKCKKK-Ahx-KSSLAFWKTDASDVKP) [55] synthesized by GL-Biochem, Shanghai Ltd, Shanghai, China, $100\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ .

# 2.3. Flow cytometric analyses

DCs were labeled with the following: anti-mouse CD16/CD32 (Fc Blocker). allophycocyanin (APC)-conjugated anti-class II major histocompatibility complex, Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated anti-CD86, phycoerythrin (PE)-conjugated anti-CD80, and PE conjugated anti-CD40 (all from eBioscience, Sa-Diego, USA). DCs were incubated with the detailed markers for $1\mathrm{h}$ and read by FACS. For T-regulatory cell analyses: FITC-conjugated anti-mouse-CD4, APC-conjugated anti-mouse CD25, PE-conjugated anti-mouse/rat FOXP3. Splenocytes following red blood cell lysis, were incubated with anti $\mathrm{CD4 + }$ anti-CD25 for $1.5\mathrm{h}$ at room temperature. Following washing with PBS, the cells were fixed and permeabilized with Fix/Perm buffer and permeabilization buffer (eBioscience) overnight at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . The day after anti-FOXP3 was added for $1.5\mathrm{h}$ incubation, washed and read by FACS, gating on $\mathrm{CD4^{+}}$ T cells.

# 2.4. Cytokine production by DCs and splenocytes

tDCs, mDCs and iDCs were cultured on recombinant mouse CD40 Ligand/TNFSF5 CD40L (R&D systems Minneapolis, MN, USA) coated plates $(10\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ PBS, overnight at $4^{\circ}C)$ . After $24\mathrm{h}$ , supernatants were harvested, and cytokine levels were determined by sandwich ELISA (R&D Systems), performed according to the manufacturers' instructions. Cytokine production by splenocytes from the APS mice treated with the different DCs was studied as follow:Splenocytes $(5\times 10^{6}$ cells per well) were incubated in enriched medium for $48\mathrm{h}$ and $72\mathrm{h}$ . IFN $\gamma$ and IL-10 were determined in the supernatants by ELISA using DuoSet (R&D) according to the manufacturer's instructions. For the detection of TGF $\beta$ , the plates were coated with a recombinant human TGF- $\beta$ sRII/Fc chimera (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, US). Supernatants were added after activation of latent TGF $\beta$ to immunoreactive TGF- $\beta 1$ , a biotinylated anti-human TGF- $\beta 1$ Ab was added thereafter, and the assay was developed according to the manufacturer's instructions (R&D Systems).

# 2.5. miRNA analyses of the DCs

Two million of cells were used for miRNA extraction using the miRNAeasy mini kit followed by miRNeasy mini kit followed by MinElute Cleanup Kit (QIAGEN Valencia, CA, USA) according the manufacturer's conditions. This procedure was made three times for each mDCs, iDCs, tDCs. The miRNA expression was analyzed by a quantitative real-time PCR. The reactions were carried out on a StepOnePlus™ Real-Time PCR machine, containing $1 \times$ Master Mix (Applied Biosystems®), with $1 \times$ probes (TaqMan® microRNA Assays, miRNA-23b; miRNA-221; miRNA-142-3p, (Applied Biosystems®), cDNA sample, and RNU44 endogenous control (TaqMan microRNA Assay 001094, Applied Biosystems, Foster City, USA) which was used to normalize the results, regarding the biomarkers, since it presents a constant expression level. Data analysis was carried out using the StepOne Software v 2.2 (Applied Biosystems®) with the same baseline and threshold set

for each plate, in order to generate threshold cycle (Ct) values for all the miRNAs in each sample. miRNA quantification was performed in duplicate.

# 2.6. Induction and treatment of APS mice with dendritic cells

Experimental APS was induced in BALB/c female mice by immunization with human $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}10~\mu \mathrm{g / }$ mouse in complete Freunds adjuvant (CFA) intradermal in the hind footpads. mDCs, tDCs pulsed with $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ D-I or D-V derivatives were applied to the mice subcutaneously at concentration of $10^{6}$ cells/mouse weekly during 4 weeks. The mice were mated and the vaginal plugs were confirmed [7,9,12]. The number of resorptions (equivalent to fetal loss) was counted on day 16 of pregnancy.

# 2.7. Proliferation assay

APS mice treated with $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ -tDCs, $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ -D-I tDCs, $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ -D-V, mDCs or PBS were used as the source of splenocytes. $\mathrm{CD4^{+}}$ T cells were isolated from the studied splenocytes' groups, using CD4 magnetic microbeads (MACS Miltenyi Biotec, Bergischgladbach, Germany) by positive selection. The response of the $\mathrm{CD4^{+}}$ T cells $(1\times 10^{6}$ cells/ml) isolated from the studied groups of APS mice, to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ $(10\mathrm{mg / ml})$ was analyzed. The rate of proliferation was measured using a colorimetric assay and Bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU) incorporation. The CHEMICON®s BrdU cell proliferation assay kit (Chemicon by Merck-Millipor,Darmstadt, Germany) was used according to the manufacturer instructions. Inhibition of proliferation was calculated in comparison to the PBS treated mice.

# 2.8. Detection of circulating anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI Abs in mice sera

Sera from APS mice treated with PBS, $\beta_{2}$ GPI-tDCs, $\beta_{2}$ GPI-D-I-tDCs, $\beta_{2}$ GPI-D-V-tDCs or mDCs, were tested for the presence of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI or its derivatives by home made ELISA. Ninety six wells ELISA plates were coated over-night at $4^{\circ}C$ with $\beta_{2}$ GPI, D-I pr D-V derivatives, $10~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ in PBS and were blocked with $3 \%$ BSA in PBS at room temperature. The sera were added at dilution of 1:200 for 2 hrs at room temperature. The binding was probed by using alkaline phosphatase conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (Jackson ImmunoResearch Laboratories, Inc., West Grove, PA, USA) diluted 1:5000 followed by appropriate substrate (Sigma-Aldrich). Extensive washings were performed between each step. Data were read by a Titertrek ELISA reader at $405~\mathrm{nm}$ filter/620 nm reference filter.

# 2.9. Passive transfer of tolerance by T-regulatory cells

Treg cells were purified by "Treg cells isolation kit-II $\mathrm{CD4^{+}CD25^{+}CD127^{+}}$ (MACS Miltenyi Biotec), from tolerized APS mice, treated with $\beta_{2}$ GPI-tDCs or with $\beta_{2}$ GPI-D-I-tDCs. The isolated Treg cells at concentration of $1\times 10^{7} / 0.2\mathrm{ml}$ PBS were passively transferred intravenously to APS mice, at 3 weeks post immunization with $\beta_{2}$ GPI molecule. The mice received 3 weekly injections of Treg cells. The percentage of fetal loss was studied following mating the mice.

# 2.10. Statistical analysis

The data are expressed as the mean $\pm$ SD. Statistical analysis was performed with one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Student's $t$ -test for comparison between groups. $P$ -values $< 0.05$ were considered to be significant.

# 3. Results

# 3.1. Characterization of $\beta_{2}$ GPI related tolerogenic dendritic cells (tDCs)

DCs were generated from naïve mouse bone marrow cells and treated with dexamethasone and vitamin D3 during LPS-induced maturation to generate tDCs. LPS-activated DCs that were not exposed to dexamethasone/vitamin D3 were coined mature DCs (mDCs) and the untreated DCs were defined as immature DCs (iDCs) and were used as a control population.

tDCs pulsed with $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , D-I derivative or with D-V derivative displayed a semi-mature phenotype as illustrated in Fig 1a. The three types of tDCs expressed higher levels of the costimulatory molecules CD80 and CD86, high level of CD40 and MHC class II, in comparison with iDCs ( $p < 0.001$ , $p < 0.02$ , $p < 0.04$ , $p < 0.001$ , respectively). The expression of CD80, CD86, CD40 and MHC class II markers on the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs, D-I tDCs, D-V tDCs, was lower in comparison to the expression by mDCs ( $p < 0.03$ , 0.04, $p < 0.02$ , $p < 0.02$ respectively). No significant changes in CD80, CD86, CD40, MHC class II markers expression was noticed between the three types of tDCs populations for all the tested markers ( $p > 0.05$ ).

Cytokines production is one of the mechanisms by which DCs contribute to tolerance induced immune-network. Therefore, we analyze the tDCs cytokine secretion upon CD40 ligation. The data depicted in Fig. 1b, show that $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs, D-I tDCs, D-V tDCs, produced lower levels of the proinflammatory cytokines IL-1 $\beta$ $(p < 0.02)$ , IL-12p70 $(p < 0.01)$ , and IL-23 (which is known to promote Th17 differentiation from CD4+ naive T cells) $(p < 0.02)$ , as compared to mDCs. In contrary, a significant increase in IL-1 $\beta$ , IL12 and IL-23 secretion by $\beta_{2}$ GPI related tDCs was documented in comparison to iDCs $(p < 0.001)$ . The production of anti-inflammatory IL-10 was decreased by $\beta_{2}$ GPI related tDCs to a lesser extent $(p < 0.05)$ (Fig. 1b).

A microRNA (miRNA) is a small non-coding RNA molecule (containing $\sim 22$ nucleotides). It is well established in the literature that miRNAs are important players in immune cell differentiation, immune responses including autoimmunity, cancer, tissue factor expression and divers metabolic pathways [45-50]. Analyses of miRNA expression by $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs, D-I tDCs and D-V tDCs upon CD40 ligation is described in Fig. 1c. miRNA-23b expression was higher in the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ related tDCs by a factor of 2.2 in comparison to miRNA-23b expression by mDCs. miRNA-23b expression in the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ related tDCs was 5.8 time higher in comparison to miRNA-23b expression by iDCs. Low expression of miRNA 142-3p and miRNA-221, by the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ related tDCs were noticed, in comparison to iDCs, $p < 0.01$ , $p < 0.02$ respectively (Fig. 1c).

To sum all the characterization of the in-vitro generated $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ related tDCs, no significant differences were found between the characteristics of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs, D-I tDCs and D-V tDCs, in the phenotype, cytokine secretion and miRNA expression.

# 3.2. Attenuated APS manifestations by $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs and D-I tDCs treatment

In order to assess the potential of $\beta_{2}$ GPI related tDCs to reduce fetal loss development, we treated the APS mice, induced with $\beta_{2}$ GPI molecule, for a month with the $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs, or D-I tDCs or D-V tDCs and mDCs as control DCs. There was a significant reduction in the percentage of fetal loss in mice which were treated either with $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs or with D-I tDCs ( $12\pm 2\%$ $7\pm 1\%$ respectively) in comparison to D-V tDCs, mDCs treated APS mice $42\pm 3\%$ or $29\pm 2\%$ respectively) (Table 1). No significant differences in rates of fetal loss were obtained upon APS mice treated with D-V tDCs, mDCs or PBS, $p > 0.05$

# 3.3. Inhibition of $\beta_{2}$ GPI T cell proliferation and induction of T regulatory phenotype upon treatment of APS mice with $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs, D-I tDCs

We further analyzed the tDCs in-animal model for their functional capabilities to induce tolerance in APS mice. Naive mice immunized with $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ were treated with $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs, D-I tDCs, D-V tDCs weekly for one month. $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs and D-I tDCs ameliorated T cell response to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , whereas D-V tDCs and mDCs did not have any effect on proliferation activity (Fig. 2a). $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs inhibited the T cell response to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ by $94 \pm 12\%$ and D-I tDC reduced the proliferation by $73 \pm 4.3\%$ , D-V tDCs and mDCs inhibited only by $14 \pm 2.1\%$ and $32 \pm 3.1\%$ respectively, which showed their impaired potential to ameliorate the proliferative activity (Fig. 2a).

Since $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - I}$ tDCs and $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs, inhibited significantly the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ T cell proliferation response, with beneficial effect of D-I-tDCs, we extended the T cell analyses, and assessed whether the tolerance induction was associated with $\mathrm{CD4^{+}CD25^{+}FOXP3^{+}}$ Treg cells phenotype expansion in the tolerized APS mice. An increase in the percentage of $\mathrm{CD25^{+}FOXP3^{+}}$ Treg cells, gated on $\mathrm{CD4^{+}}$ T cells is presented in Fig. 2b,c. Splenocytes from APS mice treated with $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs or D-I tDCs had $11.6\pm 2.4\%$ and $19\pm 3.2\%$ Treg cells $(p < 0.02)$ . APS mice treated with D-V tDCs or mDCs showed $4.3\pm 1.7\%$ and $3.8\pm 1.2\%$ Treg in splenocytes (Fig. 2c). Splenocytes derived from PBS treated mice had $2.1\pm 1.4\%$ $\mathrm{CD4^{+}CD25^{+}FOXP3^{+}}$ Treg cells, (data not shown).

# 3.4. APS mice treated with $\beta_{2}GPI$ tDCs, D-I tDCs showed increased anti-inflammatory cytokines levels

The reduction in proinflammatory cytokines production of splenocytes from tolerized APS mice compared to the secretion by splenocytes from APS mice treated with PBS was significant $(p < 0.001)$ . # 4. Discussion

The main findings of the present study is that tolerogenic DCs (tDCs) pulsed with $\beta_{2}$ GPI-domain-I derivative ( $\beta_{2}$ GPI D-I tDCs), had a better beneficial effects than tDCs pulsed with the $\beta_{2}$ GPI whole molecule, in attenuating the APS in a murine model. This tolerogenic effect was specific, since it was not observed following treatment of APS mice with tDCs pulsed with $\beta_{2}$ GPI-domain-V derivative, nor with maturated DCs. Moreover, the tolerance induced by tDCs D-I or $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs, correlated with the enhanced expression of T-regulatory cells, amelioration of proinflammatory cytokines IL-1 $\beta$ , IL-17 levels and upregulation of anti-inflammatory IL-10 and TGF $\beta$ expression. The tolerance was successfully transferred to APS mice, manifested by reduced titers of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI Abs and a significant decrease in the rate of fetal loss. Isolated T-regulatory cells from APS mice treated with $\beta_{2}$ GPI D-I tDCs had the highest capability to suppress development of experimental APS.

In previous studies, screening of human sera from APS patients substantiated the importance of domain-I related autoantibodies to the pathophysiology of the disease progression [24-27]. In 1999 Bouma et al. and Schwarenbacher et al., introducing the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ crystallographic structure, addressed the fifth C-terminal domain as the membrane binding site and the N-terminal first domain, as the most exposed to the immune system [51,52]. Interestingly, the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule appears in a circular conformation in healthy individuals and the first domain is cryptic. However, the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ can appear in different conformation structures and expose the first domain epitope in the presence of anionic phospholipids, lipopolysaccharide, or hydrophilic surfaces; in these conditions the circular conformation of the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ protein unfolds and exposes domain-I to the immune system [53,54]. In the current study the titers of anti-D-I Abs were elevated to a higher level as to the Abs to the fifth domain, which is shielded from the circulation and mainly serve as phospholipid binding site. Our current data proved that treatment of APS mice with DI-tDCs caused induction of tolerance. In our study we used the EMBI peptide as derivative of domain-V, known to be the membrane binding site [51,52]. Previously, EMBI was shown to be weakly detected by APS patient sera, yet prevented $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ -membrane binding and improved experimental APS manifestations [55]. EMBI $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ derivative penetrates into endothelial cells, inhibits the expression of procoagulation state by preventing E-selectin and tissue factor expression via inverse correlation to glycogen synthase kinase- $3\beta$ phosphorylation [55]. However, our data show that treatment with tolerogenic DCs loaded with EMBI as the domain-V derivative of the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule, did not induce tolerance in the APS mice.

It is of interest why we got such a dramatic difference in the function of D-I tDCS, D-V tDCs and $\beta_{2}$ GPI tDCs, since the three types of tDCs had similar markers profiles (CD80, CD86, CD40, MHC class II expression); similar reduced ability to secrete IL-1 $\beta$ , IL-12, IL-23 and IL-10 in response to CD40L; increased miRNA 23b expression and lower miRNA 221 and miRNA 142-3p.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are important in regulating immune cell differentiation and immune responses including inflammation, autoimmunity and cancer [45-50]. The known function of miRNAs is the post-transcriptional regulation of certain subsets of messenger RNAs (mRNAs) by binding to their 3'untranslated region (UTR), thus targeting them for degradation or translational repression [51]. Our data are in line with other groups, showing a significant upregulation of miR-23b in bone marrow DCs pulsed with ovalbumin (OVA). These DCs showed decreased tolerogenic properties by increased IL-10 production, decreased IL-12 production and an enhanced capacity to promote $\mathrm{FoxP3 + CD4 + T}$ regulatory cells differentiation [52]. Low miRNA-142-3p is known to promote survival of immature DCs, regulate pDC/cDC fate. It promotes IL-6 production which can act as either a pro- or anti-inflammatory cytokine. miR-221 expression increases upon differentiation of DCs from monocytes, leading to low levels of $\mathsf{p27}^{\mathsf{kip1}}$ in immature DCs, and thereby contribute to immature DC homeostasis. However, upon DC maturation, miR-221 is downregulated and p27kip1 levels correspondingly increase [53,54].

Treatment of experimental APS with tDC specific for $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ or $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - I}$ or $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - V}$ resulted in different degrees of tolerance induction. Immunization with the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule revealed a high T cell response to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , predominantly directed to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - I}$ and in a much lesser degree to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - V}$ . This fact is based on the reduced ability of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - V}$ tDCs to inhibit the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ T cell response. The humoral response behaved in the same manner, most of the anti $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs were directed to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - I}$ derivative and not to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - V}$ derivative which is probably a weak immunogen. Therefore, we assume that the pathophysiology of experimental APS in our murine model was probably due to the immunogenicity of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI - D - I}$

<!-- page 7 -->
rather than of $\beta_{2}$ GPI-D-V derivative. Due to the high cellular and humoral response to $\beta_{2}$ GPI-D-I versus $\beta_{2}$ GPI-D-V, the APS mice treated with $\beta_{2}$ GPI-D-I tDCs induced significantly higher levels of Treg cells and anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10 TGF $\beta$ ) resulting in tolerance induction, which had an impact on the clinical manifestations of experimental APS.

Restoring immune tolerance by treatment with DCs that possess tolerogenic functions, has become a promising tool to reinstate tolerance to self-antigens in patients and animal models with autoimmune diseases.

Prophylactic and therapeutic potential of ex-vivo generated tDCs, using biological or pharmacologic modulation, have shown beneficial effects in animal models of collagen and adjuvant induced arthritis [35-39]. Indeed, as described, strategies to induce tDCs include modulation of DC function with immunosuppressive agents, such as dexamethasone, vitamin D $1\alpha,25$ -dihydroxyvitamin D3, TNF- $\alpha$ , vitamin D and vasoactive intestinal peptide and more.

Our study go in line with the recent described cell therapy approach, using activated DCs pulsed with tumor antigens to treat cancer and tolerogenic dendritic cells to treat autoimmune diseases. Successful treatment was reported in rheumatoid arthritis, type-I diabetes mellitus, multiple sclerosis inflammatory bowel disease and immune thrombocytopenia [32-43]. In all these autoimmune conditions the treatment with the autoantigen specific tDCs differentially accelerated the T regulatory phenotype, increased anti-inflammatory cytokines and reduced proinflammatory cytokine production. For example, administration of tDCs after the onset of collagen induced arthritis, significantly reduced the severity and progression of arthritis, whereas treatment with mature DCs exacerbated arthritis in a murine model. Although no expansion of FoxP3 + Treg cells was observed, injection of tDCs resulted in decrease of type II collagen-specific T cell proliferation, a decrease in the proportion of Th17 cells and increase of IL-10-producing T cells [38]. Prophylactic administration of tDCs induced antigen specific protection of mice from autoimmune encephalomyelitis, by inducing IL-10 secreting T-reg cells [56].

The present study clearly demonstrate the efficacy of tDCs pulsed with domain-I of the $\beta_{2}$ GPI molecule, in the attenuation of experimental APS in a murine model, via acceleration of the differentiation of $\mathrm{CD4^{+}}$ T cells to the Treg cells phenotype, downregulation of proinflammatory cytokines and enhancement of the expression of anti-inflammatory cytokines IL-10 and TGF $\beta$ .

Furthermore, our study contributes to understanding the role of miRNA in the development of tolerogenic DCs in-vitro. miRNAs are important in regulating immune cell differentiation and immune responses including inflammation, autoimmunity and cancer [45-50]. The known function of miRNAs is the post-transcriptional regulation of certain subsets of messenger RNAs (mRNAs) by binding to their 3'untranslated region (UTR) thus targeting them for degradation or translational repression [57]. Our data are in line with findings of other groups, showing significant upregulation of miR-23b in bone marrow DCs pulsed with OVA. These DCs showed decreased tolerogenic properties, depicted by increased IL-10 production, decreased IL-12 production and an enhanced capacity to promote FoxP3 + CD4 + T regulatory cells differentiation [58]. Low miRNA-142-3p is known to promote survival of immature DCs and regulate pDC/cDC fate decisions. It promotes IL-6 production which can act as either a pro- or anti-inflammatory cytokine. The miR-221 expression increases upon differentiation of DCs from monocytes, leading to low levels of $\mathsf{p27}^{\mathrm{kip1}}$ in immature DCs, and thereby contributes to immature DC homeostasis. However, upon DC maturation, miR-221 is downregulated and p27kip1 levels correspondingly increase [59,60].

The role of miRNA expression in rheumatic autoimmune diseases is well established. For example, in lupus, low miRNA-146a in

PBMCs is inversely correlated with the IFN score and may be responsible for the overproduction of the proinflammatory IFN type I pathway in lupus patients that correlates with disease activity [58,61]. Down-regulation of miR-19b and miR-20a was observed in patients with SLE and APS. Low expression of miR-19a, miR-93, miR-106, and miR-126 is involved in direct regulation of protein expression of tissue factor, the main initiator of the coagulation cascade and provokes thrombotic events [48,61].

miRNA-21, miRNA-148a and miRNA-126, contribute to DNA hypomethylation in lupus $\mathrm{CD4 + T}$ cells by directly and indirectly targeting DNA methyltransferase-1 [62-65]. In rheumatoid arthritis, high miRNA-146 expression in PBMCs and synovial tissue, accelerate NF-kB, leading to prolonged production of synovial tissue proinflammatory cytokines/chemokines, including TNF- $\alpha$ and IL- $1\beta$ . miR-155 expressed by synovial tissue and fibroblast, enhance MMP-3 and MMP-1 production, causing synoviocyte joint destruction [62].

# 5. Conclusion

Tolerogenic DCs specific for domain-I of the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule have preferable tolerogenic potential than the $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ tDCs in attenuating experimental APS. The therapy is specific and hence does not lead to widespread immunosuppression that can cause serious infections or malignancies. Our results may offer a novel approach for developing therapy for APS patients.
