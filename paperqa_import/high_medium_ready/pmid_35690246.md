# ABSTRACT

APS (antiphospholipid syndrome) is a systematic autoimmune disease accompanied with venous or arterial thrombosis and poor pregnant manifestations, partly attributing to the successive elevated aPL (antiphospholipid antibodies) and provoked prothrombotic and proinflammatory molecules production. Nowadays, most researches focus on the laboratory detection and clinic features of APS, but its precise etiology remains to be deeply explored. As we all know, the dysfunction of ECs (endothelial cells), monocytes, platelets, trophoblasts and neutrophils are key contributors to APS progression. Especially, their epigenetic variations, mainly including the promoter CpGs methylation, histone PTMs (post-translational modifications) and ncRNAs (noncoding RNAs), result in genes expression or silence engaged in inflammation initiation, thrombosis formation, autoimmune activation and APOs (adverse pregnancy outcomes) in APS. Given the potential of epigenetic markers serving as diagnostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets of APS, and the encouraging advancements in epigenetic drugs are being made. In this review, we would systematically introduce the epigenetic underlying mechanisms for APS progression, comprehensively elucidate the functional mechanisms of epigenetics in boosting ECs, monocytes, platelets, trophoblasts and neutrophils. Lastly, the application of epigenetic alterations for probing novel diagnostic, specific therapeutic and prognostic strategies would be proposed.

# 1. Introduction

APS (antiphospholipid syndrome), previously termed as Hughes syndrome, is a chronic autoimmune disease characterized with venous or arterial vascular thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity [1]. Statistics show that the prevalence and incidence of APS are approximately 40-50 cases per 100,000 and 5 de novo cases per 100,000 individuals per year, respectively [2]. APS patients often have multiple organs-systems injury and develop genetical predisposition, associated with the persistent presence of serum aPL (antiphospholipid antibodies), including aCL

(anti-cardiolipin antibodies), anti- $\beta 2$ GPI (anti-beta2-glycoprotein I) and LAC (lupus anticoagulant) [3]. aPL, especially anti- $\beta 2$ GPI, is well known to activate ECs (endothelial cells), neutrophils, monocytes and platelets together with severe proinflammatory and prothrombotic effects [4]. Nonetheless, the etiology of this syndrome remains to be profoundly investigated, which brings great challenges for specific diagnosis and effective treatment of APS.

# 2. Epigenetic underlying mechanisms for APS progression

Epigenetic modifications mainly involve in histone modifications, ncRNAs and DNA methylation. Increasing observations have suggested that epigenetic alternations are important regulators of APS involving in inflammation, autoimmune response, thrombosis and pregnant outcomes through modulating chromatin accessibility, transcription factors activation and target genes expression [13]. Significantly, the interactions between transcription factors and epigenetic variations might cause the persistent APS-related cells dysfunction and APS activity (Fig. 1).

Histone modifications are composed of methylation, acetylation, phosphorylation ubiquitination, ADP ribosylation and sumoylation, each has different regulatory mechanisms and biological function [14]. There are four core histones (H3, H4, H2A, H2B) mostly modified at K (lysine) site. To date, histone acetylation and methylation are most extensively studied, mainly including H3K4me3 (histone 3 lysine 4 trimethylation), H3K4me1, H3K27me3, H3K36me3, H4K20me3, H3K9me3, H3K9ac and H3K27ac (histone 3 lysine 27 acetylation) (Fig. 2) [15,16]. Among these, H3K4me1 and H3K27ac are enriched in the enhancer regions and tightly associated with chromatin activation and genes expression [17,18]. H3K4me3 and H3K9ac are known as active histone modifications with extensive chromatin accessibility, they are often located at the promoter regions of target genes [18,19]. H3K36me3 engages in extending transcription and maintaining genomic stability via DNA mismatch repair [20]. H3K27me3 is a marker of silent enhancer and correlates with transcription repression [18]. H3K9me3 and H4K20me3 are post-translational modifications for blocking genes expression [21,22].

DNA methylation occurs via adding a methyl group to the fifth position of the six-atom ring of cytosine (5-methylcytosine) with the help of a group of DNMTs, mainly including DNMT1 (for maintaining DNA methylation), DNMT 3A and DNMT 3B (for de novo methylation) [23]. DNMTs dysregulation promotes the hypermethylation of CpGs enriched regions, further compacting DNA structure, repressing transcription factors recruitment and transcription activation [24]. Moreover, there is a tight connection between histone modifications and DNA methylation. H3K36me3, H3K4me3 and H3K27ac accompany with the decreased DNA methylation and active transcription, and H3K9me3, H4K20me3, and H3K27me3 are characterized by the increased DNA methylation and reduced genes expression [25]. Besides, DNA MBPs (methyl binding proteins) can bind with methylated DNA to cause genes silence through recruiting HDACs (histone deacetylases) [25].

NcRNAs contain miRNAs (microRNAs), lncRNAs (long noncoding RNAs) and circRNAs (circular RNAs), they play crucial roles in guiding post-transcription and regulating chromatin complexes. NcRNAs epigenetic mechanisms have pivotal roles in the progression of numerous autoimmune diseases, and have been demonstrated to be significant regulators of immune response, autoimmune breakdown, inflammatory cytokines secretion and thrombosis formation [7]. Differentially expressed ncRNAs, mainly miRNAs, have been observed in APS patients and participate in APS pathogenesis, which would facilitate the exploration of novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets in APS [25].

Evidence has shown that histone acetylation in neutrophils functions as a double-edged sword for NETosis generation and NETs-mediated APS progression [27]. On the one hand, low concentrations of HDACis (histone deacetylase inhibitors), belinostat or panobinostat treatment, augment histone acetylation and cause about $20\%$ increase of NETosis in resting neutrophils in a NOX-dependent or -independent manner, subsequently facilitating APS development [28]. Nevertheless, higher levels of HDACis significantly suppress NOX-dependent and -independent NETosis and switch neutrophils death from NETosis to apoptosis, which might alleviate autoimmune activation and APS process [29]. In addition, H3K4me3 abnormalities in neutrophils implicate LPS-mediated NF- $\kappa \mathbf{B}$ canonical activation and then accelerate inflammation in APS patients [30]. Conversely, NETs can affect histone modifications. For instance, NETs release induces histone H3 and H4 citrullination, and the inhibitor of histone citrullination, tACPA (therapeutic anti-citrullinated protein antibody), is utilized for blocking NETs formation and neutrophils-mediated inflammation [31,32]. Apparently, histone acetylation, citrullination and methylation are important roles of neutrophils, and corresponding inhibitors might recover neutrophils dysfunction.

DNA methylation profiles in neutrophils of APS patients reveal the deregulated genes methylation and expression. In primary APS neutrophils, the hypomethylated PTPN2 and DPPA3 heighten their transcription [12]. Overexpressed PTPN2 might accelerate APS pathogenesis via modulating IL-6 secretion and systemic inflammatory response [33], and DPPA3 plays a key role in early embryogenesis with a high transcription level [34]. So, DNA methylation-induced genes dysregulation in neutrophils participates in inflammation and fetal growth in APS.

The levels of miRNAs in neutrophils markedly affect APS outcomes.

In aPL IgG-treated neutrophils purified from healthy donors, the reduced intracellular transcription of miR-124a-3p, miR-125a-5p, miR-125b-5p, miR-146a-5p, miR-155- 5p, and miR-222-3p is detected. These reduced miRNAs are associated with the higher risk of thrombosis, early atherosclerosis and oxidative stress through repressing miRNA biogenesis proteins expression (Dicer, drosha, Ago-1, Ago-2 and Exportin 5) [35]. In detail, miR-146 and miR-155 are key modulators of innate and adaptive immune response, both of them modulate several TLR (toll-like receptor)-4 effectors expression, such as IRAK1, IRAK2, TRAF6, IRF3, IRF5 and TNF (tumor necrosis factor)- $\alpha$ . Previously, miR-124a and miR125 have been reported as regulators of inflammatory chemokines MCP1 and RANTES, respectively. Additionally, lncRNA GSEC in neutrophils stimulates inflammation response by promoting PFKFB3 (6-Phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-biphosphatase 3) expression and enhancing glycolytic metabolism [36]. All in all, ncRNAs variations in neutrophils take part in APS progression based on the different regulatory mechanisms.

# 4. Epigenetics-induced monocytes activation

Monocytes participate in aPL-mediated thrombosis, inflammation and autoimmune response. Activated monocytes are pivotal drivers for APS through upregulating TF, VCAM-1 (vascular cell adhesion molecule-1), IL-6, IL-8 and TNF- $\alpha$ expression [37]. Significantly, epigenetic changes in monocytes have been observed in APS patients.

HDACs associate with the phenotype shift of monocytes. For one thing, HDACs induce monocytes-mediated proinflammatory response and host immune defense together with the upregulation of IL-1 $\beta$ , TNF- $\alpha$ as well as CCL2 (chemokine (C-C motif) ligand 2), and the enhanced H3K4me3, H3K27me3 and H3K36me3 marks at the CCL2 promoter region have been observed in LPS-stimulated THP-1 monocytes that represent the in vitro model mimicking APS [9,38]. For another thing, HDACs boost monocytes towards an immunosuppressive and anti

inflammatory phenotype [38]. Besides, epigenetic alterations in monocytes are correlated with transcription factors activation and epigenetics-related enzymes expression. In SSc monocytes, IRF and STAT transcription binding sites accompany with the increased H3K4me3 and H3K27ac, which might strengthen proinflammatory cytokines production in APS [39]. Also, there are the decreased global H3ac and increased H3K27me3 as well as the downregulated epigenetic modification enzymes HDAC3, SIRT1, P300, JMJD3 and SUV39H1 and upregulated HDAC7 in monocytes of CAD (coronary artery disease) patients, which enhances the MCP-1 (monocyte chemoattractant protein-1, also called CCL2) promoter acetylation and facilitates MCP-1 expression [40].

Significantly, histone modifications in monocytes have an obligatory role in regulating neonatal immune function. In order to elucidate the related mechanisms, research has collected 45 healthy neonates and found the gradually increasing H3K4me3 marks at the promoter regions of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-12β and TNF without changing the H3K27me3 levels as gestation approach. Thus, term neonates have more open and accessible chromatin to transcription factors in these promoter regions than preterm. However, neonatal monocytes are characterized with the decreased H3K4me3 abundance compared with adult CD14+ monocytes. The inactive H3K4me3 enriches at the promoters of CCR2, ILF2, IL-1β, and TNF and represses their expression [41], providing important foundation for investigating the role of epigenetic patterns in neonatal-specific immune response and ameliorating pregnant outcomes in APS patients.

Human monocytes are heterogeneous subsets consisting of classical $\mathrm{CD14 + + CD16 - }$ , intermediate $\mathrm{CD14 + + CD16 + }$ and nonclassical $\mathrm{CD14 + }$ $\mathrm{CD16 + + }$ monocytes, each type of monocytes displays a distinct DNA methylation landscape. Aberrant DNA methylation in intermediate monocytes is associated with imbalanced immunological processes, monocytes-to-macrophages differentiation, inflammatory cytokines production, organs damage and thrombosis in APS [42,43]. The gain of DNA methylation at C/EBP and ETS motifs and loss of DNA methylation at AP-1 motif are demonstrated during monocytes-to-macrophages differentiation [44]. Thus, DNA methylation in monocytes might be celltype specific, and vital for cell identify and transition [45].

In APS monocytes, the decreased IL-8 promoter methylation switches monocytes towards a proinflammatory and procoagulant phenotype [12]. In pSS (progressive systemic sclerosis) monocytes, 1977 hypomethylated and 842 hypermethylated positions might involve with antigen presentation, transcription activity, cell adhesion and IFN signaling in APS development [46]. LPS serves as an important stimulator for methylation deregulation in APS monocytes, and T3 (triiodothyronine) treatment reverses LPS-induced abnormal DNA methylation and limits genes expression involved in metabolic pathways and immune response [47]. IFN- $\beta$ has a vital impact on monocytes-mediated cytokines, chemokines and adhesion molecules expression, and IFN- $\beta$ treatment significantly reduced the global DNA methylation pattern in monocytes instead of in lymphocytes [48]. Although, aberrant DNA methylation influences monocytes activation, there are methods to normalize DNA methylation and monocytes activity.

NcRNAs in monocytes is particularly linked with diverse APS manifestations. Impaired lipid metabolism in macrophages aggravates thrombosis in APS, but lncRNA Mirt2 encourages lipid elimination and cholesterol efflux by blocking MAPK (mitogen-activated protein kinase) and triggering NRF2 (nuclear factor erythroid-related factor 2) cascades, further attenuating macrophages-mediated inflammation and thrombosis in APS [49]. In clinic, monocytes of APS patients are characterized with the lower expression of miR-19b-3p and miR-20a-5p in contrast with healthy controls, and aPL IgG-treated healthy monocytes also secrete the decreased miR-19b and miR-20a compared to the control IgG treatment [50]. The reduced miRNAs present the hypercoagulable state and worse APS outcomes through augmenting aPL levels and TF expression [50]. Furthermore, an observation indicates the lowered miR-124a and miR-125a levels and the increased miR-146a and miR

155 expression in monocytes of APS patients and in vitro APS monocytes model, further impacting autoimmunity, thrombosis activation and oxidative reaction [35]. Meanwhile, pre-miR-124a and pre-miR-125a-transfected monocytes significantly attenuate atherothrombosis formation and related molecules expression, including MCP-1, IL-6, IL-6R, IL-8, ERK (extracellular regulated protein kinase), STAT3, p38 MAPK and peroxides [35].

To estimate the potential of altered plasma miRNAs as non-invasive monitoring and distinctive diagnostic biomarkers, an exploratory cohort has confirmed eleven altered miRNAs as the regulators of key proteins involved in abortion and thrombosis, including miR-34a-5p, miR-15a-5p, miR-145a-5p, miR-133b-3p, miR-124-3p, miR-206, miR-20a-5p, miR-19b-3p, miR-210-3p, miR-296-5p and miR-374a-5p [51]. To further verify the feasibility of combined miRNAs for distinguishing different pathological conditions, 10 miRNA ratios are calculated with logistic regression, including miR-19b/miR-34a, miR-19b/miR-15a, miR19b/miR-124, miR-19b/miR-145, miR-20a/miR-145, miR-20a/miR-374a, miR-20a/miR-210, miR-20a/miR-133b, miR-206/miR-34a and miR-124/miR-296 [51]. Among these, the elevated levels of miR19b/miR-124 and miR-20a/miR-374, and reduced levels of miR-124/miR-296 are linked with APS fetal loss, the increased miR-20a/miR-145 and miR-20a/miR-374 are correlated with APS arterial thrombosis. In line with these, in vitro aPL IgG-established APS monocytes demonstrate the dysregulated miR-19b, miR-20a, miR-145, miR-210 and miR-296 that strengthen the monocytes-mediated TF, PAI-1 (plasminogen activator inhibitor-1) and MCP-1 secretion [51]. Overall, aberrant ncRNAs landscape in monocytes initiates thrombosis formation, inflammation, immunity imbalance and fetal loss, which might serve as specific diagnostic and therapeutic targets in APS.

# 5. Epigenetics-induced ECs activation

As we all known, monolayer endothelium covers the inner surface of vascular wall to keep the intactness of blood vessels. And ECs dysfunction has a pathological part in accelerating inflammation, venous or arterial thrombosis and obstetric morbidities in APS patients. Current investigations have found that epigenetic events in ECs implicate in ECs damage boosted-APS pathophysiology.

VEGFR-2 (vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2) and VEGFR-1 are positive regulators of angiogenesis, and the strengthened H3K14ac and H4ac enrich in the broad intragenic regions of VEGFR-2 and VEGFR-1 in KAT7 (histone acetyltransferase 7)-treated ECs, and the upregulated VEGFR-2 is beneficial for maintaining the integrity of blood vessels [52]. Nevertheless, NOTCH1 signaling accelerates ECs-mediated adhesion molecules and inflammatory cytokines expression, and NOTCH1 knockdown attenuates inflammation and restrains the inflammatory endothelial phenotype shift through suppressing H3K27ac activity at the NF- $\kappa \mathbf{B}$ -directed inflammatory enhancer sites [53]. Likewise, LPC (lysophosphatidylcholine) is a momentous stimulator for ECs activation and atherosclerosis. LPC-treated ECs overexpress ICAM-1 (intercellular cell adhesion molecule-1) through upregulating mtROS (mitochondrial reactive oxygen species)-modulated site-specific H3K14ac and recruiting more proinflammatory AP-1 binding to the ICAM-1 promoter [54]. And IL-35 can impede the mtROS/H3K14ac/AP-1-mediated ECs activation, demonstrating the promising application of IL-35 for thrombosis in APS patients [54].

Additionally, the improper expression of epigenetics-related enzymes occurs in ECs. For instance, EGCG (epigallocatechin gallate) increases H3K9/14 ac, H3ac, H3K4me3 and H3K9me3 and promotes chromatin relaxation in ECs by affecting epigenome modulators expression including HDAC5 and 7, p300, CREBP, LSD1 (lysine-specific demethylase 1) or KMT2A [55]. Also, the upregulation of epigenetic factors has been observed in anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \mathrm{CXCL4}$ complex-constructed APS ECs model, including MECP2, DNMT3B, TET1, HDAC9 and ARID5B [11]. Thus, inhibitors of epigenetics might be utilized for reversing ECs function. For instance, the inhibitor of HDAC,

VPA (valproic acid) stimulates t-PA (tissue-type plasminogen activator) expression in ECs in relation with the heightened histone acetylation at the t-PA promoter, suggesting the prospective use of valproic acid for impeding endogenous fibrinolysis, ECs impairment and thromboembolism [56]. These data provide critical clues for exploring the impacts of epigenetics on ECs activation and APS pathology as well as optimizing APS diagnosis and therapy [11].

DNA methylation is the most stable epigenetic alteration for maintaining normal ECs function and mediating vascular biology. In vitro APS HUVECs model, anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \mathrm{CXCL4}$ administration restrains the CpGs methylation of TF3 first intron [11], and overexpressed TF3 is involved in ECs-mediated thromboembolism and APS development. In recent, a study has observed the augmented DNMT1 and DNMT3A levels after changing the blood flow both in vitro and in vivo, accordingly hyper-methylating the promoters of HoxA5, Klf (Kruppel-like factor) 3 and Klf4 and enhancing thrombomodulin and MCP-1 expression, which can be reversed by DNMTs inhibitor 5-aza-CdR (5-Aza- $2^{\prime}$ -deoxycytidine) and DNMT3A knockdown [57,58]. Meanwhile, NF- $\kappa \mathbf{B}$ is demonstrated to recruit DNMT3A to the KLF4 promoter and blocks KLF4 transcription, and KLF4 can conversely hinder NF- $\kappa \mathbf{B}$ transcriptional activity by constructing the NF- $\kappa \mathbf{B} / \mathrm{KLF4}$ positive feedback loop [59]. These data indicate the importance of blood flow controlling for affecting ECs-related genes expression and thrombosis process. In addition, overexpressed DNMT3B significantly promotes the CREG (cellular repressor of E1A-stimulated genes) promoter methylation and hinders CREG expression in ECs, further leading to endothelial dysfunction and atherosclerosis that linked to APS-related features [60]. Altogether, intervening DNA methylation might represent a novel therapeutic strategy for ECs-induced atherosclerosis and APS progression.

Increasing ncRNAs participate in ECs activation and vascular inflammatory diseases. LncRNA STEEL predominantly enriches in vascular ECs and controls lots of genes expression, like upregulating eNOS and KLF2 to aggravate inflammation in APS [61]. CircRNA GNAQ also abundantly locates in ECs, but it limits ECs senescence and atherosclerosis in APS [62]. On the other hand, circRNAs have impacts on ECs autophagy and apoptosis. CircRNA HIPK3 suppresses lipid accumulation in ox-LDL (low-density lipoprotein)-treated HUVECs via activating miR-190b/ATG7-induced autophagy [63]. While circRNA VEGFC deteriorates ECs apoptosis via activating miR-338-3p/HIF (hypoxia-inducible factor)-1 $\alpha$ /VEGFA axis [64]. All of these ncRNAs represent the distinctive roles in ECs pathogenesis and vascular disorders of APS patients.

In HUVECs, LPS stimulates miR-17-3p transcription to repress NIBP (NIK and IKK $\beta$ binding protein) expression and NF- $\kappa$ B phosphorylation, further causing the reduction of NF- $\kappa$ B-target genes IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1, VCAM-1 and $E$ -selectin, and the attenuation of inflammation and thrombosis [65]. Nevertheless, supernatant miR-34a-5p, miR-15a-5p, miR-145a-5p, miR-133b-3p, miR-20a-5p, miR-19b-3p, miR-210-3p, miR-296-5p and miR-374a-5p are significantly decreased in aPL IgG-established APS HUVECs model, involving in TF, PAI-1 and VEGF-R1 release and thrombosis formation [51]. Consequently, these dysregulated ncRNAs affect HUVECs-triggered atherothrombosis and orchestrate APS pathogenesis, affording potential therapeutic approaches to APS.

# 6. Epigenetics-induced platelets activation

Platelets are required for APS-correlated thrombotic manifestation and pregnancy loss through manipulating the binding of auto-receptors with anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies and promoting procoagulant factors release, such as thromboxane B2, TF and PF4 (platelet factor 4) [66]. Most importantly, epigenetic drift is a key participant in maintaining platelets homeostasis.

Histone modifications significantly affect platelets count and activation. A population pharmacokinetic/toxicity model detects the reduced number of platelets during continuous $48\mathrm{h}$ intravenously

infusion of HDACi belinostat, displaying the importance of histone deacetylation in promoting platelets production [67]. In addition, the structure and function of healthy platelets would be seriously damaged after exposure to histone H4, and further stimulating platelets ballooning and microparticles release that related with APS thrombotic features [68]. Hence, the part of histone modifications in platelets is worthy probing.

DNA methylation emerges as an indispensable regulator of platelets. PEAR-1 (platelet-endothelial aggregation receptor 1) is a transmembrane receptor, and its DNA methylation implicates in platelets activation in APS via negatively modulating platelets-monocytes conjugates and P-selectin expression, positively mediating inflammation score and the expression of CD11b on PMNs (polymorphonuclear cells) and of L-selectin on both monocytes and PMNs [69]. A pilot study shows that six months of L-carnitine supplementation heighten D-loop methylation in platelets, which influences the LDL-c (LDL-cholesterol) and TC (total cholesterol) levels [70], and L-carnitine supplementation has been applied for cardiovascular protection in the long-term [70]. Besides, mtDNA (mitochondrial DNA) methylation at MT-CO1, MT-CO3 and MT-TL1 can strongly predict the cardiovascular disease incidence, which is linked with APS thrombosis manifestation [71]. On the whole, the enhanced DNA methylation in platelets is tightly correlated with inflammation and thrombosis, and it can function as biomarkers or therapeutic targets in APS context.

It is well-known that activated platelets secret EVs (extracellular vesicles) and miRNAs into blood circulation, and their contributions to platelets activity dominantly affect APS-correlated coagulation physiology. The anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complex from APS patients stimulates healthy platelets to produce MPs (microparticles), and miR-96 and miR26a in MPs are highly expressed. Further, MPs transfer miRNA-96 and miR-26a into HUVECs to hinder HUVECs migration and tube formation through directly blocking P-selectin and PDGFRA (platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha) expression [72]. In addition, bioinformatic analysis has confirmed multiple dysregulated platelets-derived miRNAs, including miR-320a, miR-16-5p, miR-106a-5p, miR320b, miR-15a-5p, miR-15b-5p, miR-195-5p, miR-92a-3p. Top affected miRNAs and their targets primarily manipulate VEGF and MAPK cascades, interleukins secretion, platelets activation and aggregation, subsequently mediating APS clinical symptoms [73]. Similarly, a pilot study of CAD displays that platelets-produced miR-93-5p, miR-126-3p, miR150-5p, miR-423-3p and miR-1180-3p are inversely associated with platelets function [74], which might strengthen hypercoagulable events in APS patients [74]. While endogenous miR-223-3p from pDEVs (platelet-derived EVs) inhibits TF expression and procoagulant activity in monocytes, that reversed by co-transfected miR-223-3p inhibitor [75]. Altogether, these anomalous miRNAs can prevent or promote coagulation physiology in APS patients.

# 7. Epigenetics-induced trophoblasts activation

Trophoblasts play essential roles in placenta implantation and appropriate maternal blood supply to fetus, both endovascular and extravascular interstitial trophoblasts invasion are indispensable for favorable pregnancy. In vitro studies have shown that aPL is involved in trophoblasts-mediated APS pathological mechanisms through directly interacting with the cell surface molecules on trophoblasts or binding with ECs to hinder the ECs-trophoblasts interaction, resulting in inadequate trophoblasts implantation, phenotype switching and APS-related APOs (adverse pregnancy outcomes) [76].

In trophoblasts, the reduced PPAR $\gamma$ delays fetal growth via upregulating H3K4me3 and H3K9ac. The administration of specific PPAR $\gamma$ agonist ciglitazone represses H3K4me3 and H3K9ac enrichment and protects against IUGR (intrauterine growth retardation) [77], and PPAR $\gamma$ antagonist T0070907 augments H3K4me3 and H3K9ac modification and impedes fetal growth [78]. Trophoblasts are classified into side-population trophoblasts, cytotrophoblasts and EVTs (extravillous trophoblasts) populations, each subset has distinctive DNA methylation landscape involving with pregnancy disorders in APS patients. The DNA methylation data of side-population trophoblasts, cytotrophoblasts and extravillous trophoblasts derived from the same first trimester placenta show the most differential DNA methylation locating at promoter regions. These altered methylations at the promoter regions manipulate EMT-related genes during EVTs differentiation. EVTs differentiation is an EMT-like process, thus, understanding DNA methylation features of individual trophoblasts subset benefits the identification of trophoblasts subset and the exploration of APOs pathogenesis in APS patients [84].

There are several inappropriate DNA methylations involved in the regulation of trophoblasts function. For instance, Wnt/β-catenin signaling is a critical regulator of placenta implantation, and the dramatically hypomethylated WNT3 in Wnt/β-catenin signaling augments WNT3 expression and recovers trophoblasts invasion and migration, and further ameliorating APOs in APS patients [85]. Likewise, ZBTB24 is important for fulfilling successful pregnancy, the reduced ZBTB24 downregulates trophoblasts viability, differentiation and migration through inhibiting DNMT3B activity and the E-cadherin promoter methylation [86]. Placental hypoxia and soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFLT1) positively implicate in APS-related poor pregnancy. HIF-1 $\beta$ is identified to boost the hypoxia-induced FLT1 expression and trophoblasts impairment via inducing FLT1 DNA

demethylation in placental trophoblasts [87]. Besides, IGF-2 (insulin-like growth factor-2) and H19 genes-mediated glucose levels are key regulatory factors of fetal growth. High-glucose stimulates IGF-2/H19 expression in trophoblasts by repressing the IGF-2/H19 promoter methylation [88]. Thus, intervening DNA methylation using DNMTs inhibitors or activators may be a crucial therapeutic target for APOs.

Abnormal miRNAs expression triggers trophoblasts and placenta dysfunction and is closely involved in APOs onset in APS patients. In vitro treating trophoblasts with aPL from APS patients dramatically boosts cellular and exosome miRNAs expression correlating with TLR signaling activation, including miR-146a, miR-155, and miR-210 [89]. Among these, the increased miR-146a in trophoblasts deteriorates inflammation in obstetric APS by activating TLR8 and producing IL-8 [90]. Apart from these miRNAs, miR-133 accelerates the apoptosis of human placental trophoblasts via stimulating Rho/ROCK signaling and aggravates the oxidative stress injury of trophoblasts by inhibiting JAK2/STAT3 pathway, and significantly elevated miR-133 could positively predict APS-related APOs [91,92]. Nevertheless, some miRNAs have the protective effects on placental trophoblasts. For instance, miR-126-3p inhibits proinflammation signaling in trophoblasts, and the downregulated miR-126-3p might contribute to inflammatory response in APS [93]. Overexpressed miR-132 suppresses trophoblasts apoptosis and safeguards invasiveness and viability via negatively impacting DAPK-1 expression [94].

LncRNAs and circRNAs also play important roles in trophoblasts function during embryo development. CircRNA ZUFSP promotes trophoblasts migration and invasion in vitro through sponging miR-203 to affect STOX1 expression [95]. Additionally, circRNA CRAMP1L is reportedly to ameliorate trophoblast physiology via regulating MSP/ RON axis, and its reduction can forecast the risk of APOs in pregnant APS patients [96]. LncRNA MEG3 is lowly expressed in placental villi during PE (preeclampsia), and highly-expressed MEG3 facilitates trophoblasts implantation, proliferation and invasion, while restrains trophobasts apoptosis through promoting the binding of EZH2 to the RASA1 promoter and activating RAS/MAPK signaling [97]. Conversely, the high level of IncRNA NEAT1 upregulates ESRα expression by sponging miR-18a-5p, thus inducing trophoblasts apoptosis [98]. These altered miRNAs may positively or negatively affect the function of trophoblasts in APS patients.

# 8. Potential epigenetics for APS diagnosis and therapy

In recent years, there are continuous advancement in investigating the regulatory mechanisms of epigenetic factors in APS development. Moreover, a versatile number of epigenetic biomarkers have been proposed to specifically engage in the molecular and clinical changes in APS patients, providing a scaffold for probing novel diagnostic and therapeutic modalities.

Researches have established that histone modifications are tightly engaged in inflammation, thrombosis and pregnant morbidity in APS patients. The part of inhibitors of histone modifications-correlated enzymes cannot be ignored. EZH2, MLL1 (mixed-lineage leukemia-1) and SUV39H1 are most popular HMTs (histone methyltransferases) (Fig. 2, Table 1). EZH2-induced H3K27me3 is vital for modulating immune system, and selective EZH2 inhibitors, zld1039 and tazemetostat might be novel therapeutic opportunities for restraining inflammation in APS by downregulating NF- $\kappa \mathrm{B}$ p65 signal [99,100]. MLL1 is crucial for active H3K4me3, which heightens macrophages-mediated inflammation cytokines release [101]. And MLL1 inhibitors, MM-102, WDR5-0103 and OCIR-9429 could be are suitable for APS therapy via disturbing MLL1-WDR5 interaction and H3K4me3 enrichment [102,103]. However, SUV39H1-activated H3K9me3 can downregulate IL-8-triggered inflammation, and its inhibitor chaetocin strengthens inflammation by repressing SUV39H1/H3K9me3 levels [104]. Histone demethylase LSD1 is identified to enhance TLR4 transcription by removing H3K9me and H3K9me2, and its inhibitor TCP (tranylcypromine) directly inactivates TLR4/NF- $\kappa \mathrm{B} / \mathrm{JNK}$ axis and ameliorates inflammation (Table 1) [105].

Curcumin and A-485 are frequently used inhibitors of HATs (histone acetyltransferases) (Fig. 2, Table 1). Curcumin prevents venous thrombosis formation in a mouse deep venous thrombosis model via impacting miR-499-mediated PTEN/VEGF/Ang-1 signaling [106]. Also, curcumin has an important part in suppressing inflammation and oxidative stress in multiple autoimmune diseases [107]. A-485, a selective inhibitor of CBP/p300 HAT activity, suppresses genes transcription enriched in proinflammation-correlated pathways and relieving APS manifestations [108]. The inhibitors of HDAC mainly comprise tubastatin A, panobinostat, romidepsin, belinostat, VPA, SAHA (suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid), and SB (sodium butyrate) (Table 1). Tubastatin A, a highly selective HDAC6 inhibitor, has been demonstrated to preferentially repress inflammation in ECs through regulating ROS/NF- $\kappa$ B signaling [109]. Panobinostat treatment blocks LPS-induced inflammation and enhances Treg accumulation, further restoring immunity imbalance [110]. Belinostat serves as a pan-HDAC inhibitor, it restrains TLR2/MyD88 signaling activation and HDAC3 expression while promotes NF- $\kappa$ B p65 acetylation, leading to the alleviated inflammation [111], and it has entered the phase II clinical trial [112]. VPA is considered as the HDAC1 and 2 inhibitor, and it can specifically upregulates vascular endothelial t-PA and restrains arterial and venous thrombosis [113]. Nonetheless, another HDAC inhibitor, SB stimulates H3K9ac enrichment and greatly upregulates inflammatory mediators TNF- $\alpha$ and NOS2 (Fig. 2, Table 1) [114]. Taken together, drugs targeting histone modifications play a double role in APS treatment, and effective epigenetic drugs would make considerable progress when combining with antithrombotic and anti-inflammatory drugs.

Nowadays, DNA methylation is one of the widely studied epigenetic changes due to its relationship with APS development. DNMTs are responsible for DNA hypomethylation and hypermethylation, contributing to inadequate genes expression engaging in various clinical manifestations in APS patients. Given the complicated pathogenesis of APS, the application of epigenetics-related drugs is not prevalent in APS diagnosis and treatment. Therefore, it is required for understanding more profound epigenetic mechanisms and probing effective epigenetic drugs, which will afford new approaches to APS diagnosis, therapy and monitoring.

# 9. Conclusion

Evidence of epigenetic changes is observed in APS, and different DNA methylation, ncRNAs expression and histone PTMs selectively regulate the transcription of prothrombotic and proinflammatory genes. It is noteworthy that these altered epigenetic markers and genes expression profiles might be beneficial for deeply investigating the accurate pathogenesis of APS, and further providing foundation for the differential diagnosis and effective treatment of APS. In current, altered circulating miRNAs have been identified in APS patients, further facilitating the precise diagnosis of APS. The inhibitors of HDAC, HAT, HMT, histone demethylase and DNMTs are extensively indicated to alleviate inflammation and thrombosis, and some of them have been entered into clinical trials to ensure the safety. Thereby, these epigenetic inhibitors represent an excellent prospect for APS therapy, especially the combined treatment of different kinds of epigenetic drugs with antithrombotic or anti-inflammatory drugs might be more effective. But their clinic safety needs to be testified in APS patients.
