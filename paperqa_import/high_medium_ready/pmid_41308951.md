# ABSTRACT

Background: Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is closely associated with ischemic stroke. However, optimal treatment for APS-related stroke remains unestablished, as current guidelines are based on outdated studies and expert opinion rather than high-quality clinical trials. Evidence on antiplatelet agents other than aspirin, such as clopidogrel, in APS-related stroke is particularly limited. Given the relatively young age of patients with APS and the burden of warfarin use, verifying its necessity is crucial. This study compares clopidogrel-based antiplatelet therapy and warfarin for secondary prevention in APS-related stroke.

Methods: APS-STROKE is an exploratory, multicenter, prospective, randomized, open, blinded-endpoint clinical trial. Adult patients with definite APS and a history of ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA) will be included. Patients with high-risk APS (triple positivity or persistently high titers of anti-cardiolipin or anti- $\beta 2$ glycoprotein I antibodies), systemic lupus erythematosus, or other major indications for continued antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy will be excluded. Participants will be randomized 1:1 to receive clopidogrel-based antiplatelet therapy or warfarin. More than 200 patients are planned for inclusion across 32 stroke centers. The primary endpoint is a composite of any death, major adverse cardiovascular events, systemic thromboembolic events, and major bleeding during at least 4 years of follow-up. Secondary endpoints include major adverse cardiovascular events, ischemic stroke, any bleeding, major bleeding, intracranial bleeding, clinically relevant non-major bleeding, any death, and thrombosis-related death.

Conclusion: This study will provide valuable information on the optimal secondary prevention strategy for APS-related stroke.

Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05995600; CRIS: KCT0008900.

# 1. Introduction

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is an autoimmune disorder characterized by positive antiphospholipid antibody (aPL) tests and is associated with thrombosis or pregnancy complications [1]. Ischemic stroke is a common manifestation of APS [2], with up to $40\%$ of patients with cryptogenic stroke testing positive for aPL in our retrospective analysis [3]. However, evidence for optimal prevention in APS-related stroke remains limited.

Primary prevention for asymptomatic aPL carriers may include low-dose aspirin in those with high-risk aPL profiles; however, the supporting evidence is limited, and current guideline recommendations are not fully aligned [4-6], making its role in thrombosis prevention uncertain. For secondary prevention in patients with APS-related stroke, current rheumatology and hematology guidelines recommend warfarin for APS-related arterial thrombosis. However, its superiority over antiplatelet therapy has never been demonstrated in high-quality randomized trials. These recommendations are largely based on older retrospective studies and expert opinion [4-6], which often included both arterial and venous thrombosis patients [7-9], despite differing pathophysiology and treatment responses. Additionally, ischemic stroke's diverse causes, which affect treatment outcomes, are not fully considered in the guidelines.

Stroke guidelines also suggest using warfarin for ischemic stroke patients with APS, despite a low evidence level (recommendation level 2a; evidence level C) [10]. This recommendation is based on a small study of 20 patients comparing aspirin alone with warfarin plus aspirin [11]. Retrospective studies suggest antiplatelet agents may be as effective and safe as warfarin [12,13], but data on the role of antiplatelet drugs, especially clopidogrel are lacking [4,10].

Considering warfarin's limitations—higher bleeding risk, food/drug interactions, dose management challenges, and frequent blood tests—there is a need for high-quality prospective studies comparing warfarin and antiplatelet therapies in APS-related stroke. This study aims to provide robust evidence on the efficacy and safety of clopidogrel-based antiplatelet therapy versus warfarin for secondary prevention, addressing a critical knowledge gap in stroke treatment guidelines.

# 2. Methods

# 2.1. Study design

The comparison of clopidogrel-based antiplatelet therapy versus warfarin as a secondary prevention strategy for AntiPhospholipid Syndrome-related STROKE (APS-STROKE) is an exploratory, multicenter, prospective, randomized, open, blinded-endpoint (PROBE) study. The study flowchart is summarized in Fig. 1. The institutional review board of Seoul National University Hospital (IRB No. H-2304-096-1425) approved this study on June 1, 2023, with subsequent approvals obtained from the institutional review boards of all participating sites. Written informed consent is required for all participants or their caregivers/guardians. The first participant was enrolled on February 20, 2024. As of April 2025, 32 stroke centers in Korea are actively recruiting participants for the study. The APS-STROKE is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05995600) and Clinical Research Information Service (KCT0008900).

# 2.2. Patient population

Patients with a history of ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA) and diagnosed with APS are eligible. Ischemic stroke includes cerebral infarction and central/branch retinal artery occlusion. TIA is defined by the sudden onset of focal neurological deficits, presumed to result from cerebral or retinal artery ischemia, resolving completely within $24\mathrm{h}$ . TIA diagnosis requires confirmation by a neurologist through clinical and neuroimaging evaluation.

# 2.3. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria include: 1) adults aged 19 years or older; 2) a history of ischemic stroke or TIA; 3) fulfilment of the Sydney criteria for APS diagnosis (Table 1) [1]; and 4) informed consent provided by the patients or their caregivers/guardians. Exclusion criteria comprise: 1) high-risk APS patients; 2) those with systemic lupus erythematosus; 3) those with a compelling indication for antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy other than the index event of ischemic stroke or TIA (e.g., atrial fibrillation or recent percutaneous coronary intervention); 4) pregnant or breastfeeding women, or those expecting pregnancy during the study;

and 5) candidates deemed unsuitable for continuous participation in the study for over four years. High-risk APS patients are defined as those positive for all three aPL tests (lupus anticoagulant, anti-cardiolipin antibody, and anti- $\beta 2$ glycoprotein I antibody) or with persistently high titers $(\geq 80~\mathrm{U / mL})$ of anti-cardiolipin or anti- $\beta 2$ glycoprotein I antibodies [14-16].

# 2.4. Baseline characteristics

At screening, demographic information (age, sex, race, smoking history, alcohol intake) and medical history will be collected. Index stroke information will be obtained through medical record reviews and neuroimaging. Physical assessments, including modified Rankin Scale, Barthel Index, vital signs, height, and weight, will be performed. Baseline laboratory evaluations will include complete blood count, chemistry, serology, lipid panel, coagulation tests, urinalysis, and aPL tests. A human chorionic gonadotropin test will be performed for female participants of childbearing age not on contraception.

# 2.5. Randomization and treatment

Participants meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria and providing informed consent will be randomized 1:1 into either the clopidogrel-based antiplatelet group or the warfarin group. Randomization will be managed through cubeCDMS (https://www.cubecdms.com), using a secure web-based application. Stratified block randomization will be used, stratified by participating centers and stroke etiology. The clopidogrel-based antiplatelet group will receive clopidogrel $75\mathrm{mg}$ daily, with the option to add other antiplatelet agents (e.g., aspirin or cilostazol) at the investigators' discretion. The warfarin group will be prescribed warfarin to target a prothrombin time-international normalized ratio (PT INR) of 2.0-3.0. Participants already receiving antithrombotic therapy before study enrollment continued or switched their regimen based on the randomized treatment assignment.

Participants will be assessed at 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24, 36, and 48 months after randomization until the trial concludes. Each visit will collect clinical event data, drug compliance, and concomitant medication use. For any endpoint event, whether reported at a scheduled visit or prompting an unscheduled visit, investigators will record the actual date of occurrence. PT INR will be checked for warfarin group participants at each visit. Follow-up laboratory tests, including complete blood count and chemistry, will be performed at 6 and 12 months. aPL testing will be conducted at 3, 12, and 48 months (Fig. 2).

# 2.6. Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint of the APS-STROKE study is time to a composite of any death, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), systemic thromboembolic events, and major bleeding during at least 4

years of follow-up. MACE includes stroke (ischemic or hemorrhagic), TIA, and acute coronary syndrome (ST-elevation myocardial infarction, non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction, or unstable angina requiring hospitalization). Major bleeding is defined as Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (BARC) type 3 or 5 bleeding (Table 2) [17].

# 2.7. Secondary endpoints

Secondary endpoints include time to MACE, ischemic stroke, any bleeding, major bleeding, intracranial bleeding, clinically relevant non-major bleeding, any death, and thrombosis-related death. Clinically relevant non-major bleeding is defined as overt bleeding that does not qualify as major bleeding but meets at least one of the following criteria: 1) requires medical intervention; 2) results in hospitalization or increased level of care; or 3) prompts an in-person evaluation [18].

# 2.8. Data monitoring body

An external Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will oversee participant safety and maintain study integrity. The DSMB will conduct a blinded secondary evaluation of endpoints, finalizing the investigators' initial assessments. The DSMB comprises three members: a statistician and two physicians, both of whom are neurologists not involved in the study.

# 2.9. Study organization

The executive committee of the APS-STROKE study oversees protocol adherence and ethical standards, assists in protocol development, makes key trial decisions, and facilitates communication with stakeholders.

# 2.10. Sample size estimates

APS-STROKE is an exploratory study without confirmatory hypothesis testing for two reasons. First, this study aims to compare clopidogrel-based antiplatelet therapy with warfarin, the treatment suggested by current guidelines for ischemic stroke in APS patients. However, this recommendation is based on outdated retrospective studies, and warfarin is not definitively established as the standard treatment. Treating warfarin as the standard in the statistical hypothesis testing may inadvertently reinforce this assumption among stroke physicians. Second, a formal non-inferiority hypothesis would require an unfeasibly large sample size due to the low expected event rates. Assuming a $4\%$ annual event rate in the warfarin group based on recent trials [14,19,20], more than 570 patients would be required to demonstrate non-inferiority with a one-sided significance level of 0.025 and $80\%$ power. Thus, the investigators determined the study size based on the maximum possible enrollment, aiming to enroll more than 200

# 2.11. Statistical analyses

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the clopidogrel-based antiplatelet group and the warfarin group will be compared. Continuous variables will be analyzed using either the $t$ -test or the Wilcoxon Rank-Sum test, depending on normality. Categorical variables will be compared using the chi-square or Fisher's exact test.

The primary endpoints will be analyzed using the full analysis set, which includes all randomized participants who have taken the study drug at least once, excluding those who violated inclusion/exclusion criteria. A per-protocol analysis will also be conducted for participants without significant protocol violations (e.g., inclusion/exclusion breaches, randomization errors, contraindicated drug use). The Kaplan-Meier method will estimate the incidence of primary endpoint events during the study period, with differences between groups compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards models will be used for survival analysis to determine the hazard ratio for primary endpoints. Potential confounders will be selected based on the $p$ -values from the baseline comparisons between the treatment groups; variables with $p < 0.05$ will be included in the multivariable models along with age and sex. Predefined subgroup analyses for the primary endpoint will be conducted according to age, sex, major vascular risk factors, aPL profile, and prior antithrombotic therapy. Secondary endpoints will be evaluated in the full analysis set using the same time-to-event framework as the primary endpoints, employing Kaplan-Meier estimates, log-rank tests, and Cox proportional hazards models with identical covariate adjustments. Statistical significance will be set at $p < 0.05$ (two-sided).

The safety set will include all randomized participants who provided informed consent. Adverse events, adverse drug reactions, and serious adverse events occurring after study drug administration will be compared using the chi-square or Fisher's exact test.

# 3. Discussion

Ischemic stroke and TIA are among the most common thrombotic manifestations of APS. In our retrospective analysis, patients with APS-related stroke often had small-sized, low-volume infarct lesions [21]. Compared to thrombosis in other organs, the brain is more susceptible to exhibiting symptoms from small clots, which may make ischemic stroke

more likely to be detected in APS cases with a lower thrombotic burden. Given that APS-related stroke often presents as a minor ischemic stroke or TIA in relatively young patients, the necessity of lifelong warfarin therapy warrants further investigation.

Following ethical approval, new diagnostic criteria for APS were introduced by rheumatology societies in August 2023 [22]. The revision aimed to reduce patient heterogeneity in clinical research by raising the diagnostic threshold, increasing specificity but lowering sensitivity. For stroke physicians, however, sensitivity may be more critical. Ischemic stroke may be the first sign of thrombosis in mild APS cases due to the brain's sensitivity to small thrombi. Positive aPL findings should not be disregarded if they fall short of the 2023 criteria, especially in cases where APS is the only potential cause of stroke. If APS-STROKE adopts the new criteria without considering these points, patients diagnosed with APS according to the 2006 Sydney criteria but not the 2023 criteria will again face a gap in treatment strategies within stroke guidelines. Therefore, this study adopted the 2006 Sydney criteria, not the 2023 new criteria, for diagnosing APS.

As an underrecognized cause of ischemic stroke, APS has never received significant attention in the mainstream stroke community. Regardless of the outcomes, this study will provide new valuable evidence concerning secondary prevention for ischemic stroke/TIA patients with APS compared to previous studies. We anticipate our research will contribute to updates in clinical guidelines for APS-related stroke.
