# ABSTRACT

Anti-phosphatidylserine/prothrombin antibodies (aPS/PT) are classified as non-criteria antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL), and are strongly associated with thrombosis and pregnancy complications linked to antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). This study aimed to investigate whether aPS/PT positivity is associated with adverse outcomes in vitro fertilization (IVF). The study included infertile women who tested positive aPS/PT and underwent IVF cycles, as well as infertile controls with pure tubal etiology. We compared the impact of aPS/PT on baseline and clinical characteristics, immune-related indicators, IVF laboratory metrics, and pregnancy outcomes. Women with aPS/PT exhibited lower numbers of retrieved oocytes, embryos, and both perfect and available embryos, as well as reduced rates of blastocyst formation. Furthermore, an imbalanced Th17/Treg ratio and significantly elevated serum IL-17A levels were observed in women with aPS/PT compared to controls. In conclusion, the presence of aPS/PT is associated with adverse IVF and pregnancy outcomes. Early screening for aPS/PT and appropriate consultation for couples undergoing IVF-ET should be considered. Additionally, specific immune and inflammatory mechanisms related to aPS/PT warrant further investigation.

# 1. Introduction

Anti-phosphatidylserine/prothrombin antibodies (aPS/PT) are classified as the non-criteria antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL), and represent a heterogeneous group of autoantibodies that target phospholipids and phospholipid-binding proteins (Devreese, 2023). Prothrombin (PT) is a vitamin K-dependent clotting factor synthesized by the liver, which plays a critical role in blood clot formation (Girolami et al., 2018). Phosphatidylserine (PS) is a negatively charged phospholipid that constitutes a component of the phospholipid bilayer of cell membranes, primarily found in the inner layer. It is composed of glycerol phosphate, fatty acids, and the amino acid serine. Phosphatidylserine is essential for various cellular functions, particularly in signal transduction, apoptosis,

intercellular interactions, and neural transmission (Hussain et al., 2024). PS forms a phospholipid-protein complex with PT, and antibody against this complex, referred to as aPS/PT, are categorized into IgG and IgM types. Positive aPS/PT results are significantly correlated with thrombotic events and can predict their occurrence. It is believed that aPS/PT increases the risk of thrombosis in systemic lupus erythematosus (Zhang et al., 2023b). Recent studies have shown that aPS/PT and aPT are associated with the pathogenesis of antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). There is a statistically significant correlation between positive aPS/PT IgG/IgM and thrombotic events, with IgG antibodies being particularly associated with venous thrombosis (Egri et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2018). These antibodies activate endothelial cells, platelets, and leukocytes by binding to phospholipids and phospholipid-binding

proteins on the cell surface, thus creating an intravascular environment conducive to in situ thrombus formation, while also promoting other autoimmune and inflammatory complications (Knight et al., 2023). Recent studies have confirmed that aPS/PT IgM is an independent risk factor for pregnancy loss, demonstrating a positive linear correlation, coagulation indicators and immune biomarkers may play an significant role in this context (Xiang et al., 2024). It has been suggested that aPS/PT antibodies may serve as markers for antiphospholipid-related pregnancy complications, including miscarriage, preterm birth, intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR), and preeclampsia (Canti et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2023a)). The incorporation of aPS/PT testing into the diagnostic workup of APS patients has been shown to enhance the identification of affected individuals (Ding et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2022; Saadalla and Nandakumar, 2024)). However, the impact of aPS/PT positivity in women undergoing in vitro fertilization and embryo transfer (IVF-ET) has not been thoroughly investigated. Therefore, our study aimed to evaluate the effects of aPS/PT on IVF and pregnancy outcomes. We analyzed various factors, including baseline and clinical characteristics, markers of oocyte and embryo quality (such as the number of oocytes and embryos, MII oocyte rate, blastocyst formation rate, perfect and available embryo rates), pregnancy outcomes (which encompass implantation rate, clinical pregnancy rate and miscarriage rate), as well as indicators of immunity and inflammation (including immune cells and cytokines). This investigation aims to provide valuable insights into whether aPS/PT should be considered for women undergoing infertility treatment.

# 2. Materials and methods

# 2.1. Study population

This study recruited infertile women undergoing IVF-ET cycles and aPS/PT assays at the Reproductive Medicine Center, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, First Affiliated Hospital of USTC, from

January 2023 to August 2024. The study received approval from the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of USTC, and all participants signed a consent form prior to their inclusion in the study.

The inclusion criteria of the study group were women who were 1) aged between 20 and 40 years old, 2) tested positive for aPS/PT, 3) had not received any medications prior to their first IVF cycle, and 4) could confirm IVF and pregnancy outcomes. Exclusion criteria included 1) chromosomal or anatomical abnormalities, 2) hormone or metabolic disorder, 3) typical aPL (aCL, aβ2-GP1, and LA) positive, 4) severe reproductive system infections or malformations. The control group was randomly selected from women with infertility due solely to tubal factors. They also underwent testing for aPS/PT antibodies, which returned negative results. These women underwent IVF-ET cycles during the same period, and they had not received any drug treatment prior to their IVF cycles. They were matched with the cases based on age and body mass index (BMI).

In total, 57 infertile women who tested positive for aPS/PT and 87 infertile controls were included in the study, as shown in Fig. 1. To eliminate potential interference from different ovulation induction protocols on the analysis results, both cases and controls were further categorized into the GnRH agonist protocol group (26 women with aPS/PT and 47 controls) and GnRH antagonist protocol group (31 women with aPS/PT and 40 controls).

# 2.2. aPS/PT antibodies assay

On the third day of the menstrual cycle, $5\mathrm{ml}$ of venous blood was collected from each woman using a vacuum EDTA anticoagulation blood collection tube. The blood samples were taken from the antecubital vein. The samples were centrifuged at $1500~\mathrm{rpm}$ at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for 5 minutes to obtain the serum. Serum aPS/PT antibodies were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) (QUANTA Lite®, Inova Diagnostics, Inc. San Diego, CA USA). # 2.3. Cytokine assay

To detect serum cytokines, a Th1/Th2/Th17 Cytometric Bead Array (CBA) kit (cell-genebio) was utilized. This kit is specifically designed for the detection of human cytokines, including IL-2, IL-4, IL-5, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, IL-12p70, TNF- $\alpha$ , IFN- $\alpha$ , IFN- $\gamma$ , IL-1 $\beta$ , and IL-17A.

Peripheral blood samples were collected on the third day of the menstrual period. The procedure involved centrifuging $25\mu \mathrm{l}$ of the captured microsphere mixture at $200\mathrm{g}$ for 5 minutes, discarding the supernatant, and then adding an equal volume of microsphere buffer to resuspend the microspheres. This mixture was incubated in the dark for 15 minutes.

Subsequently, an equal volume of the sample, capture microspheres, and fluorescence detection reagent were combined and incubated at room temperature for 2.5 hours. Following this centrifugation was performed at $200\mathrm{g}$ for 5 minutes, and the supernatant was discarded. Finally, the samples were resuspended in $300~\mu \mathrm{l}$ of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and analyzed using a Beckman flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, CA, USA). Standard curves were generated according to the manufacturer's instructions, and the data were analyzed using Flow Jo Software (Tree Star, Ashland, USA).

# 2.4. Th17 and Treg cells assay

On the third day of the menstrual period, $5\mathrm{ml}$ of venous blood was collected from each woman using vacuum anticoagulant blood collection tubes. The blood samples were obtained from the antecubital vein. T cell subsets and serum cytokine levels were analyzed using Flow Cytometry within 6 hours of venipuncture. To isolate peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), density gradient centrifugation was performed at $400\mathrm{g}$ for 20 minutes. The isolated PBMCs were subsequently incubated at $37^{\circ}C$ with $5\%$ CO2 for 3-4 hours. Following surface staining, the cells were fixed and permeabilized using a Fixation/Permeabilization Solution kit (BD Bioscience). during which intracellular staining was also performed. Fluorescent antibodies specific to T cell subsets were employed for labeling, including anti-human CD3 peridinin chlorophyll protein-cyanine5.5 (PerCP-cy5.5), anti-human CD8 brilliant violet 510 BV510), anti-human IL-17A PE, anti-human CD25 brilliant violet 421 BV421), and anti-human Foxp3 allophycocyanin (APC). All antibodies were procured from BD Biosciences. The different T cell subsets, including Th17 cells $\mathrm{(CD3^{+}CD8^{+}IL - 17A^{+})}$ ,and Treg cells $\mathrm{(CD3^{+}CD8^{+}CD25^{high}}$ $\mathrm{Foxp3^{+}}$ ),were detected using a Beckman flow cytometer. The data obtained were analyzed using FlowJo software from FlowJo, LLC. Isotype controls were used as negative controls during the analysis process.

# 2.5. Ovulation induction protocols, egg retrieval, insemination method and embryo transfer

Ovulation induction protocols, including the GnRH agonist long protocol and GnRH antagonist protocol, were conducted as described in our previous study (Wu et al., 2023). For the GnRH agonist long protocol, $250~\mu \mathrm{g}$ of recombinant human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG, Ovitrelle®, Merck Serono, Switzerland) was administered when the diameter of two or three follicles reached $17\mathrm{mm}$ , or when at least one follicle reached $18\mathrm{mm}$ while both $0.1\mathrm{mg}$ of triptorelin acetate injection (Jinsai, Changchun China) and $250~\mu \mathrm{g}$ of recombinant human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG, Ovitrelle®, Merck Serono, Switzerland) for the GnRH antagonist protocol. Oocyte retrieval was performed 36 hours after hCG injection. All patients received conventional IVF as

fertilization method. After fertilization, observe the fertilization status at 18-20 hours. The presence of two pronuclei (2PN) indicates normal fertilization. One or two perfect embryos, which were determined by the attending physician, were selected in fresh ET cycle. And others embryos were frozen, waiting for next frozen embryo transfer.

# 2.6. Embryo quality assessment

The classification of embryo ratings was carried out as previously described (Wu et al., 2022). Based on the number, size, and degree of fragmentation of the blastomeres, the quality of the embryos on day 3 is graded as follows: Grade 1: 6-8 evenly sized blastomeres, uniform in size, with no fragmentation. Grade 2: 6-8 evenly sized blastomeres, uniform in size, with a fragmentation rate of less than $20\%$ . Grade 3: 4-6 blastomeres that are uneven or irregular in shape, with a fragmentation rate of $20 - 50\%$ . Grade 4: The embryo has a fragmentation rate exceeding $50\%$ , and the blastomeres may be ruptured, contracted, or darkened, and are considered non-viable. Embryos classified as grades I and II were considered good-quality or perfect embryos, while those classified as grades I, II, and III were considered available embryos. On days 5 or 6 after fertilization, when blastocysts are obtained, they are graded according to the Gardner blastocyst grading system, which primarily assesses the degree of expansion and hatching status, as well as the quality of the Inner Cell Mass (ICM) and Trophectoderm (TE). Here are the specific grading criteria: Blastocyst development stage (expansion and hatching status): 1: The blastocoel is less than half the volume of the embryo. 2: The blastocoel is half or more of the embryo's volume. 3: The blastocyst is fully expanded, with the blastocoel filling the entire embryo. 4: The blastocyst is expanded, with the blastocoel volume larger than that of the early embryo. 5: The hatching blastocyst, where the trophectoderm begins to emerge through the zona pellucida. 6: The hatched blastocyst, which has completely escaped from the zona pellucida. Inner Cell Mass (ICM) quality: A: The ICM consists of many tightly packed cells. B: The ICM consists of a few cells that may be loosely grouped. C: The ICM consists of very few cells. Trophectoderm (TE) quality: A: Many cells form a cohesive epithelium. B: A few cells form a loose epithelium. C: Very few large cells. Blastocysts graded as AA, AB, BA, BB were classified as high-quality blastocysts (Irani et al., 2018).

# 2.7. The aPS/PT treatments

Women with positive aPS/PT antibodies may undergo two consecutive fresh IVF-ET cycles. During the first fresh cycle, no relevant immune-related treatment is administered. Following this initial cycle, women with low-quality embryos or those who have experienced failed 1 fresh embryo transfer or have 2 times frozen embryo transfer will have their associated antibodies, including the aPS/PT test, evaluated. If the test results are positive for aPS/PT antibodies, a treatment plan will be implemented, which includes low-dose aspirin (LDA, $100\mathrm{mg}$ , orally, daily) and hydroxychloroquine sulfate $(100\mathrm{mg},$ twice daily) for a duration of 1–3 months. After embryo transfer, treatment with low molecular weight heparin (LMWH, $4000\mathrm{U}$ , daily, subcutaneous injection) will commence. Women with positive aPS/PT antibodies who have frozen embryos will undergo treatment prior to proceeding with frozen embryo transfer. However, if some women do not have any available embryos for transfer, they will enter a new IVF cycle after completing the LDA and HCQ treatment.

# 2.8. Statistical Analysis

Quantitative data are presented as mean $\pm$ standard deviation (SD). The $t$ -test is used for analysis, and differences in rates are assessed with a chi-squared test or Fisher's exact test. All statistical analyses are conducted using SPSS Version 26.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Statistical significance is defined as $p < 0.05$ (two-tailed).

We first compared the clinical characteristics of aPS/PT-positive patients with those of the control group. # 3.4. Serum cytokines levels

During pregnancy, it is essential to maintain a balance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines (Meyyazhagan et al., 2023). The presence of antiphospholipid antibodies may also contribute to an increase in pro-inflammatory cytokines (Wu et al., 2022). Additionally, we measured cytokine levels between the two groups. As shown in Table 3, women with aPS/PT exhibited significantly higher serum levels of IFN- $\gamma$ $3.3\pm 1.2$ versus $2.8\pm 0.9$ $p < 0.05$ and IFN- $\alpha$ $2.3$ $\pm 0.9$ versus $1.9\pm 0.5$ $p < 0.05$ ) compared to the control group. However, no statistically significant differences were observed in the serum levels of IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, TNF- $\alpha$ IL-17A, IL-1 $\beta$ IL-5, IL-12P70, and IL-8 between the two groups.

# 4. Discussion

Antiphospholipid antibodies are the most important antibodies associated with pregnancy complications, including miscarriage. The diagnostic criteria include anti-cardiolipin antibody (aCL), anti- $\beta 2$ glycoprotein-1 antibody (a2-GP1), and lupus anticoagulant (LA) as laboratory indicators (Caraiola et al., 2023; Pignatelli et al., 2020; Schreiber et al., 2018). However, some patients repeatedly test negative for aPLs (aCL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ LA yet still experience severe obstetric complications. Recent research has identified phosphatidylethanolamine antibodies, and anti-prothrombin antibodies as non-diagnostic standard antibodies (Liu et al., 2022; Pignatelli et al., 2020; Truglia et al., 2022). These antibodies are closely linked to pathological pregnancies, and may result in complications such as recurrent miscarriage, preterm birth, stillbirth, preeclampsia, fetal growth restriction, and fetal distress (D'Ippolito et al., 2023; Zigon et al., 2015).

PT is a vitamin K-dependent clotting factor, while PS is an anionic phospholipid. Together, they can form a phospholipid-protein complex. The combination of these two components alter the spatial structure of the promonomer, resulting in the formation of a new exposure site. The aPS/PT complex can bind to this site and exert its function. This complex can be categorized into two types: IgG and IgM. aPS/PT-IgG is significantly associated with both arterial and venous thrombosis, whereas aPS/PT-IgM serves as an independent risk factor for arterial thrombosis (Shi et al., 2018; Tonello et al., 2019). The impact of aPS/PT on obstetric complications and pregnancy outcomes has been documented. Zigon et al. collected serum from patients with Obstetric Antiphospholipid Syndrome (OAPS) to detect relevant antiphospholipid antibodies. They found that aPS/PT was the only antibody linked to early recurrent miscarriage, late pregnancy morbidity, and preterm birth (Zigon et al., 2015). Additionally, retrospective studies have indicated that aPS/PT antibodies possess effective diagnostic value for Chinese OAPS and seronegative OAPS (SN-OAPS) patients, and may predict the potential risk of obstetric complications (Zhang et al., 2023a). A recent study highlighted that aPS/PT IgM is an independent risk factor for pregnancy loss and is linearly positively correlated with pregnancy loss (Xiang et al., 2024). However, the effect of aPS/PT on IVF outcomes has not been systematically studied. In our study, we reported that aPS/PT antibodies were associated with adverse outcomes in IVF, including significantly lower number of perfect and usable embryos, reduced normal fertilization rates, lower blastocyst formation rates, and diminished rates of clinical pregnancies, implantation, and persistent pregnancies. These findings suggest that aPS/PT positivity may negatively

The presence of aPL is a key hallmark of APS and can activate various cells, including endothelial cells, monocytes, and neutrophils, resulting in alterations in immunity and inflammation (Knight et al., 2023). Consequently, we conducted further research to investigate the immunological and inflammatory status in women who tested positive for aPS/PT. We measured the proportions of specific T helper cell subsets, namely Th17 and Treg, as well as serum cytokine levels in these women. Our study revealed that women who were aPS/PT positive exhibited significantly increased proportions of Th17 cells in their peripheral blood, while the proportions of Treg cells were significantly decreased. Imbalances in Th17/Treg ratios have been reported in various autoimmune diseases (Jin and Liu, 2023; Wang et al., 2023), Our research findings align with these previous studies.

T helper cells can produce secretory cytokines and participate in essential functions such as immune regulation (Saravia et al., 2019). Consequently, serum cytokine levels often reflect the status of T cell immunity. Our study found that women who are aPS/PT positive exhibited higher levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines associated with Th17 cells, such as IL-17A. Additionally, we observed that IFN- $\gamma$ levels were significantly elevated in aPS/PT positive patients compared to the control group. Previous studies have indicated that Th1 cytokines, such as IFN- $\gamma$ and TNF- $\alpha$ , directly promote cell apoptosis and inhibit the proliferation of human granulocytes (Jiao et al., 2021). These findings align with earlier research that reported increased levels of pro-inflammatory and pro-thrombotic cytokines in aPL-positive patients (Erkan et al., 2014). Collectively, these results suggest that aPS/PT positive women undergoing IVF are in a pro-inflammatory state. This condition may not only affect oocyte and embryo quality but also impact subsequent processes such as embryo implantation. Previous reports have indicated that imbalances in the Th17/Treg ratio can lead to reproductive failure, including spontaneous abortion and recurrent implantation failure (RIF) (Ghaebi et al., 2019; Piccinni et al., 2021). Therefore, in our study, the imbalanced Th17/Treg ratio and elevated serum inflammatory factors may contribute to the impaired IVF outcomes observed in women with aPS/PT. Additionally, as one of the antibodies to phospholipid-binding plasma protein complexes, the aPS/PT complex inhibits the binding and activation of prothrombin to phospholipids and is associated with an increased risk of thrombosis. However, the mechanism by which antithrombin antibodies may be involved in the pathogenesis of pregnancy has not yet been elucidated. It is hypothesized that these antibodies may lead to pregnancy loss by promoting the formation of blood clots in placental microvessels. The placental trophoblast is a major component of the outer lobe of the cell membrane, which is highly exposed to phosphatidylserine. Previous studies have shown that during embryonic and placental differentiation, lipid asymmetry is disrupted, resulting in the exposure of phosphatidylserine on the outer surface (Blank and Shoenfeld, 2010). Increased apoptosis events of giant cells in the fetal disc exposed to phosphatidylserine may be associated with placental dysplasia, leading to adverse pregnancy outcomes (Matalon et al., 2004). The binding of aPT to endothelial cells and the subsequent activation of complement may lead to placental microvascular thrombosis, resulting in obstetric complications of APS (Oku et al., 2012; Peterson et al., 2016).

Previous studies have demonstrated that LDA and low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) can be effective in treating obstetric complications associated with antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), while hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) and statins also provide benefits for APS patients (Pleguezuelo et al., 2021; Tektonidou et al., 2019). However, for non-standard APS, there is currently no universal accepted treatment recommendation. In our study, the administration of LDA and hydroxychloroquine sulfate prior to IVF cycles has been shown to enhance IVF outcomes and improve embryo quality. As previously mentioned, patients experienced increased numbers of oocytes and embryos following treatment, along with improved rates of perfect/ viable embryos, MII oocyte rates, blastocyst formation rates, and normal fertilization rates.

These improvements may be attributed to the modulation of immune inflammatory status. Further research is necessary to validate the efficacy of these medications.

Nevertheless, this study had several limitations. Firstly, the sample size enrolled in the study is relatively small, which necessitates careful interpretation of the data. Moreover, given the retrospective design of this study, causal inferences cannot be established, and the potential for selection bias should not be overlooked. Therefore, further multicenter, prospective studies are needed in the future to validate our findings.

# 5. Conclusion

In conclusion, our study found that aPS/PT antibodies are associated with adverse IVF outcomes, including a lower number of retrieved oocytes, embryos, and available embryos, as well as reduced rates of perfect and available embryos, blastocyst formation, clinical pregnancy and implantation. An unbalanced immunological and inflammatory status, characterized by altered Th17/Treg ratios and elevated serum inflammatory cytokines, may contribute to the impaired IVF outcomes observed in infertile women with aPS/PT. Therefore, once aPS/PT is detected, it is essential to consider optimal treatment for women undergoing IVF procedures.
