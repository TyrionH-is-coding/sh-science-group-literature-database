# ABSTRACT

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is characterized by thromboembolic phenomena and recurrent fetal loss associated with elevated circulating anti-phospholipid/beta2glycoprotein-I(β2GPI)-binding-antibodies(Abs). Individual APS patients harbor diverse clusters of circulating anti-β2GPI Abs, targeting different epitopes on the β2GPI molecule. Our novel approach was to construct a peptide composed of β2GPI-ECs-binding-site (phospholipids-membrane), named "EMBI". EMBI exert dual activities: a) At first EMBI prevented β2GPI ECs binding, thus reduced by 89% the binding of β2GPI/anti-β2GPI to the cells in comparison with 9.3% inhibition by EMBI scrambled form (scEMBI). b) Longer exposure of ECs to EMBI resulted in intracellular EMBI penetration which did not prevent β2GPI/anti-β2GPI binding to HUVEC. Surprisingly, β2GPI/anti-β2GPI did not activate ECs harboring EMBI, illustrated by prevention of E-selectin and tissue factor (TF) expression. The inhibition of TF mRNA transcription was illustrated by quantitative RT-PCR. EMBI decreased the expression of phosphorylated JNK1/2, p38, HSP27 and enhanced phosphorylation of glycogen synthase kinase-3β (pGSK3β). Knocking down the GSK3β expression by siRNA-GSK3β, reduced the TF expression by β2GPI/anti-β2GPI-exposed-HUVEC. In-vivo, EMBI significantly decreased the percentage of fetal loss in naive mice infused with anti-β2GPI Abs, $p < 0.04$ . Thus, the dual activity of EMBI may introduce EMBI as a potential novel candidate peptide, to treat patients with APS.

# 1. Introduction

The antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) ("Hughes Syndrome"), is characterized by the presence of anti-phospholipid antibodies (Abs) that bind negatively charged phospholipids, platelets, and endothelial cells mainly via beta-2-glycoprotein-I ( $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ ) molecule [1-4]. Nowadays, it is well established that anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs exert a direct pathogenic effect by interfering with homeostatic reactions occurring on the surface of monocytes, platelets, or vascular endothelial

cells [5-18]. Passive transfer of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI Abs into naive mice resulted in fetal loss, thrombosis, or cognitive dysfunction [5-10]. The pathogenic activity of the anti $\uparrow - \beta 2$ GPI molecule can be reduced by oral tolerance with $\beta 2$ GPI [11]. In-vitro studies revealed that anti $\beta_{2}$ GPI activates endothelial cells via induction of NFkB expression followed by adhesion molecule upregulation including E-selectin, ICAM-I, and VCAM-I [6,7,12-14]. This process was followed by elevated expression of the adaptor molecule myeloid differentiation protein (MyD88) and the p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway [15,16].

Tissue factor (TF), a 263-residue membrane-bound glycoprotein (47kd), is a key regulator for initiation and propagation of thrombus formation. Experimental data showed that TF expression can be induced in endothelial cells, monocytes, and platelets by anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs [17-21]. The upregulation of TF expression may lead to a procoagulation state exhibited by enhanced platelet aggregation and

<!-- page 2 -->
thrombosis. Moreover, patients with APS have elevated levels of TF [21,22]. The p38 MAPK pathway is a member of the protein kinase family, which are key regulators of cellular signaling that controls inflammatory response, cell differentiation, and cell growth. It can be activated by anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ thus mediating TF upregulation [23].

During the last decade, we [8] and other groups [24-28] attempted to neutralize the pathogenic anti- $\beta 2$ GPI Abs by synthetic peptides directed to diverse epitopes on the $\beta 2$ GPI molecule, targeting domain I [11,25,26], domain II/III [8], domain IV [8], and domain V [25-28]. Plasma from patients with APS encompass heterogeneous subpopulations of circulating anti- $\beta 2$ GPI Abs, targeting different epitopes on the $\beta 2$ GPI molecule [8,26].

In order to neutralize a wide spectrum of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs, we employed a new approach suggesting a $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -phospholipid linked to a membrane binding sites revealed previously [29,30].

In the present study we report on a peptide named EMBI which has a dual activities, in addition to its ability to neutralize anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs, it penetrates into ECs and reduce anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs-induced-procoagulation state in ECs, expressed as inhibition of TF and E-selectin mediated via elevating the expression of phosphoglycogen-synthase-kinase 3 beta (pGSK3). Furthermore, administering EMBI to anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs induced experimental APS model in mice, significantly reduces the percentage of fetal loss.

# 2. Materials and methods

# 2.1. Endothelial cells

Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC) primary cultures were purchased from Lonza (Lonza, Walkersville, MD, USA). The cells were maintained in a commercial EBM-2 endothelial cell basal medium (Lonza), supplemented with VEGF. The cells were used during passages 1-4. Before the experiments, the cells were starved for 8 hrs in EBM-2 with no additional growth factors but with $0.5\%$ FCS.

# 2.2. Synthetic peptides used in the study

EMBI: GDKVSFFCKNKEKKCKKK-Ahx-KSSLAFWKTDASDVKP. Linker: Ahx: H2N(CH2)5CO2H (Sigma) based on ref 29.

scEMBI: scrambled form of EMBI: SFGKWDPKDFKVDETAK-Ahx-KSFKKCASKLKNWKC; GDK: GDKVSFFCKNKEKKCKKK; scG DK: GKVFCANAAECFADAASA; KS: KSSLAFWKTDASDVKP; scKS: SLKSWFKADPVKSATS; Octamer EMBI and scEMBI on Fmoc backbone were used in the APS experimental mice.

β2GPI-FITC conjugation and all the synthetic peptides with and without FITC were synthesized by BioSight Ltd, Karmiel, Israel.

# 2.3. Crystallographic structure of EMBI

The crystal structure of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ is available in the Protein Data bank (PDB entry 1c1z) [30]. The Accelrys molecular modeling package (Accelrys Inc., San Diego, CA) was used for visualization and analysis of the molecule.

# 2.4. Antibodies used in the study

# 2.4.1. Anti- $\beta 2GPI$ Abs

Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs were affinity purified from sera of 7 APS patients with high titers of circulating anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs confirmed by ELISA. Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs were affinity purified on a $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -column as previously described by us [6-8].

# 2.4.2. Human IgG

Commercial human IgG (Jackson ImmunoResearch Laboratories, Inc., West Grove, PA, USA) was used as a negative control for endothelial cell activation and was defined as cIgG.

# 2.5. Immunohistochemistry

# 2.5.1. Direct binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ FITC to endothelial cells

Endothelial cells (HUVEC) were grown on slides and exposed for $1\mathrm{h}$ or $4\mathrm{h}$ to EMBI and scEMBI in growth media. Following extensive washings. $\beta 2$ GPI-FITC was added to the slides for $1\mathrm{h}$ incubation at $37^{\circ}C.$ Washed slides were fixed with $4 \%$ paraformaldehyde (Electron Microscopy Sciences, Hatfield, PA, USA) in PBS for $20\mathrm{min}$ and FITC binding was visualized by a fluorescent microscope.

# 2.5.2. Penetrationof EMBI into HUVEC

The cells were grown on cover slips and exposed to EMBI-FITC or scEMBI-FITC $10~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ in growth medium for different periods of time. After the incubation the cells were washed 3 times for $5\mathrm{min}$ with PBS, fixated with $4 \%$ paraformaldehyde (Electron Microscopy Sciences 15710) in PBS for $20\mathrm{min}$ , then washed 3 times for $5\mathrm{min}$ with PBS. Actin was probed with $100\mathrm{nM}$ Rhodamine Phalloidin (Cytoskeleton, Denver, CO, USA, cat # PHDR1) for $1\mathrm{h}$ in room temperature. After 3 additional $5\mathrm{min}$ PBS washes the cover slips with the stained cells were mounted with DAPI-Fluoromount- $\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{TM}}$ (SouthernBiotech 0100-200). Immunofluorescence analysis was carried out using a Leica TCS SP5 confocal laser scanning microscope at magnification $\times 63$ .

# 2.6. Inhibition of binding of $\beta 2GPI$ /anti- $\beta 2GPI$ Abs to HUVEC, treated with EMBI, GDK, and KS synthetic peptides

Cyto-ELISA was performed as previously described [8]. Endothelial cells were grown in 96-well tissue culture plates (NUNC) until confluence was achieved. The cells were incubated with the studied peptides: EMBI, KS, GDK $(50\mu \mathrm{g / ml}1\mathrm{h})$ or their scrambled form. $\beta 2GPI$ $(30~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ for $1\textrm{h})$ and anti- $\beta 2GPI$ Abs were added at $50\%$ binding for $2\mathrm{h}$ . Data were read at OD $405\mathrm{nm}$ using an ANTHOS-HT2 ELISA reader (LABEQUIP LTD. Ontario, Canada). Each experiment was done in triplicate/plate and repeated 3 times. The percentage of inhibition was calculated as follows: $\%$ inhibition $=$ [OD anti- $\beta 2GPI - OD$ anti- $\beta 2GPI$ peptide treatment]/OD anti- $\beta 2GPI\times 100$

# 2.7. Detection of HUVEC E-selectin expression by cytoELISA

E-selectin detection on endothelial cells, was performed by cyto-ELISA as previously described [7,8]. HUVEC were incubated with EMBI or scEMBI $1 - 3\mathrm{h}$ prior to administering $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ $(10~\mu \mathrm{g}/$ ml), washed and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs $(10~\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ is added to the cultures for $2\mathrm{h}$ . Then, HUVEC were treated with PBS containing $0.2\%$ Triton X-100 to permeabilize the cell membrane. Subsequently, the cells were blocked with $3\%$ BSA, then, biotinylated mouse anti-human E-selectin (Jackson ImmunoResearch) $1\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ was added for $1\textrm{h}$ . The cells were then exposed to streptavidin alkaline phosphatase and an appropriate substrate. Data were read employing ELISA reader at OD $405~\mathrm{nm}:\%$ inhibition $=$ [OD anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}-\mathrm{OD}$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -peptide treatment]/OD anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}\times 100$

# 2.9. Flow cytometric analysis of HUVEC membrane TF expression

# 2.10. RNA isolation and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

Total mRNA was isolated using the Qiagen RNA isolation kit according to the manufacturer's instructions. RNA was then reverse transcribed into cDNA by MMLV reverse transcriptase (Promega). Five micrograms of total RNA was transcribed to cDNA in a $30 - \mu 1$ reaction volume. For transcript quantification by real-time PCR, the SYBR Green Mix containing Thermo-Start DNA Polymerase (InVitroGene, CA, USA) was used according to the manufacturer's instructions. cDNA samples were amplified using specific primers. Amplification was monitored by real-time PCR analysis using the ABI/Prism 7700 Sequence Detector System (Applied Biosystems).

# 2.11. Genetic knockdown of glycogen synthase kinase beta (GSK3β)

HUVEC cells were transfected using a reverse transfection protocol. Briefly, the cells were seeded at $1 \times 10^{5}$ cells per well in 6-well plates, then placed in a $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ incubator at $37^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ for $1 \mathrm{~h}$ prior to transfection with either silencer negative control siRNA or GSK3 $\beta$ siRNA from Santa Cruz, by using Santa Cruz transfection reagent according to the manufacturer's protocol. After $72 \mathrm{~h}$ , the cells were washed, starved, and treated with EMBI and scEMBI as described above. $120 \mathrm{nM}$ of siRNA was required to achieve $>80 \%$ knockdown of GSK3 $\beta$ . For Western blot analyses, the cells were lysed in electrophoresis sample buffer. Immunoblotting was performed as described above.

# 2.12. Treatment of mice with experimental APS with EMBI peptide

Fetal loss was induced in mice by passive transfer of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs as previously described by us [6,8]. BALB/c female mice (10-12 weeks old) and male mice (12-14 weeks old) were purchased from HARLAN-Israel and were mated. After the vaginal plugs were confirmed, the mice were infused with anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ $50~\mu \mathrm{g}$ in $200~\mu \mathrm{l}$ per mouse) intravenously on day 4 of pregnancy, in order to induce fetal loss [6,8]. Following the infusion of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , the mice were treated intravenously with EMBI or scEMBI. On day 16 of the pregnancy the mice were analyzed for fetal resorptions (equivalent to fetal loss).

# 2.13. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed by unpaired Student's $t$ -test. Results were expressed as mean $\pm$ SD. $P < 0.05$ was considered significant.

# 3. Results

# 3.1. Dual activities of EMBI: inhibits anti- $\beta 2GPI$ binding to HUVEC and penetrates into the cells

$\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ synthetic derivatives, based on endothelial-cell- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding sites [29,30], were tested for their ability to inhibit the binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ /anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to endothelial cells. The $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ derivatives used in the study were based on the phospholipid binding site (GDK), the membrane binding site (KS) and the both peptides were combined with a linker which is named EMBI, as depicted in Fig. 1a. The figure describes the peptides' location on the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule. The two segments are mostly exposed in the folded protein and include 7 Lysine residues, which are highly preferred in antigenic sites. HUVEC were preincubated for $1\textrm{h}$ with the synthetic peptides GDK, KS, EMBI, and the scrambled forms of the relevant peptides were used as controls, to study their ability to prevent the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to the cells. The peptides were subjected to the cells for one hr prior to addition of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ affinity purified Abs from 7 APS patients or human control cIgG. Fig. 1b shows that the peptide GDK decreased the binding by anti $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ by $43.5\%$ whereas KS peptide inhibited it by $18.7\%$ $(p <   0.05)$ . EMBI prevented anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to endothelial cells by $89\%$ and the scEMBI reduced it by $9.3\%$ $(p <   0.001)$ . These data show that EMBI is twice more efficient than the peptide GDK (phospholipid binding site) and by 5 folds than KS peptide (membrane binding site) in inhibiting anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to endothelial cells.

The EMBI peptide inhibited the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to HUVEC in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1c). Exposure of HUVEC to the EMBI for one hour inhibited $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to the membrane as probed with anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs, whereas a longer exposure of EMBI to HUVEC enhanced the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha \mathrm{i} - \beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to the membrane as exemplified by an inhibition assay. At EMBI concentration of $25~\mu \mathrm{g}/$ ml and $1\textrm{h}$ of incubation time, $92.4\% \pm 3.2$ inhibition of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha \mathrm{i}-$ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to the cells was noticed $(p <   0.01)$ , in comparison to control IgG. HUVEC exposure to EMBI for $2\textrm{h}$ result in 3 fold lower $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha \mathrm{i} - \beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -membrane inhibition of binding $(27.5\% \pm 3.8\%$ $p <   0.02)$ while 3 h EMBI-HUVEC incubation time did not illustrate any significant inhibition of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha \mathrm{i} - \beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -membrane binding $(P > 0.05)$ in comparison to preincubation with scEMBI followed by $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha \mathrm{i} - \beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to HUVEC $(6.3\% \pm 1.7$ $8.1\% \pm 1.2$ respectively), Fig. 1c. These results point to a complete $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha \mathrm{i} - \beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs binding to the HUVEC membrane upon $3\textrm{h}$ preincubation of HUVEC with EMBI, which is opposite to $1\textrm{h}$ preincubation with EMBI.

# 3.3. EMBI inhibits HUVEC $E$ -selectin expression induced by anti- $\beta 2$ GPI Abs

Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs bind HUVEC via $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs activate HUVEC leading to overexpression of E-selectin and TF among other molecules. EMBI (1 h prior incubation with ECs), reduced the binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs to HUVEC by prevention of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}-$ HUVEC binding (Fig 1c,d). Preincubation of HUVEC with scEMBI did not affect $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to ECs. Longer incubation time of HUVEC with EMBI $(2-3\mathrm{h})$ , resulted in a penetration of EMBI peptide to the ECs on one hand and permitted a full binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to the membrane on the other hand (Fig. 1d,e).

Data presented in Fig. 3a demonstrate that HUVEC preincubated for $1\mathrm{h}$ with EMBI at $25~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ significantly downregulated E-selectin expression by $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \mathrm{anti - }\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ activated HUVEC $(p < 0.001)$ , 7 up to 9.6 times more than did the scEMBI. The anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ were originated from 5 separate APS patients. No significant suppression was observed upon treatment of HUVEC with scEMBI at the same concentration $(p > 0.05)$ . # 4. Discussion

In the present study we constructed a peptide based on the fifth domain of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ molecule sequence that bears the phospholipid combined with membrane binding sites, termed EMBI, which is depicted in Fig. 1a. The novel EMBI peptide exert dual activities: a) the EMBI peptide is capable of reducing the binding of affinity purified anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs to HUVEC; b) EMBI penetrates into HUVEC and downregulate activation of HUVEC by $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . The inhibition was exemplified by decreased E-selectin expression and inhibition of TF expression by HUVEC, through negative regulation of GSK3 $\beta$ phosphorylation.

The novel EMBI peptide inhibition of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -mediated endothelial cells activation was associated with enhanced pGSK3 expression. Knocking down the HUVEC GSK3 gene expression by siRNA significantly reduced TF production by HUVEC. The EMBI mediated downregulation of TF expression was exemplified by TF

Active GSK3 is essential for pro-inflammatory responses following TLR stimulation [31-34]. The involvement of TLR2 and TLR4 in the procoagulation ECs state induced by anti- $\beta 2$ GPI Abs was proposed by different groups [18,35,36].

Taken together, these results provide a novel synthetic peptide. Furthermore, the data highlight for the first time the importance of the pGSK3 $\beta$ pathway in TF regulation in HUVEC, induced by anti-phospholipid Abs.

In the past, immunization of naïve mice with a synthetic peptide derived from CMV (TIFI), which share a mimotope on the β2GPI phospholipid binding site and induced pathogenic anti-phospholipid Abs that bind and activate endothelial cells, enhanced thrombus formation, and caused fetal death in pregnant mice [37,38]. Alternatively, TIFI significantly decreased thrombus size in mice injected with total IgG having phospholipid activity, derived from APS patients, in a mouse ex vivo system [39].

In our hands, the novel peptide had dual effect on endothelial cells activation: EMBI was more efficient than the peptide GDK, based on a $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -phospholipid or membrane binding sites separately, in preventing anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs binding to HUVEC as depicted in Fig. 1a. Furthermore, EMBI penetrate the cells and showed its ability to downmodulate ECs activation through intracellular signaling.

Previous studies showed that accelerated expression of TF by HUVEC exposed to IgG Abs purified from APS patients with phospholipid activity involve phosphorylation of p38 MAPK and activation of NFkB [40,41]. p38 is one of the central molecules in the intracellular signaling cascade that positively regulates various inflammatory cellular responses, which include TNF $\alpha$ , IL-1, IL-6, and IL-8. Our results support the role of p38 MAPK phosphorylation in the anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-mediated HUVEC activation, manifested by enhanced TF expression and transcription. The EMBI peptide caused dephosphorylation of p38, as well as downstream dephosphorylation of HSP27, as illustrated in Fig. 4a.

We unravel the essential role of GSK3 in TF expression induced by anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs. Our findings indicate for the first time that our novel EMBI peptide provokes phosphorylation of GSK3 and alters GSK3 expression. The knockdown of GSK3 in HUVEC by GSK3 siRNA decreased the expression of TF by anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -activated endothelial cells (Fig. 5c). GSK3 is a serine-threonine protein kinase key molecule that is constitutively active in resting cells but can be inactivated through phosphorylation by other protein kinases including Akt, which is induced by PI3K activation [42-44]. Previous studies have indicated that GSK3 is involved in diverse physiological pathways ranging from metabolism, cell cycle, gene expression, development and oncogenesis to neuroprotection and is required for NFkB activation in various tissues [42-45]. Accordingly, GSK3 is being studied as a therapeutic target for inflammation, stroke, heart failure, and Alzheimer disease [42-46]. The kinase activity of GSK3 is negatively regulated by phosphorylation and is associated with decreased GSK3 activity [45].

Endothelial cell transformation to a thrombotic and inflammatory phenotype plays a major role in the pathophysiology of APS via overexpression of TF and adhesion molecules, induced by anti $\beta 2$ GPI Abs [11-14]. Endothelial cells express both GSK3 $\alpha$ and GSK3 $\beta$ . Eto M et al. [47] indicated that thrombin as well as TNF $\alpha$ induces transient dephosphorylation and hence activation of GSK3. Inhibition of GSK3 by LiCl or synthetic inhibitor suppressed thrombin-induced TF and VCAM-1 expression and NFkB activation [47]. Consistent with these results, we show the essential role of GSK3 in inhibition of TF production by anti- $\beta$ GPI-activated HUVEC. Knockdown of GSK3 $\beta$ in the endothelial cells or inhibiting by LiCl resulted in suppression of TF upregulation by anti- $\beta 2$ GPI Abs.

It is of interest to understand how the GSK3 expression by anti $\beta 2$ GPI Abs activated ECs cross-talk with the Toll-like receptors (TLR). TLR2 proposed by P Youinou et al. [35] and TLR4 raised by PL Meroni et al. and [15,36]. TLRs are a family of type-I transmembrane receptors that have been shown to play a fundamental role in the recognition and regulation of the host inflammatory response against microbial products [35,48]. GSK3 is involved in TLR signaling and regulates the production of proinflammatory cytokines and septic shock [33]. A microbe-derived molecule such as LPS may bind to the TLR and result in recruitment of the adaptor protein MyD88, which in turn activates other molecules such as the IL-1 receptor-associated kinase (IRAK) family that lead to NFkB activation and the stress-activated kinases JNK and p38 MAPKs. The latter impel to transcriptional regulation of proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF, IL-1, and IL-12 [33]. A very similar signaling cascade was described by Meroni PL et al. [15] for anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-mediated endothelial cell activation, suggesting the involvement of TLRs. Delta TRAF6 and Delta MyD88 significantly abrogated antibody-induced as well as IL-1- or LPS-induced NF-kappa B activation. Furthermore, anti- $\beta 2$ GPI and LPS similar time kinetics of IRAK phosphorylation, raising the TLR4 family involvement in endothelial cell activation [49]. TLRs also induce phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K). The PI3K/Akt pathway is active during the early phase of TLR signaling, whereas GSK3 is inactivated through phosphorylation by other protein kinases including Akt, which is induced by PI3K activation. EMBI, which inactivates GSK3β through its phosphorylation, may inhibit the TLR signaling cascade as well.

Interestingly, platelet function is negatively regulated by GSK3 via GSK3 phosphorylation, which is correlated with inhibition of activity, in a PI3K-dependent manner [49]. DS Woulfe et al. [50] suggest that $\mathrm{GSK3}\beta$ acts as a negative regulator of platelet function in vitro and in vivo. Inhibition or reduction of GSK3 expression increases platelet aggregation and fibrinogen binding in vitro. The effect of $\mathrm{GSK3}\beta$ on platelets function in vivo was evaluated using 2 models of thrombosis in mice. In the first, $80\%$ of $\mathrm{GSK3}\beta^{+ / - }$ mice formed stable occlusive thrombi after ferric chloride carotid artery injury, whereas the majority of wild-type mice formed no thrombi.

The present study clearly demonstrates the efficacy of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ derived peptide, EMBI, in attenuating TF expression and transcription via inhibition of GSK3 activity and GSK3 phosphorylation. Moreover, in vivo EMBI injection into APS mice, significantly reduced the percentage of fetal loss mediated by anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ Abs.

# 5. Conclusion

We constructed a novel peptide named "EMBI", composed of phospholipids and membrane binding sites, with dual activity: a) it inhibits $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding to endothelial cells in a short preincubation time and in a longer incubation it permitted $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -ECs binding; b) it penetrates into endothelial cells and prevents ECs activation induced by $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \alpha$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , via negative regulation of pGSK3 expression. Treatment of APS mice with EMBI injections significantly reduced fetal loss. Therefore, the dual activity of EMBI may introduce EMBI as a potential novel candidate peptide, to treat patients with APS.
