# OxLDL/β2GPI Complexes and Autoantibodies in Patients with Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, Systemic Sclerosis, and Antiphospholipid Syndrome

Pathogenic Implications for Vascular Involvement

LUIS R. LOPEZ, $^{a}$ DANIEL F. SIMPSON, $^{a}$ BETH L. HURLEY, $^{a}$ AND Eiji MATSUURA $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Corgenix Inc., Westminster, Colorado 80234, USA

<!-- page 6 -->
<sup>b</sup>Okayama University Graduate School of Medicine and Dentistry Okayama 700-8558, Japan

<!-- page 4 -->
ABSTRACT: Oxidized low-density lipoprotein (oxLDL) interacts with $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , forming oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes. Autoimmune vascular inflammation (and oxidative stress) may promote the formation of these complexes. The coexistence of oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes with autoantibodies to these complexes suggests an active pro-atherogenic role in vascular thrombosis and atherosclerosis. Immunoglobulin G (IgG) anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies have been regarded as pro-atherogenic, whereas IgM antibodies are thought to be anti-atherogenic. For this study, oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes, IgG, and IgM anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Measurements were taken in two patient groups: (1) those with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), systemic sclerosis (SSc), and rheumatoid arthritis (RA); and (2) those with primary and secondary antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). For oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes, SLE and SSc patients had the highest mean optical densities (ODs) $(P < .001)$ , followed by RA $(P = .139)$ and healthy controls. IgG anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibody distribution followed the same pattern observed with oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes, SLE and SSc $(P < .001)$ , RA $(P = .08)$ , and controls. IgM antibodies showed a reverse pattern, with the highest mean OD in RA $(P < .001)$ , followed by SSc $(P = .007)$ and SLE $(P = .143)$ . Both IgG and IgM anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies were significantly higher in secondary APS patients compared with SLE controls without APS. In addition, the highest mean OD and prevalence of IgG anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies were observed in APS patients with a history of arterial thrombosis. These results may reflect the widespread vascular involvement seen in SLE and SSc, in contrast to the relatively low vascular involvement in RA. In SLE and SSc, high serum levels and prevalence of circulating oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes and IgG anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies indicate significant vascular oxidative stress as well as a possible pathogenic role in autoimmune-mediated atherosclerosis.

# KEYWORDS: autoimmunity; antiphospholipid syndrome; atherosclerosis; oxidized low-density lipoprotein; $\beta 2$ -glycoprotein I; systemic lupus erythematosus; systemic sclerosis; rheumatoid arthritis

# INTRODUCTION

The accelerated (or premature) occurrence of atherosclerosis in patients with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is associated with significant cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Because atherosclerosis in SLE could not be fully explained by traditional risk factors for cardiovascular disease or by therapeutic intervention, the participation of disease-specific factors such as autoantibodies has been proposed. The presence of oxidized low-density lipoprotein (oxLDL) and beta-2 glycoprotein-I ( $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ ), along with immunoreactive cells in human atherosclerotic plaques, has suggested that autoimmune mechanisms play a direct role in the development of foam cells and atherosclerotic lesions.

Lipid peroxidation (oxidative stress) modifies LDL into oxidized forms. This process is thought to play a central role in the initiation and progression of atherosclerosis. OxLDL triggers the production of pro-inflammatory and chemotactic factors, favoring the accumulation of immunoreactive cells (macrophages and lymphocytes) in the arterial wall. Autoimmune vascular inflammation may alter antioxidant regulatory mechanisms, thereby contributing to oxidative stress and oxLDL formation.

OxLDL binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ via LDL-derived ligands (oxLig-1) to form stable (or non-dissociable) and possibly pathogenic oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes in vitro. $^{7,8}$ These complexes have been detected in patients with systemic autoimmune diseases that have vascular complications (i.e., SLE and antiphospholipid syndrome [APS]) and have been implicated as atherogenic autoantigens. $^{9}$ Immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies to oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes have also been demonstrated in SLE patients with secondary APS and have shown a strong association with arterial thrombosis. $^{10,11}$ We now report on the presence of oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes and IgG and IgM antibodies to these complexes in patients with systemic sclerosis (SSc; also known as scleroderma), another systemic autoimmune disease characterized by generalized vascular complications.

# MATERIAL AND METHODS

# Subjects

Serum samples were obtained from two patient populations. One consisted of 224 consecutive patients: 86 with SLE, 59 with SSc, and 79 with rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Serum from 100 healthy blood donors served as controls. A second patient population included in the study consisted of 74 selected SLE patients, 29 with a history of APS and 45 without one. These patients were used to study the relation between oxLDL/β2GPI antibodies and APS.

An additional 93 selected APS patients were used to study the relation between oxLDL/β2GPI antibodies and clinical manifestations of APS. Twenty of these patients had primary APS and 73 had secondary APS. Forty-two had a history of an

<!-- page 3 -->
arterial thrombotic event, 37 of venous thrombosis, and 14 of pregnancy morbidity. A diagnosis of APS was established according to the Sapporo criteria.[12]

# ELISA for oxLDL/β2GPI Complexes

Murine IgG monoclonal antibodies against human $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ (WB-CAL-1) were coated onto Nunc MaxiSorp 96-MicroWell Plates (Nunc, Denmark). WB-CAL-1 monoclonal antibodies used in this assay bind only oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes, but not free $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . Diluted serum samples in diluent containing $10~\mathrm{mM}$ $\mathrm{MgCl}_2$ were added to the coated microwells and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature. $\mathrm{MgCl}_2$ dissociates intermediate or electrostatically bound oxLDL/ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes, enabling the specific detection of nondissociable or stable covalently bound complexes, if the latter are present in serum samples.

Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-human Apo B100 murine monoclonal antibody (2E10) was added to the microwells and incubated for an additional hour. Color was developed with tetramethylbenzidine $(\mathrm{TMB}) / \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ and the reaction was stopped with $0.36\mathrm{N}$ sulfuric acid. Optical density (OD) was read at a wavelength of $450\mathrm{nm}$ (650-nm reference). The reactivity of the assay was assessed with a curve built with oxLDL/β2GPI complex solution. The complexes were prepared in advance by incubating equal amounts of $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ -oxLDL and purified human $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . A normal cutoff value for the assay was established by testing serum samples from healthy blood donors (mean + 3 standard deviations).

# ELISA for IgG and IgM Anti-oxLDL/β2GPI Antibodies

OxLDL/β2GPI complexes, prepared by incubating equal amounts of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ -oxLDL and purified human β2GPI, were coated onto Immunlon 1B 96-microwell plates (Dynex Technologies Inc., Chantilly, Virginia). These oxLDL/β2GPI complexes served as antigenic substrates to capture patients' antibodies. Diluted serum samples were added to the microwells and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature. HRP-conjugated anti-human IgG (or IgM) antibody was added to the microwells and incubated for 1 hour. Color was developed with $\mathrm{TMB / H_2O_2}$ and the reaction was stopped with $0.36\mathrm{N}$ sulfuric acid. OD was read at a wavelength of $450~\mathrm{nm}$ ( $650~\mathrm{nm}$ reference). The reactivity of the assay was evaluated with a curve built with serial dilutions of selected positive sera for IgG or IgM anti-oxLDL/β2GPI antibodies. A normal cutoff value for the assay was established by testing serum samples from healthy blood donors (mean + 3 standard deviations).

# IgG and IgM Anti-oxLDL/β2GPI Antibodies in Consecutive Autoimmune Patients

For IgG anti-oxLDL/β2GPI antibodies, SLE patients had the highest mean OD $(0.235 \pm 0.226)$ , followed by RA patients $(0.185 \pm 0.210)$ , SSc patients $(0.177 \pm 0.061)$ , and healthy controls $(0.135 \pm 0.034)$ (FIG. 2). Both SLE and SSc patients' mean ODs were significantly higher than those of controls $(P < .001)$ . Thirty percent of SLE patients, $20\%$ of SSc patients, $18\%$ of RA patients, and $1\%$ of the controls reacted above the cutoff level of the assay $(\mathrm{OD} = 0.241)$ .

For IgM anti-oxLDL/β2GPI antibodies, the distribution pattern was altered. RA patients had the highest mean OD $(0.516 \pm 0.399)$ , followed by SSc patients $(0.279 \pm 0.255)$ , SLE patients $(0.240 \pm 0.224)$ , and healthy controls $(0.178 \pm 0.129)$ . The mean IgM antibody levels for RA patients $(P < .001)$ and SSc patients $(P = .007)$ were significantly different from those of healthy controls, whereas the mean IgM for SLE was not $(P = .143)$ .

The possibility that the assay was detecting IgM rheumatoid factor (RF) in patients with RA was investigated. An IgM RF reference preparation (Centers for Disease Control, Atlanta, Georgia) did not react (OD $< 0.197$ ) when tested on the assay at 200, 100, and $50~\mathrm{U / mL}$ dilutions, and IgM anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2$ GPI antibody levels did not correlate with the RF titers ( $r = .046$ ) in this group of RA patients.

# DISCUSSION

These results demonstrate significantly higher serum levels of oxLDL/β2GPI complexes in a population of consecutive patients with SLE and SSc compared with RA patients and healthy controls. We have previously shown that high serum levels of oxLDL/β2GPI complexes are frequent in SLE patients with vascular complications and proposed that these complexes play a pathogenic role in autoimmune-mediated thrombosis and atherosclerosis.[8,9] This is the first report describing oxLDL/β2GPI complexes in patients with SSc. Unlike RA, both SLE and SSc are characterized by generalized vascular complications.

Increased lipid peroxidation (oxidative stress), assessed by urinary isoprostane- $\mathrm{F}_{2\alpha}$ production and decreased antioxidant activity of the enzyme paraoxonase (PON), has been reported in patients with SLE and APS. $^{13-15}$ Augmented susceptibility to $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ LDL oxidation and increased isoprostane- $\mathrm{F}_{2\alpha}$ production in SSc patients has also been reported. $^{16,17}$ Furthermore, in vivo markers of oxidation, measured in various systemic autoimmune diseases, have shown that SLE patients have the highest degree of oxidation, followed by SSc patients, RA patients, and healthy controls. $^{18}$ However, in the present study, the degree of oxidation in RA patients was similar to that found in healthy controls. If serum levels of oxLDL/ $\beta 2$ GPI complexes are an indication of oxidative stress, our results correspond to the degree of autoimmune in vivo oxidation. $^{18}$ Widespread, chronic autoimmune vascular inflammation, together with decreased PON activity, may contribute to oxidative stress, LDL modification (oxLDL), and oxLDL/ $\beta 2$ GPI complex formation. However, the exact mechanism(s) for vascular inflammation that triggers oxidative stress in patients with SSc remains to be identified.

OxLDL/ $\beta$ 2GPI complexes may indeed function as atherogenic antigens.[8,9] In SLE and SSc patients, IgG anti-oxLDL/ $\beta$ 2GPI antibodies were significantly higher than those in RA patients and healthy controls. These antibodies were also significantly higher among SLE patients with secondary APS, showing a predictive value for APS of $90\%$ , whereas IgG aCL antibodies had a predictive value of only $71.4\%$ . APS patients with arterial and venous thrombosis also had higher IgG antibody levels than did either patients with a history of pregnancy morbidity or SLE controls without APS. The predictive value of IgG anti-oxLDL/ $\beta$ 2GPI antibodies for arterial thrombosis was $89\%$ , better than the $77\%$ value for venous thrombosis.

<!-- page 9 -->
The distribution of IgM anti-oxLDL/ $\beta 2$ GPI antibodies was different (opposite) from that of IgG antibodies, with higher levels in RA patients and lower levels in SLE patients. In patients with RA, the nature of the antigen that induces IgM antibody production in the absence of oxLDL/ $\beta 2$ GPI complexes is puzzling. One possible explanation for this phenomenon is that IgM antibodies cross-react with oxLDL/ $\beta 2$ GPI complexes. Another possibility is that natural antibodies (IgM isotype) may be anti-atherogenic,[19] so their absence (or decreased levels) may indirectly facilitate the development of atherosclerosis. Consistent with these findings, a negative association between IgM anti-oxLDL antibodies and atherosclerosis has been reported in healthy individuals.[20] Although some APS patients had both IgG and IgM antibodies, it is likely that the pro-atherogenic IgG overcomes IgM's protective effect.

Atherosclerotic plaque formation in autoimmune diseases requires the uptake by macrophages of immune complexes (e.g., oxLDL/β2GPI/IgG). In this study, the in vitro macrophage uptake of oxLDL/β2GPI complexes increased in the presence of anti-β2GPI antibodies, implying the participation of Fcγ receptors.[21] We also used enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) to test for IgG- and IgM-containing oxLDL/β2GPI immune complexes and found similar results to those of IgG and IgM anti-oxLDL/β2GPI antibodies.

It is possible that other autoantibodies further enhance platelet and endothelial activation. These antibodies would be produced by unidentified antigenic complexes created during chronic oxidative stress. Along with them, a range of metabolic and inflammatory abnormalities would result in severe autoimmune-mediated atherothrombotic disease.

Our results suggest that oxLDL/β2GPI complexes, and autoantibodies to these complexes, not only play a pathogenic role, but also may be used as serologic markers to assess a patient's risk for vascular complications. In addition, these results provide a rationale for developing vascular disease prevention programs and/or more targeted therapeutic interventions.
