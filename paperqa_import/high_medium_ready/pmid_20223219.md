# Mode of Interaction between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and Lipoprotein Receptors Suggests Mutually Exclusive Binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to the Receptors and Anionic Phospholipids

Chang-Jin Lee, $^{1}$ Alfredo De Biasio, $^{1}$ and Natalia Beglova $^{1,*}$

$^{1}$ Department of Medicine, Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center and Harvard Medical School, Boston, MA 02215, USA

*Correspondence: nbeglova@bidmc.harvard.edu

DOI 10.1016/j.str.2009.12.013

# SUMMARY

Lipoprotein receptors of the LDLR family serve as clearance receptors for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and as signaling receptors for the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ antibody complexes in antiphospholipid syndrome. We compared four ligandbinding LA modules from LDLR and ApoER2 for their ability to bind domain V of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ (2GPI-DV).We found that the LA modules capable of binding $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ interact with the same region on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - }$ DV using residues at their calcium-coordination site. The structure of a complex between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ and LA4 of LDLR, solved by molecular docking guided by NMR-derived restraints and extensively validated, represents the general mode of interaction between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and lipoprotein receptors. We have shown that $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ cannot simultaneously bind to lipoprotein receptors and anionic phospholipids, suggesting that the association of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} / \text{anti - } \beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibody complexes with anionic phospholipids will interfere with lipoprotein receptors' signaling in APS.

# INTRODUCTION

Beta2-glycoprotein I ( $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ ) is an abundant plasma protein with an established function in the pathology of antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). APS is an autoimmune disease characterized by recurrent thrombosis and pregnancy complications, and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ is a major antigen for autoimmune antibodies in patients with APS (Giannakopoulos et al., 2009; Urbanus et al., 2008a). Precise molecular mechanisms leading to thrombotic complications in APS are not well understood and, as a consequence, current options for treatment and prevention of thrombosis are limited (Lim et al., 2006).

The presence of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI antibodies is accompanied by cellular activation both in vitro and in vivo (Cugno et al., 2009; Koike et al., 2007; Pierangeli et al., 2004). A number of cell-surface molecules, including annexin A2 and Toll-like receptors (TLR) on endothelial cells and monocytes, and ApoER2 and GPI $\mathbf{b}\alpha$ on platelets, are suggested to play a role in APS (Cockrell et al., 2008; Lutters et al., 2003; Pennings et al., 2007b; Raschi et al., 2008; Shi et al., 2006; Sorice et al., 2007; Urbanus et al., 2008b; Zhang and McCrae, 2005). The importance of annexin

A2 and TLR-4 for the mediation of thrombogenic effects of APS-related antibodies was demonstrated in vivo in a mouse thrombosis model (Pierangeli et al., 2007; Romay-Penabad et al., 2009). Annexin A2, which is not a transmembrane receptor, binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ with high affinity even in the absence of antibodies (Ma et al., 2000). Bridging of annexin A2 by either anti-annexin A2 antibodies or by $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ antibody complexes induces expression of adhesion molecules on endothelial cells (Zhang and McCrae, 2005). It was suggested that annexin A2 and TLR-4 may act in concert on endothelial cells (Cockrell et al., 2008; Zhang and McCrae, 2005). On platelets, ApoER2 and GPIbα bind a recombinant chimeric dimer of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ that mimics the dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ antibody complex, and synergize in platelet activation (Pennings et al., 2007b; Urbanus et al., 2008b).

ApoER2 is a member of the low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) family. Closely related members of the family, including LDLR, VLDLR, ApoER2, LRP and megalin, are present in all 79 human tissues analyzed by gene expression array (Su et al., 2004). These receptors not only mediate the cellular uptake of lipids and nutrients from the circulation and function as scavenger receptors, but are also involved in fundamental signaling processes (Herz et al., 2009; May et al., 2005; Stolt and Bock, 2006). Lipoprotein receptors were implicated in the clearance of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ bound to apoptotic cells, and it was demonstrated that ApoER2 contribute to platelet activation by the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ antibody complexes (Maiti et al., 2008; Pennings et al., 2007a, 2007b; Urbanus et al., 2008b). Biochemical studies have shown that all lipoprotein receptors can bind dimerized $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and these interactions are inhibited by RAP, which is a universal inhibitor of ligand binding by lipoprotein receptors (Pennings et al., 2006). Other lipoprotein receptors besides ApoER2 are expressed on endothelial cells and monocytes and might contribute to the activation of these cells by $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes, thus playing a part in the pathology of APS (Korschineck et al., 2001; May et al., 2005; Moestrup et al., 1992; Yang et al., 2009).

Lipoprotein receptors interact with their ligands by LA modules of about 40 residues each, which are arranged in sequence to form a ligand-binding domain (Blacklow, 2007). The LA modules have little sequence homology, but adopt a very similar three-dimensional structure. The structure of an LA module is maintained by three disulfide bonds and a bound calcium ion. Lipoprotein receptors interact with domain V of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ ( $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}-\mathrm{DV}$ ) (van Lummel et al., 2005). All five domains of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ have similar fold and few interdomain contacts (Bouma et al., 1999; Hammel et al., 2002; Schwarzenbacher et al., 1999). In these studies, we uncovered the characteristic features of the LA modules capable of binding $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and determined the structural details of the binding interface in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/\mathrm{LA}$ complex by comparing different LA modules for their ability to bind $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . We used four different ligand-binding LA modules, LA3, LA4, and LA6 from LDLR, and LA1 from ApoER2, as examples to investigate how LA modules interact with $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . LDLR is a primary internalization receptor for LDL. ApoER2, which has high level of expression in the brain and testis, is critical both for neuronal migration and for selenium uptake through endocytosis of selenoprotein P (Masiulis et al., 2009). The first LA module in ApoER2, ApoER2-A1, was shown to contribute the most to the binding of dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ (Pennings et al., 2007a). Both LA3 and LA4 bind RAP (Fisher et al., 2006), which also inhibits the interaction between dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and lipoprotein receptors (Pennings et al., 2006).

Using solution nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, we have shown that the LA modules capable of binding $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ interact with the same site on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ by residues at their calcium-coordination region. We solved the structure of a complex between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ and LA4 by molecular docking guided by NMR-derived restraints. In the structure, extensively validated by binding studies and site-directed mutagenesis, the contact site for LA modules on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ is formed by 3 lysine residues: K308, K317, and K282. The structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ complex represents how $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ interacts with LA modules and establishes the characteristic features that distinguish LA modules capable of binding $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . We have shown that $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ cannot simultaneously bind to lipoprotein receptors and anionic phospholipids, suggesting that the association of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI / }$ antibody complexes with anionic phospholipids will interfere with lipoprotein receptors' signaling in APS.

# RESULTS

# Stoichiometry of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ Complex

We determined the stoichiometry of binding between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}-$ DV and LA4 by measuring R1 and R2 relaxation rates for the backbone amides of $^{15}\mathrm{N}$ -labeled $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} - \mathrm{DV}$ and $^{15}\mathrm{N}$ -labeled LA4 in isolation and in the presence of unlabeled interacting domains at saturating concentrations. The measured relaxation

rates were used to calculate an overall rotational correlation time, $\tau c$ , for the labeled molecules (Table 1). The values of $\tau c$ for LA4 and $\beta 2GPI$ -DV in isolation are consistent with previously published data (Beglova et al., 2001; Hoshino et al., 2000). The effective molecular weight of the $\beta 2GPI$ -DV/LA4 complex estimated from the NMR relaxation measurements indicates that the complex has 1:1 stoichiometry (Table 1).

# Mapping the Binding Site for LA Modules onto $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$

To determine which residues of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ form the contact site for LA modules, we monitored changes in the spectra of the $^{15}\mathrm{N}$ -labeled $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ in the presence of different LA modules. The residues of the flexible C-terminal loop of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ from H310 to A320 are either weak or missing from the spectra and, therefore, excluded from the analysis. Comparison of the chemical shift perturbation spectra of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ revealed that the same subset of residues is perturbed by LA4, LA3, and ApoER2-A1 (Figures 1A-1E). In addition to these core residues that likely comprise the major contact site for LA modules on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , another group of residues is perturbed by LA4 and ApoER2-A1, suggesting that both LA4 and ApoER2-A1 form additional contacts with $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ that are not present in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA3}$ complex. Finally, several additional $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ residues are perturbed when ApoER2-A1 is used for titration. The perturbation of the residues I263, M271, and I301, which form a hydrophobic cluster in $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , suggests that the binding of ApoER2-A1 to $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ affects the backbone structure of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ distant from the contact site. The extent of perturbations caused by different LA modules suggests that ApoER2-A1 binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ with a higher affinity than LA4 or LA3, and that LA3 has the weakest affinity for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ .

# The Binding Affinities of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to LA Modules

# Measured by Fluorescence Polarization

Changes in the global mobility of the fluorescently labeled LA modules upon binding to $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ are reflected in an increase in the measured fluorescence polarization (Figure 2). Fitting of the binding curves resulted in the $K_{d}$ values of $1.14\pm 0.06~\mu \mathrm{M}$ and $46\pm 7~\mu \mathrm{M}$ for the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / ApoER2 - A1}$ and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ complexes, respectively. The binding of LA3 to $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ did not reach saturation even when $1\mathrm{mM}$ $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ was added to LA3 (data not shown), suggesting that $K_{d}$ for this complex is in a high micromolar range. The increase in viscosity of the protein solution at high protein concentration, which would lead to an overestimation of $K_{d}$ (Lefebvre, 1982), precluded us from measuring $K_{d}$ of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA3}$ complex by fluorescence polarization.

# Mapping the Binding Site for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ onto LA Modules

The $^{15}\mathrm{N}$ -labeled LA modules, LA4 and ApoER2-A1, were titrated with unlabeled $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to identify the residues perturbed by complex formation (Figures 3A-3E). The relatively small changes in the chemical shifts of the backbone amides of the LA modules in the presence of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ suggest that these modules bind $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ predominantly by the side chains. For both LA modules, the majority of perturbed residues are either directly involved in coordination of the calcium ion or are adjacent to the calcium-binding residues, suggesting that the contact site for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ on the LA modules overlaps with the calcium-binding site. # Structural Features

# of the $\beta 2$ GPI-DV/LA4 Complex

The intermolecular interface in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}-$ DV/LA4 complex (PDB ID 2KRI) is maintained by a number of hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic contacts (Table 3). As clearly seen from Table 3, the majority of intermolecular contacts are created by K308 and K317 of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}-\mathrm{DV}$ . These contacts are further augmented by the hydrogen bonds between K282 of

LA4 module, F132 and I140, and the corresponding I26 of ApoER2-A1 are also perturbed in the presence of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ . Because the structure of the LA modules is dependent on the bound calcium ion, the perturbation of the residues in the hydrophobic core of the LA modules supports our observation that the binding interface for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ on LA modules is centered at the calcium-binding site.

# Docking Model of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ Complex

Because the structures of LA4 and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ are available in the Protein Data Bank (PDB) database, we used these molecules for docking. We built a docking model of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ complex using the program HADDOCK2.0 and the interaction restraints derived from the NMR data. The clustering of the 200 refined structures resulted in only two clusters with more than 10 structures in each. The first cluster of 141 structures had an average HADDOCK score of $-97.7 \pm 13.5$ , and the second cluster of 26 structures had an average HADDOCK score of $-70.3 \pm 12.4$ . The intermolecular interface in the complex is relatively small and maintained primarily by electrostatic interactions between positively charged lysine residues of β2GPI-DV and negatively charged aspartates of LA4 grouped around the bound calcium ion (Table 3 and Figures 4B and 4C). A solvent-accessible surface area of β2GPI-DV buried in the interface of the β2GPI-DV/LA4 complex with a minimal HADDOCK score is $455\AA^2$ .

# LA Modules Inhibit Binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to Cardiolipin

A sequence of hydrophobic residues 313-316 (Mehdi et al., 2000) and a lysine-rich region from residue 281 to 288 (Hunt and Krilis, 1994; Sheng et al., 1996) are both important for

$\beta 2$ GPI-DV binding to anionic phospholipids. In the complex, LA4 wedges between these two regions. Therefore, we evaluated, if LA modules interfere with the binding of $\beta 2$ GPI-DV to cardiolipin. $\beta 2$ GPI-DV bound to cardiolipin was detected by a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated antibody directed to HA tag added at the N terminus of $\beta 2$ GPI-DV (HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV).

First, we compared the binding affinities for cardiolipin of a monomeric HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV and of HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV dimerized by anti-HA antibodies. A concentration of HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV at halfmaximum binding was $22\pm 4$ nM and $1.6\pm 0.3$ nM for monomeric HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV and for HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV preincubated with antibodies, respectively (Figure 6A). As expected, the binding affinity of domain V for cardiolipin is close to that of a full-length $\beta 2$ GPI (Hagihara et al., 1995) and the dimerization of HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV increased the apparent affinity of HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV for cardiolipin more than 10-fold (van Lummel et al., 2005). When the total concentration of HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV is $10~\mu \mathrm{M}$ and above, HA $\beta 2$ GPI-DV saturates all available antibodies and free HA $\beta 2$ GPI-DV competes with HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV/antibody complexes for binding sites on cardiolipin, resulting in diminished binding of the complexes to cardiolipin and lower measured OD (Figure 6A).

To investigate whether LA modules interfere with the binding of HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to cardiolipin, we used either LA4 or a pair of the modules, LA3-4. We have already shown above that each of these modules, LA3 and LA4, can bind $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ . A gradual decrease of bound HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ indicates that both LA4 and LA3-4 interfere with the binding of HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to cardiolipin. The shape of the inhibition curve strongly suggests that LA3-4 competes with cardiolipin for the same binding site on HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ . The concentration of LA3-4 that inhibits $50\%$ of binding of HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to cardiolipin was $106\pm 9~\mu \mathrm{M}$ (Figure 6B). The apparent Ki of LA3-4 for HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ in the presence of anti-HA antibodies was calculated using an affinity partitioning approach to estimate amount of HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ bound to LA3-4 (Olson et al., 1991). Assuming that the amount of HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ bound to cardiolipin is much smaller than the total amount of HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , the calculated apparent Ki of LA3-4 was $6~\mu \mathrm{M}$ .

This experiment provides additional validation for the structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ complex, confirms that LA3 and LA4 interacts with the same site on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , and shows that a pair of LA modules binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ dimerized by antibody significantly stronger than a single LA module binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ . It also demonstrates that the interactions of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ with lipoprotein receptors and negative phospholipids are mutually exclusive.

# DISCUSSION

ApoER2, a lipoprotein receptor expressed on platelets, functions as a signaling receptor for dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ (Lutters et al., 2003; Pennings et al., 2007a, 2007b; Urbanus et al., 2008b). Besides platelets, several members of the LDLR family are expressed on endothelial cells and monocytes, and might contribute to thrombosis in APS. We used solution NMR spectroscopy, molecular docking, and binding studies to investigate and compare the structural features of the complexes between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ and the ligand-binding LA modules from lipoprotein receptors.

# The Docking Model of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ Complex Is Supported by Independent Experimental Data

We solved the structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA}$ complex by molecular docking guided by the NMR-derived restraints. This is the preferred approach in the case of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA}$ complexes. Our NMR titration studies have shown that the binding interface in the complexes is relatively small and formed predominantly by the side chains, suggesting that it might not yield enough distance restraints for accurate structure determination by NMR. The relatively weak affinity of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ for the LA modules makes crystallization of the complex difficult. For the modeling studies, we selected LA4 because its structure is available in the PDB database. The carbohydrate chains of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ are not expected to interfere with the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA}$ complex and, therefore, are not taken into account for the modeling. $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$

has four N-linked glycosylation sites at the residues N143, N164, and N174 in domain III and N234 in domain IV. All four glycosylation sites are far from the binding interface for LA modules.

The structure of the complex is consistent with independent experimental observations. First, we confirmed the structure by site-directed mutagenesis. Based on the structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ complex we selected two types of mutations. One type of mutations disrupts the complex formation and the other creates additional hydrogen bonds improving the binding affinity of the complex. Mutation of the critical lysine residues of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , K308, K317, and K282, to aspartates completely abolished the binding of LA4 to the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ mutant. The N148/E mutation of LA4 resulted in a tighter complex with $K_{d}9.0 \pm 1.6~\mu \mathrm{M}$ compared with $46 \pm 7~\mu \mathrm{M}$ of LA4. Second, the

# Structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ Complex Represents the General Mode of Interaction between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and Lipoprotein Receptors

Our results show that not all LA modules can bind $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , and that those LA modules capable of binding $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ will interact with it in the same fashion as observed in the structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ complex.

We have demonstrated by solution NMR spectroscopy that LA3, LA4, and ApoER2-A1 bind to the same site on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$

and that the binding site for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ on these LA modules is centered on the residues involved in coordination of the calcium ion. The dissociation constants measured here for different LA modules are in the range from $1~\mu \mathrm{M}$ (for ApoER2-A1) to more than $100~\mu \mathrm{M}$ (for LA3). The difference in the binding affinity is due to the auxiliary contacts that augment the interactions of the residues at the core of the binding interface. These auxiliary contacts depend on the primary sequence of the LA modules. When we mutated N148 of LA4 to glutamate to resemble E34 at the corresponding position in ApoER2-A1, which binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ with $1~\mu \mathrm{M}$ affinity, we improved Kd of LA4 by a factor of five. The changes in the intrinsic fluorescence of W316 of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ upon titration with LA3 confirmed that W316 is close to the binding interface and remains solvent exposed in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA3}$ complex, similarly to what is observed in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA4}$ complex.

LA modules capable of binding $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ could be distinguished based on the primary sequence in the vicinity of the calcium-coordinating residues of the LA modules. # Effect of Avidity in Interaction between $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and LA Modules

We have shown that a single LA module binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ with only micromolar affinity. Compared with a single LA module, a pair of modules connected by a flexible linker was able to efficiently bind HA-tagged $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ dimerized by anti-HA antibodies and inhibit its attachment to cardiolipin. Although LA3 binds $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ with low affinity $(K_{d} > 100~\mu M)$ , its presence in the LA3-4 pair significantly improved the binding of the LA module-pair to dimerized $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ . Typically, lipoprotein receptors use several LA modules connected by flexible linkers to bind diverse ligands which, in turn, have binding sites for several LA modules, suggesting that a single module would be insufficient to confer high-affinity binding (Andersen et al., 2003; Fisher et al., 2004). # The Binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to Lipoprotein Receptors versus Anionic Phospholipids

The structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA}$ complex and the observation that the LA modules inhibit binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to cardiolipin indicate that $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ cannot simultaneously interact by its domain V with lipoprotein receptors and anionic phospholipids. When bound to $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , LA modules prevent two phospholipid-binding regions on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ (residues 282-287 and 313-316) from simultaneously reaching the cellular membrane. On the other hand, when $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ is bound to the cellular surface, the binding site for LA modules is not accessible even though LA does not bind residues that interact with phospholipids. We performed cardiolipin binding and inhibition studies at low ionic strength to match the buffer used for NMR titration studies and to minimize the amount of proteins used in the experiment. We do not expect that the presence of sodium ions at physiological concentration alters the structure of the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV / LA}$ complex. The LA module inserted between the two phospholipid-binding regions of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ in the complex will still interfere with the binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to anionic phospholipids, although the binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ to both LA and phospholipids will be attenuated by the increased ionic strength.

Normally, anionic phospholipids are present on the inner leaflet of the cellular membrane and become exposed on activated or apoptotic cells (Zwaal and Schroit, 1997). The content of phosphatidylserine on the outer membrane of nonactivated cells is about $7\%$ (Murphy et al., 1992). The measured binding affinity of $\beta 2GPI$ for phospholipid membrane composed of $10\% / 90\%$ PS/PC in the presence of $120~\mathrm{mM}$ NaCl and $3\mathrm{mM}$ $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ was $14~\mu \mathrm{M}$ for monomeric $\beta 2GPI$ and $0.4~\mu \mathrm{M}$ for $\beta 2GPI/$ antibody complexes (Willems et al., 1996). The increase of phosphatidylserine to $20\%$ resulted in a much better binding of $\beta 2GPI$

to phospholipids. The measured affinity of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ /antibody complexes improved to $75~\mathrm{nM}$ and $5\mathrm{nM}$ in the presence and absence of calcium ions (Willems et al., 1996). Similarly, the measured $K_{d}$ of the dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ for $20\% /80\%$ PS/PC in the absence of calcium was $18~\mathrm{nM}$ (van Lummel et al., 2005). Because lipoprotein receptors bind dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ with a low nanomolar affinity (Pennings et al., 2006), the interactions of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ /antibody complexes with lipoprotein receptors will not be efficient on activated cells exposing high levels of phosphatidylserine.

On platelets, the signaling by dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ involves two receptors, ApoER2 and GPIbα (Urbanus et al., 2008b). Both receptors act independently and neither receptor alone is sufficient to support platelet adhesion to fibronectin. The dimeric W316/Ser mutant of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ that cannot bind to anionic phospholipids does not support platelet adhesion either (Lutters et al., 2003; van Lummel et al., 2005). Because the W316/Ser mutant binds ApoER2 similarly to wild-type $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ (van Lummel et al., 2005), it is likely that the mutation perturbs the binding of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to GPIbα. Because the binding site for GPIbα on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ is in domain V (Pennings et al., 2007b), it is conceivable that the W316/Ser mutation interferes with the binding of dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to GPIbα, abolishing platelet activation.

Current knowledge indicates that APS-related antibodies only predispose to thrombosis. Despite the presence of persistent antibodies, APS patients do not continuously suffer from thrombosis, which is often triggered by infection or trauma (Sherer et al., 2007; Shoenfeld et al., 2008; Urbanus et al., 2008a). In in vivo experiments, thrombosis must be provoked by infection or vessel injury (Fischetti et al., 2005; Jankowski et al., 2003; Pierangeli et al., 1996). Adhesive surfaces are required for detectable platelet activation by dimeric $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ in vitro (Lutters et al., 2003; Urbanus et al., 2008b; van Lummel et al., 2005). Lipoprotein receptors are constitutively expressed in different combinations on cellular membranes of endothelial cells, monocytes, and platelets. Acting in parallel with other receptors, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ antibody complexes bound to lipoprotein receptors might continuously sensitize cells, making them vulnerable to other prothrombotic factors. On endothelial cells annexin A2 provides high-affinity binding sites for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}/$ antibody complexes (Ma et al., 2000; Zhang and McCrae, 2005). The binding site for annexin A2 on $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and whether lipoprotein receptors act alongside with annexin A2 on stimulation of endothelial cells are not yet known.

The role of lipoprotein receptors expressed on endothelial cells and monocytes in the pathology of APS and the signaling pathways initiated by lipoprotein receptors in response to $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ /antibody complexes are not well understood. Our structural and binding studies might be helpful in further research addressing the contribution of lipoprotein receptors to the pathology of APS.

# EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES

# Proteins

The fragments of the human LDLR (residues 86-122 [LA3], 126-165 [LA4], and 212-251 [LA6]) and mouse ApoER2 (ApoER2-A1 residues 12-47) were ex

pressed in $E.$ coli and purified from inclusion bodies as previously described (North and Blacklow, 2000). β2GPI-DV (residues 244-326) was subcloned into a pET15b vector (Novagen). The encoded protein has an N-terminal histidine tag followed by the sequence recognized by the tobacco etch virus (TEV) protease so that the His tag can be removed. β2GPI-DV expressed in $E.$ coli was recovered from inclusion bodies, cleaved with TEV, and refolded under conditions permitting disulfide exchange before final purification by reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) on a C18 column.

# NMR Spectroscopy

For NMR spectroscopy, the labeled protein domains were produced in M9 minimal media supplemented either with $^{13}\mathrm{C}_6$ -D-glucose and/or $^{15}\mathrm{N}$ ammonium chloride. All NMR experiments were conducted on a Varian Inova 500 MHz spectrometer equipped with four RF channels and a triple-resonance probe with pulsed-field gradients. Spectra were acquired at 298 K using BioPack (Varian Inc.) pulse sequences and processed with GIFA v4.3 software (Pons et al., 1996). Chemical shifts were referenced to 2,2-dimethyl-2-silapentane-5-sulfonate sodium salt (DSS) used as an internal reference. The buffer used for the NMR experiments contained $90\% \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} / 10\% \mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}$ , 10 mM bis-Tris with 10 mM $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ (pH 7.1). Backbone $^1\mathrm{H}$ , $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ , and $^{15}\mathrm{N}$ resonance assignments of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ , LA3, LA4, and ApoER2-A1 were carried out by analyzing HNCA, NHCOCA, HNCACB, and HNCOCACB spectra.

$^{15}\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{R}1$ and ${}^{15}\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{R}2$ relaxation rates were measured in a series of 7-11 experiments with increased relaxation time delays. To determine the relaxation rates, the experimentally measured resonance intensities were fitted to a monoexponential decay model using the xcrfit program (http://www.bionmr.ualberta.ca/bds/software/xcrfit). The overall rotational correlation time $\tau_{C}$ was calculated from the R2/R1 ratio using the r2r1(tm program.

# Fluorescence Polarization Measurements

Experiments were performed on an EnVision plate reader (Perkin-Elmer) using 480 nm excitation, 535 nm emission filters with 30 nm and 40 nm bandwidths, respectively, and a FP FITC dual optics module. The LA modules were labeled with Atto488 (Sigma) according to the manufacturer's protocol and the labeled modules were purified by reverse-phase HPLC. Fluorescence polarization measurements were carried out in 10 mM bis-Tris buffer with 10 mM $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ (pH 7.0). Experiments were performed in triplicates on a 384 well plate using $40~\mu \mathrm{l}$ of a reaction mixture per well containing either 80 nM LA4, 150 nM ApoER2-A1, or 130 nM of the LA4 N148/E mutant. GNUPLOT was used to fit the $K_{d}$ values to the experimental data via a one-site binding model.

# Measurements of the Intrinsic Tryptophan Fluorescence of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$

Fluorescence measurements were performed on a Spectramax GeminiXS (Molecular Devices) using a 96 well plate. Each sample contained $100~\mu \mathrm{M}$ of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ in $10~\mathrm{mM}$ bis-Tris buffer with $10~\mathrm{mM}$ $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ $(\mathsf{pH}7.2)$ and varied amounts of LA3. Samples were allowed to equilibrate for $25\mathrm{min}$ before spectra were acquired. The excitation wavelength was set to $280~\mathrm{nm}$ and the emission scan was read from $330~\mathrm{nm}$ to $400~\mathrm{nm}$ with $1\mathrm{nm}$ step. To account for the fluorescence of the C-terminal tyrosine of LA3, the measured fluorescence at each titration point was corrected for the fluorescence of the blank sample that contained the same amount of LA3 in the absence of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ . # Molecular Docking with HADDOCK

The coordinates of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ were extracted from the crystal structure of the full-length protein (PDB code 1c1z), and the structure of LA4 including a calcium ion was taken from the X-ray structure of the RAP/LA3-4 complex (PDB code 2fCW). The docking was performed with HADDOCK 2.0 using standard protocols (Dominguez et al., 2003). The active and passive residues were selected based on the NMR chemical shift perturbation and the solvent accessibility data. The relative solvent-accessible surface area of residues was calculated with NACCESS v2.1.1 (Hubbard and Thornton, 1993). Only residues with more than $50\%$ relative solvent accessibility of the main chain or the side chain were considered. The following solvent-accessible residues perturbed by complex formation in the NMR titration experiments were designated as active: K251, K286, K287, K305, K308, E309, W316, D322, K324, and C326 (for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ ), and L143, W144, and D149 (for LA4). The passive residues, defined as solvent-exposed neighbors of the active residues, were P248, K250, K284, D293, E302, S311, S312, L313, F315, and P325 (for $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ ), and Q142, N148, P150, and D151 (for LA4). A 6.0 Å cutoff of the pairwise backbone rmsd in the interface was used for clustering of 200 refined structures.

# Cardiolipin Binding Assay

We used 96 well plates precoated with cardiolipin available as part of the ImmunoWELL cardiolipin IgG test kit (GenBio). We constructed a recombinant $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ with HA epitope tag added to the N terminus of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ (HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ ). HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ bound to cardiolipin was detected by HRP-conjugated anti-HA-tag antibodies (Abcam ab1265) using $2 - 2^{\prime}$ -azino-di-[3-ethylbenzthiazoline] sulfonate (ABTS) chromogenic reagent by measuring OD at $405~\mathrm{nm}$ . All measurements were done in triplicate and corrected to blank before data fitting. The blank contained all components except for HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ . The assay buffer was $10~\mathrm{mM}$ bis-Tris with $10~\mathrm{mM}$ $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ $(\mathsf{pH}7.6)$ . Cardiolipin-coated wells were blocked for $1\mathrm{hr}$ with $0.5\%$ skim milk and $2\%$ bovine serum albumin in the assay buffer. The binding and inhibition data were fitted to one-site models using GNUPLOT.

For the binding studies, $50\mu \mathrm{l}$ HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV was applied to wells and incubated for $30\mathrm{min}$ . After washing, $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ anti-HA antibody was added to wells and incubated for $30\mathrm{min}$ before detection. In the second set of experiments, $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV was first incubated for $30\mathrm{min}$ with the anti-HA antibodies. Then, the mixture was applied to cardiolipin, incubated for $30\mathrm{min}$ , washed, and bound HA- $\beta 2$ GPI-DV was detected.

For the inhibition studies, $44\mathrm{nM}$ HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ was incubated with LA4 for $30\mathrm{min}$ . Then, $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ of the mixtures was applied to wells and incubated for an additional $30\mathrm{min}$ . After washing, $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ anti-HA antibody was added to wells and incubated for $30\mathrm{min}$ before detection. In the second set of experiments, $50~\mu \mathrm{l}3.5\mathrm{nM}$ of HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ was first incubated for $20\mathrm{min}$ with the anti-HA antibodies. Then, various concentrations of the LA3-4 module-pair were added to the mixture and incubated for an additional $40\mathrm{min}$ . The mixture was applied to cardiolipin, incubated for $30\mathrm{min}$ , washed, and bound HA- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - DV}$ was detected.
