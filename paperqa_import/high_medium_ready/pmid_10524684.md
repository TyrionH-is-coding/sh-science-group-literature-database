# AN IgG ANTIPROTHROMBIN ANTIBODY ENHANCES PROTHROMBIN BINDING TO DAMAGED ENDOTHELIAL CELLS AND SHORTENS PLASMA COAGULATION TIMES

YAN ZHAO, ROBERT RUMOLD, MIN ZHU, DAOBIN ZHOU, ALAA E. AHMED, DZUNG T. LE, BEVRA H. HAHN, VIRGIL L. WOODS, JR., and POJEN P. CHEN

Objective. To test the hypothesis that some lupus anticoagulants are antiprothrombin antibodies, and that such antibodies enhance prothrombin binding to endothelial cells (EC) and thus promote clotting on the cell surface.

Methods. We generated a monoclonal antiprothrombin antibody (designated IS6) from a patient with primary antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). The antibody was analyzed for its binding properties, lupus anticoagulant activity, and pathophysiologic activity, using an EC-based plasma coagulation assay.

Results. IS6 is the first patient-derived monoclonal IgG antiprothrombin antibody. It bound to prothrombin with low affinity, reacted with 3 phospholipids (cardiolipin, phosphatidylethanolamine, and phosphatidylserine), and showed lupus anticoagulant activity. Moreover, IS6 enhanced the binding of prothrombin to damaged EC and shortened the EC-based plasma coagulation times.

Conclusion. These findings suggest that IS6 may promote coagulation in areas of damaged EC in the host, and thus contribute to thrombosis in patients with APS.

Antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) have been associated with recurrent thrombosis, recurrent fetal loss, and thrombocytopenia. The combined presence of serum aPL and $\geq 1$ of these clinical symptoms is referred

to as antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) (1-4). Initially, aPL are detected as anticardiolipin antibodies (aCL) by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and/or as lupus anticoagulants (LAC) by in vitro phospholipid (PL)-dependent coagulation assays (5,6). To date, studies have shown that aPL represent a heterogeneous group of antibodies that recognize various PL, PL-binding plasma proteins, and/or PL-protein complexes; the involved plasma proteins include $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I ( $\beta_{2}$ GPI), prothrombin, protein C, and protein S (7-15). Among these aPL, antibodies against $\beta_{2}$ GPI and/or its complexes with cardiolipin (CL) probably account for most of the positive findings on tests for aCL in APS (16), while antiprothrombin and/or anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies are responsible for the majority of the LAC activity (12,13,17-19).

Recently, increasing attention has been paid to antiprothrombin antibodies and the role they may play in thrombosis in APS patients (20-22). Antiprothrombin antibodies are found in $\sim 60\%$ of APS patients when tested by ELISA using immobilized human prothrombin on activated polyvinyl chloride plates (20,21). However, no more than two-thirds of the IgG antiprothrombin antibodies detected by ELISA have LAC activity (21,22). Importantly, when Fleck et al (12) and Permpikul et al (18) affinity purified IgG antiprothrombin antibodies, they found that the purified antibodies bound to immobilized phosphatidylserine (PS) in the presence of $\mathrm{Ca^{+ + }}$ and prothrombin, which suggested that IgG antiprothrombin antibodies first bound to prothrombin and then came down onto PS as a passenger when prothrombin bound to PS. Essentially, antiprothrombin IgG cross-links prothrombin molecules and thus increases the valence of interactions between prothrombin and PS. Subsequently, those investigators showed that IgG purified from an LAC-positive plasma sample (designated LA IgG, from a patient with hy

<!-- page 2 -->
poprothrombinemia) enhanced the binding of prothrombin to human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC) (23). Moreover, they also showed that the same LA IgG increased thrombin generation on the surface of HUVEC (23). Unfortunately, the latter studies used LA IgG (which contain IgG against prothrombin and other antigens) instead of purified antiprothrombin IgG.

To test the hypothesis that antiprothrombin antibodies may concentrate prothrombin on cell surface PL and thus lead to a hypercoagulable state, we generated and characterized a monoclonal IgG antiprothrombin antibody (designated IS6) from an APS patient. We determined the antibody's LAC activity, measured its effect on binding of prothrombin to endothelial cells (EC), and studied its thrombogenic potential, using a recently developed HUVEC-based in vitro system (24).

# PATIENT AND METHODS

Patient. The patient has been described previously (25). Briefly, she is a 23-year-old woman with APS. At age 16, she had spontaneous deep venous thrombosis of the calf and was treated for 3 months with heparin/warfarin. At age 18, she developed right-sided hemichorea associated with basal ganglia infarcts visible on magnetic resonance imaging and was treated with prednisone $(10\mathrm{mg / day})$ and low-dose aspirin, which resulted in complete resolution of neurologic symptoms. At age 19, she delivered a full-term infant after an uncomplicated pregnancy. There have been no fetal losses. She does not meet the criteria for systemic lupus erythematosus and has no features of that disease except borderline positivity for antinuclear antibodies (1:40) and mild proteinuria (26).

Since age 18, the patient has had continuously high titers of IgG aCL (mean of $\sim 700$ IgG phospholipid [GPL] units [1 GPL unit is equivalent to $1\mu \mathrm{g}$ of affinity-purified standard IgG aCL reference sample]) (27). The presence of LAC was established by 1) a prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time not corrected by a 1:1 mix with normal plasma, 2) low activity levels of intrinsic factors IX, XI, and XII, with levels increasing when assayed at higher dilutions, and 3) a prolonged dilute Russell's viper venom time (dRVVT) (LA-Screen) and a positive DRVVT-Confirm test result (both tests from Gradipore, North Ryde, Australia). No Factor V Leiden mutation was found. The patient has been treated with full-dosage warfarin ( $\sim 10$ mg/day), which she has continued to take to date. Her medications at the time of blood donation included warfarin, nifedipine (30 mg/day), and atenolol (50 mg/day).

Generation and preparation of the IS6 monoclonal IgG antiprothrombin antibody. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells from the patient were transformed with Epstein-Barr virus and cultured in 96-well plates. Supernatants were screened for antiprothrombin antibodies by ELISA as previously described, with slight modifications (20). Briefly, high-binding ELISA plates (Costar, Cambridge, MA) were coated with $10~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ of human prothrombin (Enzyme Research Laboratories, South Bend, IN) in Tris buffered saline (TBS; $0.05M$ Tris HCl, $0.15M$ NaCl, pH

7.5). After incubating overnight at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , plates were blocked with TBS containing $0.3\%$ gelatin. Supernatants were distributed to wells in duplicate and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature. After washing with TBS, bound human IgG was detected with affinity-purified horseradish peroxidase-labeled goat anti-human IgG ( $\gamma$ chain-specific; Caltag Laboratories, Burlingame, CA). After an additional incubation for 1 hour at room temperature, $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ of the substrate tetramethylbenzidine/ $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ (Kirkegaard & Perry, Gaithersburg, MD) was added, and the reaction was terminated with $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ of $1M\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{PO}_4$ . Results were read at a wavelength of $450~\mathrm{nm}$ against a background of $650~\mathrm{nm}$ on a Thermomax plate reader (Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, CA).

Positive cells were subcloned to 1 cell/well and fused with the Oubain-resistant K6H6/B5 human-mouse heterohybridoma cell line (28). Positive hybridomas were subcloned twice at 1 cell/well, and the resultant monoclonal antibody was designated IS6. To ensure the monoclonality of IS6, its light chain isotypes and IgG subclasses were determined by ELISA using isotype- and subclass-specific reagents.

To purify antibodies, IS6 hybridomas were first switched to a serum-free culture medium. Culture supernatants were passed through a HiTrap Protein G column (Pharmacia, Piscataway, NJ), and the bound IgG was eluted with $0.1M$ glycine HCl $(\mathrm{pH}2.8)$ and dialyzed against phosphate buffered saline (PBS).

Immunologic characterization of IS6. Binding properties of IS6 were studied by ELISA. First, IS6 was analyzed for its reactivity with the following antigens: CL, phosphatidylethanolamine, PS, collagen, protein A, bovine thymus single-stranded DNA (ssDNA), keyhole limpet hemocyanin (KLH), and $\beta_{2}$ GPI. CL and PS were from Avanti (Alabaster, AL) and $\beta_{2}$ GPI was purified from human plasma as described previously (29,30), while the other antigens were from Sigma (St. Louis, MO). ELISA was performed in a manner similar to the antiprothrombin ELISA mentioned above, except for the following modifications. Plates were coated with CL or phosphatidylethanolamine $(50\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ in ethanol) or PS $(50~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ in a 4:1 [volume/volume] mixture of methanol/chloroform), air-dried, and then blocked with $0.3\%$ gelatin in PBS. For the remaining antigens, plates were coated with $10~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ of each antigen in PBS. Binding to gelatin alone was subtracted from binding to all test antigens.

Second, the affinities of IS6 for PS and prothrombin were determined according to the previously described competitive inhibition method (31). This was done in a manner similar to the regular ELISA, except that IS6 was premixed with the corresponding soluble antigen at the indicated serial concentrations.

LAC tests for IS6. LAC activity of IS6 was determined by a modified dRVVT test using the DRVV screening test and DVVconfirm confirmatory test (American Diagnostica, Greenwich, CT) on a Sysmex CA600 (Dade, Lahila, FL) according to the instructions of the manufacturer (18,32). Clotting time was monitored after mixing a 1:4 ratio of IS6:normal pooled plasma and adding either the DRVV screening reagent or the DVVconfirm reagent (which contains excess PL to neutralize the LAC effect). LAC activity was considered positive when 1) the clotting time of the test IgG sample was 5 seconds longer than the mean clotting time of 14 individual IgG preparations from normal controls (resulting in a cutoff value equivalent to the mean $+3$ SD clotting time of 14 normal IgG preparations), and 2) the clotting time ratio of the

<!-- page 3 -->
DRVV test: DVV confirm test was $>1.5$ , which was the mean of the clotting time ratios for the 14 normal IgG preparations.

Flow cytometry analysis of IS6 binding to EC. HUVEC were obtained from Cell Applications (San Diego, CA) and cultured in serum-free HUVEC culture media according to the manufacturer's protocol. The EAhy926 human endothelial hybridoma cell line was established by fusing HUVEC with the A5498-8 human lung carcinoma cell line (33), and was kindly provided by Dr. Mohamad Navab of the University of California, Los Angeles. On the day of the experiment, HUVEC cells (between third and fifth passages) or EAhy926 were washed with PBS containing $4\%$ fetal calf serum (FCS). After 1 wash, approximately $1 \times 10^{6}$ cells in 100 $\mu$ l of PBS/4% FCS were incubated simultaneously with 20 $\mu$ g/ml of IS6 (or $100~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ of the control human IgG) and phycoerythrin-conjugated anti-human $\lambda$ light chain antibodies (PharMingen, San Diego, CA). After a 2-hour incubation on ice, cells were washed twice with PBS/4% FCS and analyzed on a FACScan flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson, San Jose, CA). Damaged cells were differentiated from viable cells based on their shrunken/hypergranular morphology, using the forward scatter (FSC) and side scatter (SSC) parameters. FSC and SSC reflect the size and the granularity, respectively, of a cell.

Conjugation of fluorescein isothiocyanate to prothrombin. Prothrombin was labeled with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) isomer I (Sigma) by adding $80\mu \mathrm{l}$ of FITC $(5\mathrm{mg / ml}$ in anhydrous DMSO) to $2\mathrm{ml}$ of prothrombin $(2\mathrm{mg / ml})$ in FITC-labeling buffer $(0.05M$ boric acid, $0.2M$ NaCl, pH 9.2) and incubating at $25^{\circ}C$ for 2 hours in the dark. Unbound FITC was removed by dialysis against PBS for 72 hours at $4^{\circ}C$ with 2 buffer changes.

Flow cytometry analysis of prothrombin binding to EC. Prothrombin binding to EC was analyzed in a manner similar to the analysis of the binding of IS6 to EC. Briefly, approximately $1 \times 10^{6}$ washed cells in $100\mu l$ of PBS/4% FCS were incubated simultaneously with FITC-labeled prothrombin (final concentration $20~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ or $100~\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ and IS6 (or a control human monoclonal IgG3) (final concentration 20 $\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ . After a 2-hour incubation on ice, cells were washed twice with PBS/4% FCS to remove unbound prothrombin and analyzed.

HUVEC-based plasma coagulation study. The coagulation study was performed according to the procedure described by Rand et al (24), with minor modifications. Briefly, HUVEC cells at the third passage were seeded in 96-well culture plates (Costar) and allowed to grow to confluence. Cells were washed 3 times with HEPES buffer containing 5 mM $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ and were overlaid with $100\mu \mathrm{l}$ of the normal pooled plasma preincubated with IS6 $(40~\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ or the control human IgG $(200\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ at $37^{\circ}C$ for 2 hours. Coagulation was initiated by adding $11\mu \mathrm{l}$ of $70~\mathrm{mM}$ $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ and formation of fibrin was monitored kinetically as an increase in the optical density (OD) to 0.1 OD units at $405~\mathrm{nm}$ in a plate reader (Molecular Devices) (24).

Statistical analysis. Where appropriate, 4-parameter logistic regression analysis (34) and Student's $t$ -test were used.

# DISCUSSION

We have generated and characterized the first monoclonal IgG antiprothrombin antibody from an APS patient. Besides binding to prothrombin, IS6 reacts with CL, PS, and phosphatidylethanolamine, possesses LAC activity, enhances prothrombin binding to damaged EC, and, most important, shortens plasma coagulation times in the presence of EC. Given these features, IS6 probably plays a role in promoting and/or sustaining thrombosis in APS. The isolation of IS6 complements the current repertoire of patient-derived monoclonal aPL

The fact that IS6 bound to prothrombin and 3 unrelated PL raises the possibility that IS6 may represent a mixture of 2 monoclonal antibodies, 1 against prothrombin and 1 against PL. However, this is extremely unlikely, since the IS6 hybridomas had been subcloned 4 times at 1 cell/well during their generation. Moreover, the antibody consists of 1 single heavy chain subisotype $(\gamma 3)$ and 1 single light chain isotype $(\lambda)$ . To further demonstrate that it is 1 single antibody that binds to prothrombin and to 3 PL, the IS6 antiprothrombin antibodies were affinity purified on a prothrombin column. The purified IgG behaved identically to IS6 in binding to prothrombin and the PL (data not shown), indicating that IS6 is a monoclonal IgG that binds to prothrombin, PS, and phosphatidylethanolamine.

The cross-reactivity by IS6 to prothrombin and PL, however, may be explained by unappreciated trace contamination of our PL preparations with prothrombin, or vice versa. Nevertheless, in a separate study of serum aCL and antiprothrombin antibodies in 53 IgG preparations from APS patients, we did not see cross-reactivity of these 2 antibody activities when using the same commercial preparations of CL and prothrombin (unpublished data). This suggests that trace contamination of either prothrombin or PL is unlikely to explain the observed activity of IS6 with prothrombin and PL.

Fleck et al (12) and Permpikul et al (18) have shown that antiprothrombin antibodies can bind to immobilized PS in the presence of $\mathrm{Ca}^{+ + }$ and prothrombin, and Rao et al (23) have shown that an LAC IgG preparation can enhance the binding of prothrombin to HUVEC and increase thrombin generation on HUVEC. This suggests that antiprothrombin antibodies may concentrate prothrombin on cell surface PL and thus lead to a hypercoagulable state. Our current findings support the above hypothesis and provide an explanation for the LAC paradox. They indicate that a single, homogenous, and highly purified aPL (IS6) can behave as a LAC in the regular coagulation assays (such as dRVVT), and can paradoxically increase the rate and magnitude of thrombosis in EC-based coagulation assays.

In addition, our findings provide new insights into the mechanisms by which aPL like IS6 may promote thrombosis. First, IS6 enhances the binding of prothrombin primarily to damaged EC (Figure 5). It is known that high-titer aPL by themselves do not reliably predict the occurrence of thrombosis, suggesting that aPL alone are unlikely to be the triggering agents. Instead, aPL probably promote and sustain thrombosis

initiated by other, as-yet-unidentified, factors. In addition, studies have shown that cell death and damage can cause redistribution of anionic PS to the outer leaflet of cell membranes (37), and that apoptotic EC acquire procoagulant properties (38). Taken together, these findings suggest that IS6-like antiprothrombin antibodies may not initiate thrombosis in areas of normal EC, but may promote thrombus formation when EC are damaged by other triggering factors.

Considering that IS6 binds to PS (Figure 1), it is conceivable that IS6 may bind to other damaged cells due to the PS exposure (37). Accordingly, IS6 was examined for binding to the Jurkat human T cell line and to a normal human subject's B cells transformed by Epstein-Barr virus, cultured in the presence or absence of $10\%$ FCS. The preliminary studies showed that IS6 bound to damaged T and B cells, but not to viable cells (data not shown). These findings are interesting and may have important implications for IS6-like antiprothrombin antibodies in APS patients. Additional experimentation is warranted to carefully investigate this issue.

Second, IS6 shortens HUVEC-based plasma coagulation times (Figure 6). In light of the previous finding that LAC/antiprothrombin antibodies increased HUVEC-based thrombin generation (23), these findings suggest that IS6 may promote thrombosis by enhancing prothrombin binding to damaged EC and thus catalyzing prothrombin conversion to thrombin on EC.
