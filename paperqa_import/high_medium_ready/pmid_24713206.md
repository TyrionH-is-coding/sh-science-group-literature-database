# ABSTRACT

Introduction: Abnormal fatty acid oxidation (FAO) and lipid metabolism have been found related to preeclampsia (PE). Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) as a clinical risk factor for PE has also been reported with abnormal lipid metabolism. However, the role of FAO in PE accompanied with APS is unknown. We aimed to investigate long-chain FAO changes in a PE-like rodent model induced by beta 2-glycoprotein I ( $\beta$ 2GPI).

Methods: The PE-like model was established by injection of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ ( $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group) or normal saline (control group) into C57BL/6J mice which were sacrificed on day 14 or 18 of gestation. Serum levels of anti-cardiolipin antibodies (aCL), anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies (a $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ ) and serum lipids were assayed. Lipid deposition in the placenta and maternal liver was detected by lipid staining. Long-chain 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase (LCHAD) mRNA and protein expression in the placenta and maternal liver was analyzed.

Results: The $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group showed PE-like symptoms including hypertension, proteinuria and adverse pregnancy outcomes. Serum aCL, a2GPI, free fatty acid (FFA) and triglyceride (TG) levels in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group were significantly elevated compared with the corresponding control group $(P < 0.05)$ , while cholesterol showed no significant changes. Placenta and maternal liver fatty infiltration was found in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group. LCHAD mRNA and protein expression in the placenta and maternal liver in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group were significantly elevated compared with the corresponding control group $(P < 0.05)$ .

Conclusion: $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ can induce PE-like symptoms, elevated serum FFA and TG, and abnormal LCHAD expression in pregnant mice. Changes in long-chain FAO could be a factor linking PE and APS.

# 1. Introduction

Preeclampsia (PE) is a serious obstetric complication and a major cause of increased maternal and perinatal mortality [1]. Despite a great deal of basic research and clinical studies, the pathogenesis of PE remains unclear. It has been found that PE is associated with oxidative stress, abnormal lipid metabolism, endothelial cell activation and injury and many other factors.

Fatty acid changes have also been found in PE, and several studies have suggested that elevated total serum free fatty acid (FFA) levels might be a predisposing factor for PE [2,3]. In recent years, we and other researchers have revealed that fatty acid oxidation (FAO) disorders are associated with PE, especially early-onset PE and hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets (HELLP) syndrome. Bartha and colleagues reported that mRNA level of long-chain 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase (LCHAD) and FAO capacity were significantly reduced in placentas from women with PE [4]. Robinson et al. [5] found that in human umbilical vein endothelial cells cultured in vitro, plasma from PE patients or free fatty acids could lead to lipid droplet aggregation and decreased mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity. Previously we found LCHAD protein level in placental tissues of patients with early-onset severe PE was also decreased [6]. In animal experiments, we found significant pathological changes in the placentas of Nw-nitro-L-arginine-methyl ester (L-NAME) induced PE-like mice in the early and

<!-- page 2 -->
mid-gestational stages and LCHAD expression was significantly decreased [7,8].

Long-chain FAO disorders are associated with the occurrence of PE, but not all patients with PE have FAO disorders. Previously we found no significant change in LCHAD protein expression between late-onset PE and controls [6]. In another study, there were no triglyceride (TG) abnormalities in a reduced uterine perfusion pressure (RUPP)-established PE-like model [9]. Therefore, it is possible that different FAO disorders exist in different subtypes of PE.

Recent clinical studies have shown that basic maternal conditions and history of underlying diseases play an important role in the occurrence and development of PE. The underlying maternal disease, antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), is a clinical risk factor for PE. APS consists a group of clinical signs caused by antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) which mainly include lupus anticoagulant (LA), anti-cardiolipin antibodies (aCL) and anti-beta 2-glycoprotein I antibodies (aβ2GPI). Heilmann L and his colleagues [10] found elevated aCL levels in $20\%$ of patients with severe PE before 34 weeks' gestation. Furthermore, some studies have also shown that aPL are associated with the prevalence and severity of HELLP syndrome [11,12].

On the other hand, changes in lipid metabolism in APS patients have also been noticed. Significantly increased serum TG and decreased high-density lipoprotein (HDL) levels were reported in patients with primary APS, while no significant changes in total cholesterol (TC) or low-density lipoprotein (LDL) levels were found [13]. In addition to finding increased serum TG and decreased HDL levels in patients with primary APS, Ames and colleagues [14] found decreased TC and LDL levels. There is also another study showing greater serum LDL and TG levels in patients with primary APS than controls, but with no change in TC level [15]. These studies suggest the presence of a lipid metabolic disorder in APS patients.

Lipid metabolic disorder has been found present in both PE and APS, however little is known about the potential interaction among FAO, PE and APS, which requires further investigation. We hypothesize that a FAO disorder might occur in APS-associated PE. Thus, in this study we established an APS-associated PE-like animal model by subcutaneously injecting human $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ to analyze the changes in long-chain FAO. Although APS animal models are very mature [16], to our best knowledge it is the first time they are used in PE research.

# 2. Materials and methods

# 2.1. Establishment and identification of animal models

The animal experiment was approved by the Animal Care Committee and Medical Ethics Committee of Peking University and procedures were conducted in strict accordance with guidelines for Principles of Laboratory Animal Care promulgated by the NIH. Eight- to 10-week-old virgin female and 10- to 14-week-old male C57BL/6J mice purchased from the Department of Laboratory Animal Science, Peking University Health Science Center were housed under controlled conditions and fed with standard mouse chow with water available ad libitum. Mice were immunized by subcutaneous injection of $0.1\mathrm{ml}$ human $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ (25 $\mu$ g, Sigma, USA) ( $\beta$ 2GPI group, $n = 20$ ) or physiological saline (control group, $n = 20$ ) mixed with complete Freund's adjuvant into five injection sites in their back. During the second and third weeks, the two groups were subcutaneously injected with incomplete Freund's adjuvant-dissolved $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ [17] and physiological saline respectively. On the fourth week, mice were mated and inspected daily for vaginal plugs. The day when vaginal plugs were found was designated as day 1 (D1) of pregnancy.

From day 2 of pregnancy, a CODA non-invasive tail-cuff acquisition system (Kent Scientific Corporation, USA) was used to measure blood pressure every two days. The mice were placed in standard metabolism cages on day 13 or 17 of pregnancy and $24\mathrm{-h}$ urine was collected for testing. The detection of urinary protein concentration was performed with a protein assay kit (Bio-Rad Protein Assay Kit I, USA).

# 2.2. Sample collection

Pregnant mice in each group were randomized into D14 sub-group $(n = 10)$ and D18 sub-group $(n = 10)$ , and samples were collected on day 14 and day 18

respectively. Pregnant mice were anesthetized with $10\%$ chloral hydrate $(3\mathrm{ml} / \mathrm{kg})$ and blood samples, taken from retro-orbital plexus immediately, were centrifuged and the supernatant was frozen at $-80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . Cesarean section was performed, and the number of live births, absorption number, fetal and placental wet weight were recorded. Placental and maternal liver tissues were collected; some were fixed in formalin for hematoxylin-eosin staining, some were embedded with Optimal Cutting Temperature compound (OCT) for oil red O staining, and the remainder were frozen at $-80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for mRNA and protein detection.

# 2.3. Serological testing

Serum aCL and aβ2GPI concentrations were detected with a mouse ELISA kit (R & D, USA) and serum FFA, TC and TG levels were analyzed with a blood lipid chemical assay kit (Wako Chemicals, Japan).

# 2.4. Histopathology

Mouse placental and maternal liver tissues were embedded in paraffin and underwent routine hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining after being sliced into $5\mu \mathrm{m}$ sections. Frozen placental and maternal liver tissues were sliced into $10~{\mu\mathrm{m}}$ sections, stained with oil red O and finally sealed with glycerogelatin. The sections were observed and photographed under the optical microscope (Nikon, Japan).

# 2.5. Real-time quantitative PCR

TRlzoI reagent (Sigma, USA) was used to extract total RNA from placental and liver tissues. Total RNA, $1\mu \mathrm{g}$ , was reverse-transcribed to cDNA with the Revert Aid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo, USA). The real-time quantitative PCR reaction system involved SYBR Select Master Mix reagent (Invitrogen Life Technologies, USA) and PCR amplification was run on a 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Life Technology, USA). The primer synthesis was completed by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China) with the primer sequences for LCHAD, forward: $5^{\prime}$ TGCATTTGCCGCAGCTTTAC- $3^{\prime}$ , and reverse: $5^{\prime}$ -GTTGGCCCAGATTCGTTCA- $3^{\prime}$ ; and GAPDH (as an internal control) forward: $5^{\prime}$ -TGATGACATCAAGAAGGTGGTGAAG- $3^{\prime}$ , and reverse: $5^{\prime}$ -TCCTTGGAGGCCATGTAGGCCAT- $3^{\prime}$ . PCR conditions were $94^{\circ}C$ for $2\mathrm{min}$ ; $55 - 60^{\circ}C$ for $30~s$ , $72^{\circ}C$ for $1\mathrm{min}$ , 40 cycles.

# 2.6. Western blot detection

Protein was extracted from placental and maternal liver tissues with RIPA lysis buffer (cwbiotech, China). An equal amount of protein sample was used for electrophoresis in $10\%$ polyacrylamide gel and then transferred onto a $0.45\mu \mathrm{m}$ PVDF membrane (Millipore, USA) which was blocked with $5\%$ milk (BD, USA) at room temperature for $1\textrm{h}$ , then incubated with primary antibodies rabbit anti-mouse LCHAD (1:500, Abcam, UK) and rabbit anti-mouse $\beta$ -actin (1:1000, Cell Signaling, USA) at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ overnight. Membranes were washed with TBS-Tween $(0.1\%)$ . Then horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody was added (1:10,000, Thermo, USA) for incubation at room temperature for $1\textrm{h}$ . Membranes were washed again with TBS-Tween. KODAK gel logic 4000 MM PRO imaging system (Kodak, USA) was used for scanning and detecting the band intensity. The relative expression of the target protein to $\beta$ -actin was calculated.

# 2.7. Statistical analysis

Quantitative data were expressed as mean $\pm$ SD. Student $t$ -test followed by equal variance two-sample test was used for comparing 2 groups, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Student-Newman-Keuls or Games-Howell test was used for multiple groups. Repeated measures ANOVA were used for blood pressure analysis. Qualitative data were compared by chi-square test. $P < 0.05$ was considered statistically significant.

# 3. Results

# 3.1. Confirmation of PE-like model

Hypertension and proteinuria are the clinical diagnostic criteria for PE. Thus, for the confirmation of PE-like model we measured blood pressure and urinary protein levels in both the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and control groups. From day 2 of gestation, mean arterial pressure (MAP) was significantly higher in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group than control group $(P < 0.05)$ , with an increasing trend over gestational time (Fig. 1A). MAP on day 18 of pregnancy in the D18 $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group was significantly higher than MAP on day 14 of pregnancy in the D14 $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group $(P < 0.05)$ (Fig. 1A).

Urinary protein levels in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group were significantly higher than in the corresponding control $(P < 0.05)$ and urinary protein level was higher in the D18 $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group than the D14 $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group $(P < 0.05)$ (Fig. 1B).

# 3.2. Pregnancy outcome

PE patients are often accompanied by adverse pregnancy outcomes, so we compared pregnancy outcomes between groups. The live birth rate was significantly lower in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group than the corresponding control group, while the fetal absorption rate was significantly increased $(P < 0.05)$ (Table 1).

The fetal weight and placental weight were significantly lower in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group compared to the corresponding control group $(P < 0.05)$ (Table 1).

Women presenting with preeclampsia or APS often have lipid metabolic disorders, so we measured serum FFA, TG and TC in this model. FFA and TG concentrations were significantly higher in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group than the corresponding control groups $(P < 0.05)$

# 3.4. Placenta and liver histological changes and fat staining

Histological analysis indicated interstitial edema and cytoplasmic lipid droplets in placental tissues in $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ groups (Fig. 3A). In addition, maternal liver tissues exhibited liver cell edema and cytoplasmic vacuolization in $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ groups (Fig. 3B).

Fat staining showed almost no formation of lipid droplets in placental tissues of control groups, whereas a large amount of lipid droplets aggregated in placental tissues in $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ groups (Fig. 4A). Similar aggregation of lipid droplets was also found in maternal liver tissues (Fig. 4B).

In a prospective case-control study, no association of aPL and PE was found [19]. Another study demonstrated that even in women at risk with a history of PE, the detection of aPL was not helpful for the prognostic of PE [20]. Thus no consistent conclusion has been drawn, which may be due to different clinical detection methods and diagnostic criteria. But the key point is that the links and factors connecting PE and APS are unclear.

Despite several methods to induce PE-like symptoms in animal models, PE-like models associated with APS have not been reported. In this study, we observed that blood pressure and urinary protein levels in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group were both significantly increased compared with the corresponding control group, indicating that pregnant mice had PE-like symptoms in the APS models. Similarly, in some studies with human subjects, concurrent PE in some cases of APS during pregnancy have been reported and the incidence has been reported to be up to $50\%$ [21], but the mechanism remains unclear.

PE and APS patients are often accompanied by adverse pregnancy outcomes, including preterm delivery, fetal intrauterine growth restriction and recurrent miscarriage. We compared the fetal absorption rate, placental weight and fetal weight and found placental development abnormalities and fetal growth restriction in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group. Placental dysfunction is considered as an important cause of PE [22]. Due to the robust proliferation and differentiation of trophoblasts, the expression of negatively charged phospholipids on the cell surface is increased which results in more $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ binding and trophoblasts themselves can also synthesize $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . Therefore, the placenta has become a major target

organ for aPL [23]. Mulla et al. [24] found that aβ2GPI could restrict trophoblast migration by reducing the activity of interleukin-6 and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), thereby resulting in abnormal spiral artery remodeling and shallow implantation. These reasons may lead to abnormal placental development which further causes fetal growth restriction.

In recent years, lipid metabolic disorders have been found in APS patients, and several studies have shown that FAO and lipid metabolic disorders are related to PE. Whether fatty acid and lipid metabolic disorders are related with the occurrence of PE in APS patients is unclear.

A large number of studies have shown the existence of lipid metabolic disorders in patients with primary APS [13-15]. In this study, we found that the serum FFA and TG levels were higher in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group than the corresponding control group. We also found histopathological changes and lipid deposition in placental and liver tissues, which was similar to our previous findings and suggested the presence of lipid metabolic disorders in the $\beta 2\mathrm{GP}$ induced PE-like model. In the control group, TG concentration was greater in the D18 subgroup than D14 subgroup, indicating that during normal pregnancy TG concentration may increase over gestational time, while FFA level does not significantly change. This suggested that although the sources of serum FFA increased during normal pregnancy, FFA utilization in the liver also increased, which eventually enable serum FFA level to remain stable to protect the body against injury. However, TG and FFA concentrations were higher in the D14 $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ group than the corresponding control, indicating that FFA utilization disorders existed in PE-like mice early in the mid gestational stage and may be one of the early events in the occurrence and development of PE.

Concerns about the relationship between long-chain FAO and PE originate from finding that LCHAD defect is related to some maternal-fetal diseases and placental lesions [25,26]. During pregnancy with LCHAD defect, most pregnant women suffer from fatty liver of pregnancy or HELLP syndrome [27]. However, the incidence of LCHAD gene defects is low. In previous studies, we found decreased LCHAD expression and abnormal fatty acid metabolism intermediates in PE patients without LCHAD defect and in L-NAME-established PE-like animal models [6-8]. However, in this study, we found increased rather than decreased LCHAD mRNA and protein levels in placental and liver tissues in our $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ induced PE-like model, which suggests the existence of other regulatory pathways affecting LCHAD mRNA and protein expression in this model. In a recent study [28], LCHAD was found to play an important role in cardiolipin (CL) remodeling. CL, a constituent phospholipid of the mitochondrial membrane, plays an important role in respiratory chain and ATP production, and reduced CL synthesis may result in mitochondrial dysfunction. We speculate that increased aCL and a2GPI may bind to CL on mitochondrial membrane, thus affecting mitochondrial function. The body upregulates LCHAD expression in a compensatory manner to synthesize more CL, which leads to decreased role of LCHAD as a long-chain FAO enzyme. This situation results in further fatty acid $\beta$ -oxidation disorders and consequently increased serum FFA and TG levels. These intermediates of fatty acid metabolism accumulate in placental trophoblasts or endothelial cells to form intracellular lipid deposits, thus resulting in cellular dysfunction. High FFA levels can activate the cellular oxidative stress, leading to excessive production of reactive oxygen species. These free radicals could attack lipids, proteins, nucleic acids and phospholipids of cell membrane, react with polyunsaturated fatty acids to form lipid peroxides and cause endothelial dysfunction [29].

In conclusion, our study indicated the presence of PE-like symptoms including hypertension, proteinuria, placental and fetal development abnormalities in a $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -established model. These PE-like symptoms occurred early and lipid metabolism and long-chain FAO also exhibited abnormalities early in the mid stage of pregnancy. Therefore, we hypothesize that APS as a maternal underlying disease affects the placental and fetal development, and long-chain FAO disorders together with lipid peroxidation, oxidative stress and other factors may be involved in the occurrence and development of APS-associated PE in early stage of pregnancy. Changes in long-chain FAO may play an important role in the pathogenesis of APS-associated PE which needs further investigation.
