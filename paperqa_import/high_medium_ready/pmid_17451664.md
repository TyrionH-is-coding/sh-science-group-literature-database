# Induction of antiphospholipid antibodies and antiphospholipid syndrome-like autoimmunity in naive mice with antibody against human parvovirus B19 VP1 unique region protein

Bor-Show Tzang a, Yi-Ju Lee b, Tzi-Peng Yang c, Gregory J Tsay b,d, Jing-Yu Shi b, Chun-Chou Tsai b, Tsai-Ching Hsu b,*

$^{a}$ Institute of Biochemistry and Biotechnology, Chung Shan Medical University, Taichung, Taiwan, R.O.C.

<sup>b</sup> Institute of Immunology, Chung Shan Medical University, Taichung, Taiwan, R.O.C.

$^{c}$ School of Medical Laboratory and Biotechnology, Chung Shan Medical University, Taichung, Taiwan, R.O.C.

$^{\mathrm{d}}$ Department of Internal Medicine, Chung Shan Medical University Hospital, Taichung, Taiwan, R.O.C.

# Abstract

Background: Previous studies have postulated a connection between human parvovirus B19 (B19) infection and anti-phospholipid antibodies (APhL). B19 infection and anti-phospholipid syndrome (APS) exhibit congruent symptoms. Recently, phospholipase A2 (PLA2)-like activity has been linked to the VP1 unique region (VP1u) of B19. However, the precise role of B19-VP1u in pathogenesis of autoimmunity is still obscure. Methods: To elucidate the roles of VP1u in B19 infection and autoimmunity, the reactivity of B19-VP1u proteins with various autoantibodies were evaluated by ELISA and immunoblotting. Rabbits were immunized with purified recombinant B19-VP1u protein to generate anti-sera. Absorption experiments were conducted to determine the binding specificity of rabbit anti-sera against B19-VP1u, cardiolipin (CL) and $\beta$ -2-glycoprotein I ( $\beta$ 2GPI). Moreover, the effects of passive transfer of polyclonal rabbit anti-B19-VP1u IgG antibodies on platelets, activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), and autoantibodies were assessed.

Results: Autoantibodies against CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and phospholipid (PhL) in sera from patients with B19 infection, were cross-reactive with B19-VP1u. Consistently, sera from rabbits immunized with recombinant B19-VP1u protein displayed raised detectable immunoglobulins against B19-VP1u, CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and PhL. Additionally, the mice immunized with anti-B19-VP1u IgG developed thrombocytopenia, prolongation of aPTT, and autoantibody against $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and PhL.

Conclusions: These experimental results suggested the association between B19-VP1u and production of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI antibodies, APhL, and APSlike autoimmunity. Altogether, it may provide a clue in understanding the role of B19-VP1u in inducing autoantibodies and B19-associated APS manifestations.

# 1. Introduction

Human parvovirus B19 (B19) is the only parvovirus known to be a human pathogen consisting of a small non-enveloped

particle enclosing a single-stranded linear 5.6-kb DNA genome [1,2]. The icosahedral capsid consists of two structural proteins, VP1 (83 kDa) and VP2 (58 kDa), which are identical except for 227 amino acids at the amino-terminal end of the VP1-protein, the so-called VP1-unique region (VP1u) [3,4]. Over $95\%$ of capsid proteins are VP2, whereas VP1 accounts for less than $5\%$ . Although VP2 proteins predominate the capsid of B19, VP1 is critical to eliciting an appropriate immune response in both humans and animals [5-7]. A previous study indicated that

<!-- page 2 -->
antiserum produced by immunizing rabbits with a fusion protein containing the entire unique region sequence of VP1 neutralized the activity of B19 [8]. Additionally, most neutralizing epitopes in the VP1u of B19 are linear in contrast to those in VP2 that are likely conformational [9,10]. Moreover, phospholipase A2 $(\mathrm{PLA}_2)$ -like activity has been linked to the VP1u of B19. Recent studies indicated that VP1u mutant in the phospholipase domain loses its activity, proportionally, and viral infectivity [11-14].

B19 infection has been associated with the production of various autoantibodies, including anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody, anti-phospholipid antibody (APhL), and anti-cardiolipin autoantibody (aCL) [15-18] as well as various autoimmune diseases [19-22], especially systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). Notably, a remarkable similarity exists in the specificity of APhL between patients with B19 infection or SLE has been observed [15], and the anti-phospholipid syndrome (APS) has been hypothesized to have an infectious origin [22]. However, the relationship among B19 infection, APhL, and APS manifestation remains unclear. In this study, the binding activity of specific antibody with B19-VP1u is investigated. The relationship between B19-VP1u and autoantibody production was also examined by using immunized rabbits with purified recombinant B19-VP1u protein, and absorption experiments were performed to clarify the specificity of the raised antibodies in rabbits. Moreover, induction of passive APS by anti-B19-VP1u IgG antibodies was also evaluated.

# 2. Materials and methods

# 2.1. Preparation of recombinant human B19 VP1 unique protein

E. coli (BL21-DE3) clone containing VP1u cDNA in pET-32a expression vector (Novagene, Cambridge, MA) were grown overnight in one liter L-Broth containing $100\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ ampicillin at $37^{\circ}C$ with shaking [23]. When the OD 600 reached 0.7-0.9, protein expression was induced by addition of IPTG (USB Corporation, Cleveland OH) to a concentration of $1\mathrm{mmol / l}$ and incubated for another $3\mathrm{h}$ . The cells were harvested by centrifugation at $4000\times g$ for $20\mathrm{min}$ and resuspended in $20~\mathrm{ml}$ sonication buffer (50 mmol/l NaPO4 pH 8/0.25 mmol/1 EDTA). Lysozyme (Sigma, Saint Louis Mo, USA) was added to a final concentration of $1\mathrm{mg / ml}$ and kept on ice for $30\mathrm{min}$ . The cells were sonicated (W385, Heat systems-ultrasonic, INC) for a total of $30\mathrm{min}$ at $5\mathrm{min}$ intervals, centrifuged $10,000\times g$ for $30\mathrm{min}$ . The pellet was dissolved with $10\mathrm{ml}$ buffer B (8 mol/l urea; 0.1 mol/l NaH2PO4; 0.02 mol/l Tris-HCl; pH 8.0) for $1\mathrm{h}$ at room temperature, and centrifuge lysate at $10,000\times g$ for $30\mathrm{min}$ at room temperature to pellet the cellular debris. The supernatant was loaded onto a Ni-NTA spin column (Qiagen, Chatsworth, CA) and washed with buffer C (8 mol/l urea; 0.1 mol/1 NaH2PO4; 0.02 mol/l Tris-HCl; pH6.3). Protein was then eluted with buffer E (8 mol/l urea; 0.1 mol/l NaH2PO4; 0.02 mol/l Tris-HCl; pH4.5) and analyzed by SDS-PAGE for further use. Since the B19-VP1u cDNA is constructed into pET32a expression vector, the expressed recombinant proteins will contain S-tag and His-tag that represent the molecular weight of nearly $47\mathrm{kDa}$ . In addition, yield of purified recombinant B19-VP1u proteins are $25.67\mathrm{mg / l}$ . To avoid contaminative and degraded proteins, these recombinant proteins have been purified by Ni-NTA spin column (Qiagen, Chatsworth, CA) and spun through P50 and P30 Amicon (Millipore Billerica, MA). The purified recombinant B19-VP1u proteins are also analyzed by HPLC and the purity is nearly $98\%$ .

# 2.2.Rabbit antisera

For generation of antisera directed against the B19-VP1u, 4 female New Zealand White rabbits were immunized subcutaneous in the neck region with

$0.5\mathrm{mg}$ of purified recombinant B19-VP1u protein in Freund's complete adjuvant (Sigma, St. Louis MO) followed by injection at two-week intervals with $0.25\mathrm{mg}$ of B19-VP1u protein in Freund's incomplete adjuvant (Sigma). A control group were immunized with Freund's complete adjuvant and followed by injection with Freund's incomplete adjuvant. All sera reacted specially with the B19-VP1u by immunoblotting analysis. Anti-B19-NS1 anti-sera were obtained by immunization of recombinant His-tag NS1 [24].

# 2.3. ELISA

We used direct antigen-specific ELISA kits to detect anti-dsDNA, anti-CCP IgG, aCL IgG, anti- $\beta 2$ GPI IgG antibodies (INOVA Diagnostics, Inc. San Diego, CA, USA), and AphL IgG (Louisville APL Diagnostics, Inc. Seabrook, TX) according to the manufacture's description. All serum samples were assayed at a dilution of 1:101. After incubation at room temperature for $30\mathrm{min}$ , liquid of each sample well was removed and was washed with phosphate buffered saline (PBS) and subsequently incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG or anti-mouse IgG (Sigma) at a dilution of 1/1000. After incubation at room temperature for $30\mathrm{min}$ , liquid of each sample well was removed and was washed with PBS and subsequently incubated with the color reaction TMB Chromogen (INOVA Diagnostics, Inc. San Diego, CA and Louisville APL Diag.) in the dark for $30\mathrm{min}$ at room temperature. Finally, $100\mu \mathrm{l}$ of HRP stop solution (INOVA and Louisville APL Diagn.) was added to each well and read the absorbance (OD) of each well at $450\mathrm{nm}$ within $1\mathrm{h}$ of stopping the reaction. For absorption experiments, the sera were pre-incubated with various VP1u recombinant proteins for $1\mathrm{h}$ at $37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ before ELISA was performed. A cutoff value determined by sera from rabbits or mouse immunized with PBS or B19-NS1 was obtained (mean +3SD) and the absorbance above the value is regarded as positive. Additionally, to detect the reactivity of various anti-sera against B19-VP1u, $10\mathrm{mmol / l}$ of B19-VP1u recombinant proteins were immobilized on the surface of each sample well of 96-well plates. ELISA was performed as described elsewhere [25]. All anti-sera and the peroxidase conjugated goat anti-rabbit or anti-mouse IgG (Sigma, Saint Louis Mo, USA) were assayed at a dilution of 1/100 and 1/1000, respectively. The color reaction was done with $1\mathrm{mg / ml}$ substrate ABTs [2,2'azino-di-(3-ethylbenzthiazolin-6-sulphonic acid)] (Sigma) in the presence of $0.005\%$ $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ at room temperature for $15\mathrm{min}$ . For absorption experiments, anti-sera were pre-incubated with $50\mathrm{mmol / l}$ of purified B19-VP1u or B19-NS-1 recombinant proteins for one hour at $37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ before ELISA was performed.

# 2.4. Immunoblotting

Sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) was performed as described elsewhere [26] and the protein samples were electrophoretically transferred to nitrocellulose membranes according to the method of Towbin [27]. The membranes were then cut into strips and soaked in $5\%$ nonfat dry milk in PBS. After incubating with different antiserum, the strips were then washed and incubated with alkaline phosphatase conjugated goat anti-human or rabbit IgG antibodies (Sigma) at a dilution of 1/1000. The substrate nitroblue tetrazolium/5-bromo-4-chloro-3 indolyl phosphate (NBT/BCIP) (USB Corp.) was used to detect antigen-antibody complexes. ACA HRP IgG Calibrator A, $\beta 2$ GPI IgG Calibrator A, dsDNA IgG Positive, and CCP IgG Calibrator A were purchased from INOVA. APhL HRP IgG standard 1 was purchased from Louisville APL Diagnostics, Inc (GA, USA). All anti-sera were assayed at a dilution of 1/2 except those anti-sera against B19-VP1u and B19-NS1 that were assayed at a dilution of 1/1000.

# 2.5. Mice and induction of experimental APS by passive transfer

BALB/c female mice at age of 6 weeks were from National Laboratory Animal Center, Taiwan and housed under supervision of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at Chung Shan Medical University, Taichung, Taiwan. Induction of experimental APS by passive transfer was performed according to the method of Blank [28]. A group of 15 BALB/c mice (8 weeks of age) were infused with the rabbit anti-VP1u IgG antibodies $(20\mu \mathrm{g}$ per mouse) through the tail vein. Affinity-purified IgG from control rabbit that were immunized with adjuvant and PBS were also used as controls for immunization.

# 3. Results

# 3.1. Antibody to CL, $\beta 2GPI$ , and PhL cross-reacts with human parvovirus B19 unique region protein

Recent reports indicate that B19-VP1u is linked to phospholipase A2 activity and the pathogenesis of autoimmune diseases including production of autoantibodies [15,18-20]. However, the definite relationship between B19-VP1u and autoantibody production remains unclear. To clarify the association between anti-B19-VP1u antibodies and various autoantibodies, we firstly examined the binding activities of B19-VP1u protein with various autoantibodies against CL,

$\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , and PhL that were obtained from commercial kits. Fig. 1 illustrates the immunoblot results of IgG reactivity to B19-VP1u. Auotoantibody against CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , and PhL were cross-reacted with B19-VP1u (Fig. 1), but not other autoantibodies against dsDNA or CCP. This result is also consistent with our recent observation [23] that sera against B19-VP1u from patients with solely B19 infection in acute phase (diagnostic pattern of $\mathrm{DNA}^{+}/\mathrm{IgM}^{+}/\mathrm{IgG}^{+}$ ) revealed high frequency $(57\%)$ in recognition of CL and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ .

# 3.2. Anti-sera from rabbits immunized with B19-VP1u protein reveal the reactivity against-cardiolipin, $\beta 2GPI,$ and PhL

Previous studies have postulated a connection between B19 infection and APhL [15-18]. To clarify the possible role of B19-VP1u protein in induction of antibody against various autoantigens, rabbits were immunized with purified recombinant B19-VP1u for 3 times at 2-week intervals. Other two rabbits were immunized with B19-NS1 protein or PBS as control groups. Binding reactivity of antibodies against various autoantigens including CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , PhL, dsDNA, Sm, RNP, SSA, SSB, Jo-1, Scl-70, CENP, and CCP were analyzed by ELISA. Purified IgG from the sera of rabbits immunized with B19-VP1u protein exhibited detectable binding activity against B19-VP1u, CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and PhL (Table 1). Additionally, none of these IgG exhibited detectable antibodies against dsDNA, SSA, SSB, Jo-1, Scl-70, Sm, RNP, CENP, and CCP (data not shown). In control experiments, IgG obtained from the sera from rabbits immunized with B19-NS1 or PBS displayed nondetectable reactivity against B19-VP1u, CL and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , dsDNA, Sm, RNP, SSA, SSB, Jo-1, Scl-70, CENP, and CCP, respectively. To clarify the binding specificity of rabbit's IgG against B19-VP1u, CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and PhL, absorption experiments were performed and the experimental results was shown as Table 2. The binding activity in sera from rabbits immunized with B19-VP1u protein was inhibited by purified B19-VP1u at various concentrations. The inhibition of binding activity by purified B19-VP1u protein in sera from rabbits immunized with B19-VP1u protein was $72\%$ . Additionally, the inhibition of binding to CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , and PhL by absorption with $50\mathrm{mmol}/$ 1 of B19-VP1u was $40\%$ , $55\%$ , and $45\%$ , respectively. Notably, none of these sera exhibited detectable antibodies against dsDNA, SSA, SSB, Jo-1, Scl-70, Sm, RNP, CENP, and CCP by inhibition assay (data not shown). Consistently, these results are similar to our previous observations in B19 infected patients [23].

# 3.3. Induction of passive APS by anti-B19-VP1u antibody

A previous study demonstrated the induction of APS in naive mice with mouse monoclonal anti-DNA and human polyclonal anti-cardiolipin antibodies [28]. Therefore, to further clarify the association between anti-B19-VP1u antibodies in B19 infection and autoimmune disease, we employed and modified an experimental model as described previously by immunizing mice intravenously with anti-B19-VP1u antibodies. BALB/c mice were immunized with purified anti-B19-VP1u antibodies from sera of rabbits as described in materials and methods. The titers of the aCL, APhL, and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies in the sera of the immunized mice were evaluated after 1 month of immunization. The sera of mice immunized with anti-B19-VP1u IgG from rabbit antisera, revealed significant increased titer of APhL and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibody as compared to the sera from mice immunized with IgG from control rabbits (Table 3). The immunoglobulin isotype of APhL and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies in sera from mice immunized with rabbit anti-B19-VP1u immunoglobulin were found to be IgG but not IgM. Additionally, no increased titer of aCL antibodies in sera from each group of mice was observed (Table 3). Moreover, the mice immunized with anti-B19-VP1u IgG also revealed thrombocytopenia and prolonged aPTT (Table 3) but not in the control mice immunized with IgG or PBS.

# 4. Discussion

B19 infection has been associated with autoantibody induction [15-18] and implicated as a trigger of various autoimmune diseases [19-22]. However, the role of B19-VP1u in induction of autoimmune diseases remains unclear. This study investigates the reactivity of anit-B19-VP1u IgG from sera of rabbits immunized with purified recombinant B19-VP1u protein in recognition of various autoantigens. Additionally, a modified anti-idiotypic network was performed to clarify the relationship between B19-VP1u antibodies and autoimmune diseases. However, experimental results indicated that anti-B19-VP1u IgG from sera of rabbits immunized with purified recombinant B19-VP1u protein revealed the binding reactivity against B19-VP1u, CL, $\beta 2$ GPI and APhL. Additionally, the mice immunized with purified anti-B19-VP1u IgG developed thrombocytopenia, prolongation of aPTT, and antibodies against $\beta 2$ GPI and PhL. These experimental results strongly suggested the association between B19-VP1u and production of APhL, anti- $\beta 2$ GPI autoantibodies, and APS-like autoimmunity.

Recent studies indicate that B19-VP1u is linked to phospholipase A2 activity [11-14] and the phospholipase domain observed in the B19-VP1u may contribute to the production of anti-phospholipid-protein complex antibodies [11,22]. B19-VP1u has been determined to have phospholipase activity by sPLA assay ELISA kit (Cayman chemical, Company, MI, USA) (unpublished data) that implicated the interaction of B19-VP1u and phospholipid. Additionally, we firstly reported the autoantibodies against both $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and CL in B19-infected patients with antibodies against B19-VP1u and suggested the phospholipase domain observed in the B19-VP1u may have contributed to the production of APhL [23]. Moreover, adsorption experiment revealed the partially reduced reactivity of anti-B19-VP1u antibody to CL and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ [23], suggesting similar epitopes or conformation may exist between B19-VP1u, CL and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ . On the other hand, homologies peptides between B19-VP1u (aa 48 to 54-SLDNPLE; aa 117 to 122-KPGQVS) and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ (aa 239 to 245-SLDGPEE; aa 52 to 57-KPGYVS) were found by employing the protein database (BL2SEQ). These evidences could account for the cross-reactivity of anti-B19-VP1u antibodies to B19-VP1u, CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , and phospholipid. Since rare literature about B19-VP1u structure and function were found, structure simulation and prediction of conformational cryptic epitopes of both $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and B19-VP1u will be crucial for further clarifying the relationships among induction of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and APhL antibodies by B19-VP1u.

Our previous study identified four cases of B19 infection associated with the production of aCL and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies [17]. Consistently, the experimental results presented in this study demonstrate a spectrum of experimental APS-like autoimmunity induced via immunization with purified rabbit ant-B19-VP1u IgG antibodies. APS is characterized by raised titers of circulating APhL that bind target molecules primarily via $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , and/or lupus anticoagulant in association with recurrent fetal loss, thromboembolic phenomena thrombocytopenia, CNS, heart, and other organ involvement [29,30]. $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ shares a common amino acid sequence with various microbial pathogens, and may be the cause of APS or production of the cross-reacting autoantibodies [31-33]. Recent reports indicated that $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ may interact with TLRs and annexin A2 and suggested the complex recognized by anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies may cross-react with TLRs, ultimately triggering the signaling cascade [34,35]. Moreover, numerous infections may lead to increased APhL and clinical manifestations of APS [36,37]. Although the incidence and clinical significance of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies in infectious diseases remain unclear, B19 infections did trigger the generation of various autoantibodies and development of APS.

Taken together, our experimental results demonstrated the cross-reactivity of anti-B19-VP1 antibodies in sera from rabbits immunized with purified recombinant B19-VP1 protein against CL, $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ and APhL. Additionally, passive transfer of APS manifestations and generation of autoantibodies including APhL and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ were also observed in mice immunized with purified rabbit anti-B19-VP1u antibodies. These findings may provide clues in clarifying the role of B19-VP1u among B19 infection, autoantibodies generation, and B19-associated APS.
