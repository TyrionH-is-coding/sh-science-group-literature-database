# ABSTRACT

Background: Anti-phospholipid antibodies (aPLs) are frequently associated with arterial and/or venous thromboembolic complications and recurrent fetal loss in patients with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). We recently reported that the clinical picture of SLE apparently depends on subclasses of aPLs in the patient's sera, but the contribution of each subclass remains uncertain.

Methods: We newly developed an ELISA system for simultaneous detection of six specific categories of aPLs: anti-cardiolipin (aCL), anti- $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I (a $\beta_{2}$ GPI), anti-cardiolipin/ $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I (aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI), anti-phosphatidylserine (aPS), anti-prothrombin (aPT), and anti-phosphatidylserine/prothrombin (aPS/PT). They were measured in 331 patients with SLE including 63 patients with arterial thromboembolic complications, 64 with venous thromboembolic complications, and 43 with recurrent fetal loss. Lupus anticoagulant (LA) activity in their plasma was measured according to the guidelines recommended by the Subcommittee on Lupus Anticoagulant/Phospholipid-Dependent Antibodies.

Results: Multivariate logistic analysis revealed that the concentration of aPS/PT was most closely associated with arterial thrombosis. In contrast, the concentration of $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}\mathsf{GPI}$ was most closely related to venous thrombosis. Furthermore, both aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI and aPS/PT were independently associated with episodes of recurrent fetal loss. Regarding the relation between APLs and LA activity, aPS/PT, followed by $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}$ GPI and aPT, showed the closest association with the presence of LA activity.

Conclusions: Anti-phospholipid syndrome in patients with SLE can be classified by antigenic specificities of their aPLs as to their susceptibility to arterial and/or venous thromboembolic complications or obstetric complications.

Anti-phospholipid antibodies (aPLs) are a heterogeneous group of autoantibodies that appear in a variety of autoimmune diseases, particularly systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) [1-3]. The presence of aPLs is associated with clinical events such as arterial and/or venous thrombosis and recurrent fetal loss [4,5]. At present, the most common and best characterized aPLs are anti-cardiolipin (aCL) and anti- $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I (a $\beta_{2}$ GPI), and lupus anticoagulant (LA) activity [5,6]. ACL and a $\beta_{2}$ GPI are measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) [4,8]. LA activity is currently detected as the inhibitory effect of aPLs on certain in vitro phospholipid-dependent coagulation reactions [7].

A number of clinical studies have established that aPLs are present in approximately $40\%$ of patients with SLE and that the presence of aPLs apparently constitutes a risk factor for arterial and/or venous thrombosis [3,9,10]. Moreover, recent reports indicated that the presence of LA activity detected by the phospholipid-dependent coagulation assay is closely linked with thrombotic events in patients with SLE [11,12]. However, the detection of LA activity involves a series of cumbersome procedures requiring careful quality control. According to the guidelines proposed by the Scientific and Standardization Committee on LA and phospholipid-dependent antibodies of the International Society of Thrombosis and Haemostasis [7,13], the laboratory diagnosis of LA should be done on plasma prepared by double centrifugation and

should follow a four-step procedure respecting the following principles: 1) prolongation of a phospholipid-dependent coagulation assay, 2) evidence of inhibition demonstrated by mixing tests, 3) evidence of phospholipid-dependence demonstrated by confirmation tests, and 4) lack of specific inhibition of any one coagulation factor. Despite internationally accepted guidelines and many efforts to standardize procedures for LA assays, the laboratory diagnosis of LA remains to be harmonized.

Recent reports suggest that ELISAs specific for subclasses of aPLs may be of help to confirm the presence of LA activity [14-16]. It is ideal if a highly specific ELISA can replace the cumbersome assay for LA activity and can also predict clinical complications of each patient by allowing differential detection of aPLs. Therefore, we sought to develop a novel ELISA system for simultaneous detection of six aPL subclasses: aCL, $\alpha \beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , anti-cardiolipin/ $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I (aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI), anti-phosphatidylserine (aPS), anti-prothrombin (aPT), and anti-phosphatidylserine/prothrombin (aPS/PT). We applied this system to the differential detection of these aPLs in 331 patients with SLE including those with arterial thrombosis, venous thrombosis, and recurrent fetal loss.

# Materials and methods

# Study cohort

We studied plasma samples from 331 patients (311 females, 20 males; aged 8-82 years, mean 44.7 years) with SLE. Diagnosis of SLE was made according to the revised criteria of the American College of Rheumatology Criteria for Classification of Systemic Lupus Erythematosus. All the 331 patients with well-defined SLE have been clinically followed regularly for at least 10 years. All the thromboembolic complications occurred either at the onset or during the follow-up. Clinical history revealed that thromboembolic complications were observable in 127 of the 331 SLE patients: arterial thrombosis in 63 patients and venous thrombosis in 64 patients. All incidences of thrombosis had been documented by venography, arteriography, angiography, Doppler ultrasound, and/or computed tomography scan. We defined fetal loss, seen in 43 patients, as abortion, stillbirth, or dead fetus more than twice during our clinical observation. For the control group, we studied plasma samples from 200 normal healthy volunteers (174 women, 26 men; 22-60 years of age; mean, 42.5 years). None of these subjects had any history of thrombosis, and there were no abnormalities in their blood examinations (blood cell counts, coagulation tests, liver function tests, and examinations for autoimmunity). Blood samples were taken into vacuum tubes (5.0-mL total volume, SEKISUI, Japan) containing $0.5\mathrm{mL}$ of $3.13\%$ trisodium citrate $\mathrm{(Na_3C_6H_5O_72H_2O)}$ , and platelet-poor plasma was prepared by double centrifugation at $2800\times g$ for $15\mathrm{min}$ at $18^{\circ}C.$ The plasma samples were then frozen at $-80^{\circ}C$ until batch assays could be performed. Since the LA assay requires the use of plasma samples, we employed plasma samples as a specimen for the ELISAs as well. However, we confirmed that serum and plasma gave identical results by the ELISAs. Informed consent was obtained from all patients and control subjects.

# Detection of LA activity

Using an automated coagulation analyzer KC4 Delta™ (Tcoag Ireland Limited, Ireland), two clotting tests, activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) and dilute Russell's viper venom time (dRVVT) test, were performed for LA activity determination according to the guidelines recommended by the Subcommittee on Lupus Anticoagulant/Phospholipid-Dependent Antibodies [7]. For APTT, a sensitive reagent with low phospholipid concentration (PTT-LA, Diagnostica Stago) was used for screening test and mixing test. A mixing test (plasma samples:normal pooled plasma concentrations of 9:1, 4:1, 1:1, 1:4, and 1:9) was used to confirm the presence of LA activity. The dRVVT test was used to screen

and confirm the presence of LA activity, using a LA-screen and LAconfirm reagents (Gradipore Ltd). The Staclot LA test (Diagnostica Stago) was also used to confirm the presence of LA activity.

# Detection of aCL AND aCL/β2GPI

The concentrations of aCL and aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI were measured by a specific ELISA system. Polystyrene ELISA plates (Nunc-Immunoplate, PolySorp; Kamstrup, Roskilde, Denmark) were coated with $30~\mu \mathrm{L}$ $(50~\mathrm{mg / L}$ in ethanol) of cardiolipin (Sigma, St. Louis, MO) and dried for $3\mathrm{h}$ at room temperature. To avoid nonspecific binding of proteins, the wells were incubated $120\mathrm{min}$ at room temperature with $200~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of Trisbuffered saline (TBS; 50 mmol/L Tris-HCl, 0.1 mol/L NaCl; pH 7.4) containing $10\mathrm{g / L}$ bovine serum albumin (BSA; Sigma). After each well had been washed 4 times with TBS containing $5\mathrm{g / L}$ BSA and $0.5\mathrm{mL / L}$ Tween 20, the wells were then incubated with $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ of TBS containing $5\mathrm{g / L}$ BSA (aCL wells) or $50~\mu \mathrm{l}$ (20 mg/L in TBS containing $5\mathrm{g / L}$ BSA) of human $\beta_{2}$ -GPI (SCIPAC, Sittingbourne, UK) (aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI wells). After 30 min incubation at room temperature, $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of plasma sample (diluted 101-fold with TBS containing $5\mathrm{g / L}$ BSA) was added to each well and incubated for 60 min at room temperature. Thereafter, the wells were washed with TBS containing $5\mathrm{g / L}$ BSA and $0.5\mathrm{mL / L}$ Tween 20 and then incubated with $100~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of affinity-purified peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-human IgG ( $\gamma$ -chain specific) $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{ab}^{\prime})^{2}$ fragment (Sigma) for 60 min at room temperature. The wells were then washed 4 times with TBS containing BSA and Tween 20, and $100~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) solution (Moss Inc., Pasadena, MD) was added to each well. After 30 min incubation, the reaction was terminated by addition of $100~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of 1 N HCl, and the absorbance was measured at $450~\mathrm{nm}$ .

# Statistical analysis

The Dunnett's test was used to compare concentrations of each antibody between the control group (without complications) and other groups with complications (arterial/venous thrombosis, or fetal loss). Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate how well each category of antibody can classify the following clinical statuses of patients: (1) presence of LA activity, past episodes of (2) arterial thrombosis, (3) venous thrombosis, and (4) fetal losses. Each of these clinical statuses was coded as a binary variable (0 or 1) to be used as an object variable. As explanatory variables, age, sex, and concentrations of the 6 categories of aPLs were introduced into the regression model. The stepwise method was used to build an optimal regression model. The importance of each factor (age and subtypes of aPLs) can be expressed as an odd ratio based on its partial regression coefficient. Since it is supposed to be multivariately adjusted for possible influence of other factors, we designated it as the adjusted odds ratio (aOR). Its associated $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) was calculated from the standard error of the partial regression coefficient. All statistical analyses were performed by use of StatFlex statistical software (Ver. 6.0, Arthch Inc., Japan). Overall goodness-of-fit of the regression model was expressed as the adjusted area under the curve (aAUC) of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve: i.e., aAUC was computed based on the distribution of estimated probability of each individual's occurrence of a given clinical event.

# Results

# Prevalence of each aPL

We measured the concentrations of aCL, $\alpha \beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , aCL/ $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , aPS, aPT, and aPS/PT in each absorbance level in milliabsorbance units (mAU). The concentrations detected by the ELISAs of aCL, $\alpha \beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , aCL/ $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , aPS, aPT, and aPS/PT in 200 normal healthy control subjects were logarithmically transformed to adjust for skewness of the distribution before performing the statistical analyses. The upper 95 percentile points of each antibody concentration in the 200 normal controls were set as the cut-off point. The cut-off values were as follows: aCL, $520.7 + 368.3\mathrm{mAU}$ ; $\alpha \beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , $324.4 + 296.6\mathrm{mAU}$ ; aCL/ $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ , $468.0 + 314.4\mathrm{mAU}$ ; aPS, $495.3 + 322.4\mathrm{mAU}$ ; aPT, $298.2 + 146.2\mathrm{mAU}$ ; and aPS/PT, $263.0 + 192.1\mathrm{mAU}$ . A result was regarded as positive when the absorbance exceeded each cut-off value. aCL was detected in 41 (12.4%), $\alpha \beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ in 78 (23.6%), aCL/ $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ in 79 (23.9%), aPS in 43 (13.0%), aPT in 130 (39.3%), and aPS/PT in 133 (40.2%) of the 331 SLE patients.

# Relation between LA activity and each aPL

LA activity was detected in 116 $(35.0\%)$ of the 331 patients with SLE. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that the presence of $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}\mathsf{GPI}$ , aPT, and aPS/PT correlated independently with LA activity (Table 1): a $\beta_{2}$ GPI: aOR = 4.01, $95\%$ CI = 2.14 - 7.54; aPT: aOR = 4.05, $95\%$ CI = 1.69 - 9.73; aPS/PT: aOR = 7.11, $95\%$ CI = 3.40 - 14.85. By combining the test results of these three aPL subclasses using the regression model, the accuracy of predicting the presence of LA activity expressed as aAUC was as high as 0.91 (Fig. 1).

# Discussion

It is now generally accepted that aPLs do not bind directly to the negatively charged phospholipid itself but rather to complexes of the phospholipid and phospholipid-binding proteins and that the most common and well-characterized antigenic targets are $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ and prothrombin [2,17-19]. Recent studies have also indicated that the epitope for aPLs expressed on $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ or prothrombin changes conformationally by interaction with an anionic phospholipid surface or with an oxygen-substituted solid surface [14,15,20-23]. Although several aPL-ELISAs were established for the diagnosis of APS, the clinical importance of these aPL-ELISAs has not yet been determined [24]. Therefore, we newly designed ELISA systems specific for the aPLs reactive with conformationally changed structures of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ and prothrombin with negatively-charged phospholipids such as cardiolipin and phosphatidylserine or $\gamma$ -irradiated surface. We measured concentrations of six categories of aPLs by epitope-specific ELISA systems with specificity for each category in 331 patients with SLE including those with arterial thrombosis, venous thrombosis, and recurrent fetal loss to investigate the clinical significance of aPLs detected by ELISA. The present study showed that positive results of aPS/PT-ELISA correlated most closely with the presence of arterial thrombosis, and those positive results of a $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ -ELISA could serve as a marker of the presence of venous thrombosis. Furthermore, both aCL/ $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ and aPS/PT were significantly related to the status of recurrent fetal loss.

In our aPS/PT ELISA system, purified human prothrombin was added to the phosphatidylserine-coated wells. However, we measured the concentrations of aPS by phosphatidylserine-coated prothrombin absence wells. The presence of aPS was not considered to be associated with thrombotic complications and recurrent fetal loss because there were no statistically significant differences in the median aPS

concentrations in the SLE patients with arterial thrombosis, venous thrombosis, and fetal loss in comparison with those in the SLE patients without these complications. These results suggest that aPS/PT detected by our ELISA system was not directed against phosphatidylserine but recognized the complex of phosphatidylserine and prothrombin and that aPLs that recognize this complex are a significant risk factor for both thrombotic complications and recurrent fetal loss. The aPT concentrations were significantly higher in the SLE patients with arterial thrombosis, venous thrombosis, and recurrent fetal loss than in those SLE patients without complications. However, multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that the presence of aPT was not reliable as a risk factor for these complications. These results suggest that the antibodies detected by the aPT-ELISA differ from those detected by an aPS/PT-specific ELISA and that the OR for the presence of aPS/PT was much higher than that for the presence of aPT.

A very close correlation was observed between the concentrations of $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}\mathsf{GPI}$ and aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI in the plasma of the 331 SLE patients. Therefore, both $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}\mathsf{GPI}$ and aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI concentrations were significantly higher in the SLE patients with thrombotic complications and recurrent fetal loss than in those SLE patients without complications. However, simultaneous comparison of the two factors by multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that the factor most closely associated with venous thromboembolic complications was the presence of $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}\mathsf{GPI}$ but not aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI. It has been suggested that the antibodies detected by the aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI-ELISA differ from those detected by an $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}$ GPI-specific ELISA. The $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}$ GPI-ELISA detects antibodies that are specific for the conformationally changed epitope of $\beta_{2}$ GPI by interaction with an oxygen-substituted solid surface. However, the aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI-ELISA detects not only antibodies that recognize the complex of cardiolipin and $\beta_{2}$ GPI but also antibodies that bind directly to cardiolipin. Therefore, both aCL and aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI were detected in a large number of SLE patients who had no thrombotic complications.

Recent experimental evidence suggested that aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI-ELISA may also detect antibodies that react with some components of human plasma proteins bound to the anionic phospholipid surface [1,15,19]. The finding that the presence of aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI was significantly linked with the status of recurrent fetal loss but not with thrombotic complications suggests that recurrent fetal loss may be associated with antibodies detected by the aCL/ $\beta_{2}$ GPI-ELISA but not by the a $\beta_{2}$ GPI-ELISA.

A number of clinical studies have established that LA activity detected by a phospholipid-dependent coagulation assay is the most significant risk factor for thrombotic events in patients with SLE [11,12,25]. However, plasma factors responsible for LA activity are heterogeneous with respect to the specificities and functional capacities of antibodies [20,24,26]. Some investigations showed that the magnitude of LA activity depends on the avidity of autoantibodies to bind to phospholipid-bound prothrombin or $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ [2,14,15,24]. Our results indicated that antibodies detected by the aPS/PT-ELISA differ from those detected by the aβ2GPI-ELISA, and that aPS/PT, aPT, and aβ2GPI, independently and in that order of significance, associated with the presence of LA activity. By combining the three aPL subclasses, the accuracy of predicting the LA activity was raised to 0.913 in terms of aAUC compared with AUCs for individual antibodies of 0.88, 0.85, 0.81, respectively. Meanwhile, we revealed by multivariate logistic regression analysis that both aPS/PT and aβ₂GPI contribute to predicting the occurrence of thrombotic complications, with aPS/PT possessing specificity to arterial thrombotic events and aβ₂GPI specificity to venous thrombotic events. Therefore, we believe that both aβ₂GPI and aPS/PT should be tested together in the ELISA for all cases of SLE with suspicion of thrombotic complications.

In summary, patients with SLE frequently possess various subclasses of aPLs such as $\mathsf{a}\beta_{2}\mathsf{GPI}$ , $\mathsf{aCL} / \beta_{2}\mathsf{GPI}$ , and aPS/PT. The occurrence of recurrent thrombotic and obstetric complications in SLE apparently depends on variable combinations of these subclasses of aPLs. Therefore, in the differential diagnosis of APS, it is essential to analyze the spectrum of aPL subclasses by use of the ELISA.
