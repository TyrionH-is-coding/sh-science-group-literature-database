# Quantitation of Total and Free Thiol $\beta_{2}$ -Glycoprotein I Levels for Diagnostic and Prognostic Purposes in the Antiphospholipid Syndrome

M. Qi, James C. Weaver, S. A. Krilis, and B. Giannakopoulos

# Abstract

<!-- page 5 -->
$\beta_{2}$ -Glycoprotein I is the major autoantigen in the antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), a prothrombotic disorder characterized by the occurrence of either venous or arterial thrombosis. In women it is also associated with an increased risk of obstetric complications such as recurrent miscarriages. We have identified that the plasma protein $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I in healthy individuals exists in an optimal ratio between two distinct forms, an oxidized and free thiol, reduced form. This ratio is disrupted in pathophysiological conditions associated with increased oxidative stress such as the APS, but also in the setting of age-related macular degeneration and gram-negative sepsis. We have developed assays that quantify plasma/serum levels of total and free thiol $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I which can potentially be used for risk stratification and prognostic purposes in the early stages of the aforementioned conditions.

<!-- page 7 -->
Key words $\beta_{2}$ -Glycoprotein I, Free thiols, Quantitation, ELISA, Antiphospholipid syndrome, Prediction

# 1 Introduction

<!-- page 2 -->
The antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is a disorder whereby patients may develop thrombosis in either the arterial or the venous circulation [1]. Women are also at risk for obstetric complications such as recurrent miscarriages [1]. The established methods for diagnosing patients with APS are ELISA assays that detect specific antibodies that bind the major autoantigen $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I ( $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ ) [2]. The levels of the autoantibodies are measured using semiquantitative methods which have a number of methodological limitations outlined previously [3]. These limitations are a hindrance to securing an accurate diagnosis of APS.

$\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ is a highly conserved and abundant plasma protein $(200~\mu \mathrm{g / mL}$ in humans) [4]. It consists of five domains (DI-V), where each of the first four domains contains two disulphide bonds and the domain V contains three [5]. $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ can undergo posttranslational modification at the disulphide bond, specifically at position Cys288-Cys326 [6-8]. The major form of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ in the circulation is the free thiol form [9] (see Notes 1-7), which is generated by the cleavage of the disulphide bond by the thiol oxidoreductases, thioredoxin-1 (TRX-1), and protein disulphide isomerase [6-8].

We have developed an ELISA to quantitate the free thiol form of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ and when used in conjunction with an ELISA for total plasma $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ we can derive the levels of oxidized $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ in human plasma [9]. We have shown that measuring total and free thiol $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ potentially allows for stratification of thrombotic risk in the setting of APS [9] and disease progression in age-related macular degeneration [10] and possibly in the setting of sepsis [11]. In this chapter we provide the detailed methodology for performing these assays.

# 2 Materials and Buffers Required to Perform the Assays

All reagents are of analytical grade and all buffers used are produced with deionized ultrapure water. Universal precautions should be followed with the handling of all patient biosamples.

1. Vacutainer EDTA (K2) Tube (Becton, Dickinson and Company, Franklin Lakes, NJ).   
2. Maxisorb 96-well plates (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA).   
3. Polyclonal rabbit anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibody [9].   
4. Monoclonal mouse anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibody [9].   
5. Coating buffer: 0.05 M Carbonate-bicarbonate, pH 9.6.   
6. PBS: Dissolve one phosphate-buffered saline tablet (Sigma, St. Louis, MO) in $200~\mathrm{mL}$ of water.   
7. PBST (0.1%): Add 1 mL of Tween 20 into 1 L PBS.   
8. PBST (0.05%): Add 0.5 mL of Tween 20 into 1 L PBS.   
9. Blocking buffer: Dissolve $0.2\mathrm{mg}$ BSA in $10~\mathrm{mL}$ PBST $(0.1\%)$ .   
10. Antibody dilution buffer: $0.25\%$ BSA in PBST $(0.1\%)$ .   
11. Substrate for alkaline phosphatase: Add $5\mathrm{mg}$ para-nitrophenyl phosphate (pNPP) tablet into $5\mathrm{mL}$ substrate buffer, vortex for $5\mathrm{min}$ at room temperature (RT), and protect from light.   
12. Substrate buffer: 1 M Diethanolamine buffer, pH 9.8, with $0.5 \mathrm{mM} \mathrm{MgCl}_2$ .   
13. Stop solution: 1 M NaOH.

14. Free thiol-specific biotinylated probe (MPB): $N^{\alpha}$ -(3-maleimidylpropionyl) biocytin (Thermo Fisher Scientific) dissolved in dimethylformamide at a final concentration of $100\mathrm{mM}$ .   
15. HBS buffer: $20~\mathrm{mM}$ HEPES, $0.14\mathrm{MNaCl}$ , $1.5\mathrm{mM}$ $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ , $4\mathrm{mM}$ KCl, $0.5\mathrm{mM}$ $\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{HPO}_4$ , pH 7.4.   
16. Purified human native $\beta_{2}$ GPI [9].   
17. Pooled normal human plasma $(n = 10)$   
18. Streptavidin microtiter plates (Thermo Fisher Scientific).   
19. $\beta_{2}$ -Glycoprotein I human deficient plasma (Affinity Biologicals Inc., Ancaster, Canada).   
20. Argon gas.

# 3 Patient Samples

1. Collection: Plasma or serum can be used for this assay. Collect blood using Vacutainer EDTA (K2) tubes.   
2. Preparation: Blood samples are centrifuged at $3000 \times g$ for $10\mathrm{min}$ . Plasma samples should then be aliquoted into $1.5\mathrm{mL}$ screw-cap microcentrifuge tubes and stored under argon gas to prevent oxidation of plasma post-collection. Samples are then stored at $-20^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ .   
3. Internal $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I standard: An internal standard for $\beta_{2}$ GPI is derived by pooling ten individual plasma samples which are aliquoted and stored at $-20^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . Each aliquot is thawed once and used on each ELISA plate and serves as an internal standard for the total $\beta_{2}$ GPI ELISA. The amount of $\beta_{2}$ GPI in this pooled plasma sample is quantified using a $\beta_{2}$ GPI commercial ELISA kit (Hyphen Biomed, Neuville-Sur-Oise, France). For the free thiol $\beta_{2}$ GPI ELISA the pooled internal standard is used to express the results of the patient sample as a percentage of the free thiol in the pooled normal plasma internal standard.

# 4 Methods

4.1 ELISA for Measuring Total $\beta_{2}$ GPI Levels

1. Maxisorb 96-well plates are washed with coating buffer twice and then plates are coated with $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of a polyclonal rabbit anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibody (10 nM) in coating buffer. Wells are covered with a sealing tape and the plate is incubated at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ overnight.   
2. After overnight incubation, plates are washed with PBST $(0.1\%)$ four times using an automatic plate washer (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA). To completely remove liquid in the microtiter

well plates they are inverted and the contents emptied on an absorbent paper towel. The wells are then blocked with blocking buffer $(200~\mu \mathrm{L / well})$ and incubated for $1\textrm{h}$ at RT.

3. Standard curve derivation: The $\beta_{2}$ GPI standard is diluted in $\beta_{2}$ GPI-deficient plasma serially to derive a standard curve ranging from 200 to $12.5\mathrm{ng / mL}$ of $\beta_{2}$ GPI.

An example:

The starting concentration of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ used to derive the standard curve is $200~\mu \mathrm{g / mL}$ .

The $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ $(200~\mu \mathrm{g / mL})$ standard is added to $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ -deficient plasma.

Plasma is then diluted 1:1000, 1:2000, 1:4000, 1:8000, and 1:16,000 to derive a standard curve with the following $\beta_{2}$ GPI concentrations at 200, 100, 50, 25, and $12.5\mathrm{ng / mL}$ , respectively.

4. Patient samples: Samples are diluted at 1:100 in PBST (0.1%) and then 1:40 into PBST (0.1%) giving a final dilution of 1:4000.   
5. Negative control: A 1:4000 dilution of $\beta_{2}$ GPI-deficient plasma is used as the negative control.   
6. After the blocking step, plates are washed with PBST $(0.1\%)$ four times as described in step 2.   
7. $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of a mouse anti-human $\beta_{2}$ GPI monoclonal antibody (25 nM in $0.25\%$ BSA/PBST $(0.1\%)$ ) is added at the same time with $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of standard curve samples or patient samples and incubated for 1 h at RT.   
8. The plates are then washed with PBST $(0.1\%)$ four times as described above.   
9. $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of 1 in 4000 dilution (in $0.25\%$ BSA/PBST $(0.1\%)$ ) of alkaline phosphatase-conjugated anti-mouse IgG is then added and incubated for $1\textrm{h}$ at RT.   
10. The plates are washed with PBST $(0.1\%)$ four times as described above.   
11. $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of pNPP substrate is added into each well and incubated for a further $30\mathrm{min}$ at $37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and covered with foil to protect the substrate as it is light sensitive.   
12. $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of stop solution is then added to each well.   
13. Absorbance is read using a microplate reader at a wavelength of $405~\mathrm{nm}$ .

# 4.2 ELISA for Measuring Free Thiol $\beta_{2}$ GPI Levels

1. Labeling plasma protein free thiols with the thiol-specific probe MPB:

We determined the optimal concentration of MPB labeling of human plasma to be $4\mathrm{mM}$ [9]. $2\mu \mathrm{L}$ of $100~\mathrm{mM}$ MPB solution was added into a $200~\mu \mathrm{L}$ Eppendorf tube containing $48~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of patient sample or pooled plasma internal standard. Argon gas was used during the incubation. The tubes were covered by foil to protect them from light and tubes were shaken using a microplate shaker (DLAB Scientific, Riverside, CA) at $600~\mathrm{rpm}$ for $30\mathrm{min}$ at RT.

2. $8\mu \mathrm{L}$ of the MPB-labeled plasma was further diluted into $392~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of Hanks' balanced salt solution (HBSS) in $2\mathrm{mL}$ Eppendorf tubes. These were incubated for $5\mathrm{min}$ at RT.   
3. Acetone precipitation to remove unlabeled MPB:

$1.6~\mathrm{mL}$ of ice-cold acetone was added to $400~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of the diluted MPB-labeled plasma and samples were incubated overnight at $-20^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ .

4. The following day, samples were centrifuged at $18,000 \times g$ at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $20\mathrm{min}$ and the supernatant was discarded.   
5. The protein pellet was washed with $1\mathrm{mL}$ of 4:1 ice-cold acetone:water. The pellet was resuspended in this buffer and centrifuged again for another $20\mathrm{min}$ .   
6. The supernatant was then discarded and the pellet was left to stand for $5\mathrm{min}$ .   
7. The protein pellet was then resuspended in $800~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of PBST $(0.05\%)$ and further incubated for $30\mathrm{min}$ at RT. The protein pellet was vortexed until it was fully dissolved.   
8. The samples and the internal standard were diluted 1:40 in PBST $(0.05\%)$ prior to the ELISA assay. A further 1:20 dilution of the internal standard in PBST $(0.05\%)$ was used to calculate the intraplate coefficient of variation (CV).   
9. BSA-pre-coated streptavidin plates were used for this ELISA assay. $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of 1:4000 final dilution of samples, or internal standard or an additional 1:2000 dilution of the internal standard, was added to the microtiter wells. $50~\mu \mathrm{L}$ of PBST $(0.05\%)$ was used to derive the background absorbance. Plates were incubated for $1.5\mathrm{h}$ at RT (Fig. 3).   
10. Calculations:

The mean value of the replicate readings for each sample was calculated (Fig. 4). # 5 Notes

1. EDTA is the recommended anticoagulant for blood collection when plasma is used.   
2. To avoid the oxidation of samples by oxygen in the atmosphere, argon is used as it is an inert gas and is heavier than oxygen. For long-term storage of plasma samples, screw-cap tubes have to be used to prevent leaking of the argon gas.

5. All materials and reagents are equilibrated at RT prior to use.   
6. It is very important that the pellet of the precipitated protein is never allowed to dry as this can affect the ability of the pellet to be redissolved and can give spurious results. The maximum time that the pellet is left to stand after the acetone solution is removed is 5 min at RT.
