# ABSTRACT

Introduction: Anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-Domain 1 ( $\beta 2$ GPI-D1) antibodies are considered to be a pathogenic subset of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI antibodies and have been strongly associated with thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity in patients with antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). We evaluated the clinical utility of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies for stratifying the risk of thrombosis and/or pregnancy morbidity (PM) in a cohort of Chinese patients with APS and also assessed its correlation with the Global Anti-Phospholipid Syndrome Score (GAPSS).

Materials and methods: Sera and plasma from 192 consecutive APS patients, 17 aPL carriers, 193 patients with other systemic autoimmune diseases, and 120 healthy controls were collected and the presence of aCL IgG/IgM, anti-β2GPI IgG/IgM and anti-β2GPI-D1 IgG antibodies were assessed by chemiluminescence assays (CIA). Detection of LAC was performed according to international guidelines with the use of screening, mixing and confirmation tests. Anti-phosphatidylserine-prothrombin (aPS/PT) IgG and IgM antibodies were detected by commercial ELISA kits.

Results: Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies showed high specificity $(97.12\%)$ and moderate sensitivity $(64.32\%)$ for the diagnosis of APS. Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ antibodies levels were significantly higher in patients with triple aPL positivity than in those with double $(P < 0.001)$ and single positive aPL $(P < 0.001)$ and correlated well with the GAPSS (rho $= 0.60$ $\mathbb{P} < 0.001$ ). Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ antibodies presented with a higher prevalence and higher titers in patients with late pregnancy morbidity $(\geq 10$ weeks) and thrombotic APS compared to those with early pregnancy $(< 10$ weeks) morbidity. Higher anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GP1 - D1}$ antibodies titers effectively distinguished APS from other autoimmune diseases.

Conclusion: This study suggests a predictive role of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies as a strong risk factor for both thrombotic and obstetric APS (OAPS), especially for stratification comparing early PM with late PM and thrombosis.

# 1. Introduction

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is a systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by clinical manifestations of thrombosis and/or obstetric complications with persistent positivity $(>12$ weeks) for antibodies to phospholipid-binding proteins (aPL). Anti-PL are regarded as markers of APS and included among the laboratory criteria currently proposed for the classification of this syndrome [1,2]. The current criteria aPL include anticardiolipin (aCL) antibodies (IgG and IgM) and anti- $\beta 2$ glycoprotein I (anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ ) antibodies (IgG and IgM) detected by solid phase immunoassays and the functional assay lupus anticoagulant (LAC). Anti-PL antibodies are in fact directed against phospholipid-bound proteins and it has been widely demonstrated that $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ is the main antigen recognized by aPL [3]. Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies have been associated with thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity (PM) in APS patients [4].

The key target antigen of aPL is $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , a $50~\mathrm{kDa}$ plasma protein composed of 5 short consensus repeat domains (D1-D5). All domains have been reported to be recognized by subgroups of aPL antibodies, resulting in a large heterogeneous group of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies. To identify patients with a substantial risk of clinical complications, interest has focused on a subset of aPL specifically directed to the domain 1 (D1) of $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ [5,6]. The ability of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -D1 antibodies to induce thrombi has been demonstrated in a mouse model of thrombosis [7,8], in contrast a recent study reported that antibodies against D5 fail to cause thrombosis in rats [9]. Substantial evidence shows that anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -D1 IgG antibodies are pathogenic and strongly associated with thrombosis in both APS patients and aPL carriers [10-12]. In addition, anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -D1 antibodies have presented with the highest frequency and titers in patients with classical triple aPL positivity (aCL IgG/IgM, anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ IgG/IgM, and LAC) implying that these antibodies may further help classify the risk of thrombosis [13]. Several studies have reported that anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -D1 antibodies were a risk factor for classifying obstetric APS [11,14]. Meneghel et al. found that anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -D1 IgG antibodies titers were lower in obstetric APS than in thrombotic APS [15], and a recent study also found that anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ -D1 antibodies were much higher in patients with late pregnancy morbidity than in patients with early pregnancy morbidity [16] suggesting that there may be some differences in the pathogenic mechanisms between early and late pregnancy morbidity in APS patients.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the prevalence and the clinical significance of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 antibodies using a commercially available chemiluminescence immunoassay (CIA) in a large cohort of wellcharacterized Chinese patients with APS, with particular focus on the obstetric subgroup. We also analyze the relationship of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 antibodies levels with the Global Anti-Phospholipid Syndrome Score (GAPSS) which has been proposed to act as a risk stratification tool for patients with APS [17].

# 2. Patients and methods

# 2.1. Patients

The research was performed according to the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Broad of Ruijin Hospital (ID: 2016-61), Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, Shanghai, China. Informed consent was obtained from all individuals included in this study. Sera from 192 APS consecutive patients fulfilling the Sydney criteria and 17 aPL carriers were collected from the APS-SH database (APS-Shanghai), which was established in the year 2000 by expert rheumatologists and statisticians at the Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (Shanghai, China) [18]. And samples were collected from APS patients both at diagnosis and in their follow-up. In this study, conventional aPLs and anti-β2GP1 D1 antibodies were all tested with the samples collected at diagnosis. Of the 192 APS patients, 88 suffered from primary APS (PAPS) with no

underlying autoimmune diseases, and the remaining 104 had secondary APS (SAPS), a clinical entity where the syndrome is present in association with other autoimmune diseases, such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). Patients with other autoimmune diseases were recruited as controls, including 103 with SLE, 29 with Sjögren's syndrome (SS), 31 with rheumatoid arthritis (RA), and 30 with ankylosing spondylitis (AS). A total of 120 samples from age- and sex-matched healthy individuals without any autoimmune diseases were collected as healthy controls.

Thrombotic and PM were defined according to the 2006 revised APS classification criteria [2]. In this study, we classified early fetal loss (< 10 weeks) as "early PM" and late fetal loss and premature delivery (≥ 10 weeks) as "late PM". Patients who experienced both early and late PM were classified as "late PM". "APL carriers" were defined as those having at least two consecutive positive (medium to high titer) results for the same criteria aPL (aCL, anti-β2GPI IgG/IgM, or LAC) carried out no < 12 weeks apart using ELISA assay, and without history of any type of vascular thrombosis. Women with pregnancy morbidity, but not fulfilling the Sydney classification criteria for APS, were classified as aPL carriers [12,19].

# 2.2. LAC

Detection of LAC was performed according to international guidelines with the use of screening, mixing and confirmation tests [20]. Dilute Russell viper venom time (dRVVT) and activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) tests were used to screen samples. Samples with prolonged times for the screening tests were subjected to mixing studies. Reference values were calculated according to the recommendations of the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis. Confirmatory tests were performed by the dRVVT coagulation test in all samples using Diagen Russell's viper venom (Diagnostic Reagents Ltd., Oxon, UK), as previously described [21]. The LAC was considered positive if the ratio of dRVVT screening time/dRVVT confirming time $>1.20$ .

# 2.3. Autoantibodies detection

Anticardiolipin IgG/IgM and anti- $\beta 2$ GPI IgG/IgM were detected by commercially available ELISA kits (QUANTA Lite®, Inova Diagnostics, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) that are routinely used. The suggested cutoff for aCL IgG/IgM is $>20$ GPL/MPL and for anti- $\beta 2$ GPI IgG/IgM is $>20$ units as recommended by the manufacturer [22]. Positive results of either 1, 2, or 3 criteria aPL assays (including aCL IgG/M, anti- $\beta 2$ GP1 IgG/M antibodies, and LAC) were defined as single, double, and triple positivity, respectively. Anti-phosphatidylserine-prothrombin (aPS/PT) IgG/IgM was detected by commercial ELISA kits (QUANTA Lite®, Inova Diagnostics, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). The suggested cutoff values for aPS/PT IgG and IgM antibodies were defined as $>30$ units.

Serum aCL IgG/IgM and anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ IgG/IgM antibodies and anti $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG were also tested by chemiluminescent immunoassays (QUANTA Flash, Inova Diagnostics) using the BIO-FLASH instrument (Biokit s.a., Barcelona, Spain) [23]. The cut-off values for the aCL and $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ IgG/IgM assays were set at 20 chemiluminescent units (CU) based on the manufacturer's recommendations. The manufacturer's cutoff of 20 CU for the anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG assay was tested on a panel of 120 normal healthy controls from the local Chinese population and found to be appropriate.

# 2.5. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (Version 3.5.1). Data that did not conform to the normal distribution detected by the Shapiro-Wilk test were presented as the median [interquartile range, IQR]. $t$ -Tests, $\chi^2$ tests, or Fisher's exact test were used for two-group comparisons where applicable. Sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values (PPV and NPV), positive and negative likelihood ratios (LR+ and LR-), odds ratio (OR) and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis of the aPL assays tested were calculated. Logistics regression analysis was used to calculate the odds ratios for aPL and clinical events. The results were expressed as OR together with $95\%$ Wilson confidence interval (CI). P values of $< 0.05$ were considered statistical significant.

# 3. Results

# 3.1. The diagnostic value of anti- $\beta 2GPI - D1$ IgG antibodies

Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ antibodies titers, expressed as chemiluminescent units (CU), of all subjects are presented in Fig. 1. Patients with APS presented with higher levels of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ antibodies compared to aPL carriers $(\mathrm{P} = 0.02)$ , patients with SLE, SS, RA, and AS $(\mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ , and healthy controls $(\mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ (Fig. 1A). Patients with PAPS presented with higher levels of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ antibodies compared with SAPS patients $(\mathrm{P} < 0.01)$ (Fig. 1B). Using the $20~\mathrm{CU}$ cut off as recommended by the manufacturer, the presence of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies in patients with APS (119/192 $(61.97\%)$ ),

PAPS (61/88 (69.32%)), and SAPS (58/104 (55.77%)) was more frequent than in aPL carriers (6/17 (35.29%)) $(\mathrm{P} < 0.05)$ .

An overview of the clinical performance characteristics of the anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies is shown in Table 1. Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies showed high specificity (97.12%) and moderate sensitivity (64.32%) for the diagnosis of APS. Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ IgG presented the highest sensitivity (80.21%) and aCL IgM showed the highest specificity (98.08%) among the single aPL assays. ROC analysis for the detection of APS showed anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG with an AUC of 0.87, while anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ IgG had the highest AUC of 0.92. Combining the criteria aPL with anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG assays resulted in a sensitivity of $95.68\%$ and a specificity of $87.22\%$ .

The association between anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG positivity and criteria anti-phospholipid antibodies is shown in Fig. 2A. A total of 125 of 170 APS patients $(73.53\%)$ with anti- $\beta 2$ GPI IgG/M antibodies were also positive for anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1antibodies. For 143 patients with LAC positivity, 86 $(60.14\%)$ were positive for anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG and for 163 patients with positive aCL IgG/M, 123 $(75.46\%)$ patients were positive for anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies. Additionally, the levels of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies were significantly correlated with the levels of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI IgG antibodies in all subjects according to Spearman's test (rho $= 0.85$ , P $< 0.001$ (Fig. 2B).

# 3.3. Risk assessment in antiphospholipid syndrome with the anti- $\beta 2GPI - D1$ IgG antibodies assay

The odds ratios (OR) were calculated to evaluate the association between anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GP1}$ antibodies with clinical manifestations. Anti $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies correlated with thrombotic events $(\mathrm{OR} = 12.19,95\%)$ CI:7.58-19.6, $\mathbb{P} <   0.001$ ),arterial thrombosis $(\mathrm{OR} = 9.68,95\%)$ CI:5.41-17.31, $\mathbb{P} <   0.001$ ),venous thrombosis $(\mathrm{OR} = 5.98,95\%)$ CI:3.54-10.09, $\mathbb{P} <   0.001$ ),stroke $(\mathrm{OR} = 7.22,95\%)$ CI:3.69-14.14, $\mathbb{P} <   0.001$ ),and deep vein thrombosis (DVT)

$(\mathrm{OR} = 6.57, 95\% \mathrm{CI}: 3.84 - 11.26, \mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ . Positive anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies were also identified as predictors for PM $(\mathrm{OR} = 12.30, 95\% \mathrm{CI}: 7.19 - 21.06, \mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ , early PM $(\mathrm{OR} = 3.43, 95\% \mathrm{CI}: 1.71 - 6.89, \mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ and late PM $(\mathrm{OR} = 12.12, 95\% \mathrm{CI}: 6.84 - 21.49, \mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ . For these clinical subgroups, D1 is more strongly associated with thrombosis and late PM than with early PM (Fig. 4). For conclusion, anti-B2GPI D1 antibodies played as a risk factor for both thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity. More importantly, anti-B2GPI D1 antibodies can be used for stratification comparing early PM with late PM and thrombosis.

Studies have shown that triple aPL positivity was a risk factor for aPL-mediated new thromboembolism and pregnancy morbidity [25,26]. As shown in Fig. 5, anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies levels were significantly higher in patients with triple aPL positivity compared to patients with single $(\mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ or double aPL positivity $(\mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ . Additionally, patients with double aPL positivity presented with significantly higher levels of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies compared to patients with single aPL positivity $(\mathrm{P} < 0.001)$ .

# 4. Discussion

In this study, we assessed the levels of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies in relation to the clinical phenotypes of APS in the Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine APS-SH database. Most importantly, our study cohort included asymptomatic aPL carriers as well as patients with other systemic autoimmune diseases. The well-balanced composition of the study population allowed us to investigate the distribution of the fine specificities of the anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies across different subgroups. Our results showed that anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies appear to be a promising biomarker with high specificity $(97.12\%)$ and moderate sensitivity $(64.32\%)$ for the diagnosis of APS, while conventional anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GP1}$ IgG antibodies showed higher sensitivity $(80.21\%)$ ,

but lower specificity $(91.37\%)$ . The anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GP1}$ D1 IgG has been reported as a biomarker with $100\%$ specificity and relatively low sensitivity of $52.5\%$ [28]. The positivity of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ antibodies correctly identified patients with a diagnosis of APS and distinguished them from patients with other autoimmune diseases.

Anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies were detected in $61.98\%$ of APS patients, $69.32\%$ of patients with PAPS and $55.77\%$ of patients with SAPS. In addition to assays measuring antibodies against the whole molecule $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ , antibodies reacting with different domains of the protein, particularly to domain D4/5 have also been detected [29]. While anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ D1 antibodies are highly specific for APS, anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ D4/5 antibodies are detected more frequently in non-APS autoimmune diseases. Their detection in apparently healthy anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ positive children has led to the suggestion that the D4/5 antibodies are "innocent" [30] and that determining the ratio between levels of anti-D1 and anti-D4/5 antibodies may be a useful parameter for identifying APS [14,31,32].

<!-- page 7 -->
The prevalence of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies in APS patients in the present study (119/192 $(61.98\%)$ ) was higher than in the cohort from Spain (14/39 $(36\%)$ ) [33], the meta-analysis on APS from 11 different centers (241/548 $(44\%)$ ) [34], and data from Japan (32/61 $(52.5\%)$ ) [28], possibly reflecting differences in patient study populations. Importantly, in this study we found significantly higher levels of anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI - D1}$ IgG antibodies in patients with a triple-positive aPL profile compared to those with double- and single-positive aPL. Triple positivity of aPL has been identified as an important parameter for risk assessment [11,35].

Considerable effort has been focused on developing scoring systems that can pull together clinical and biomarker information in order to predict risk for future clinical events. The GAPSS is one recent model developed for risk assessment of thrombosis in patients with APS [36-38]. The GAPSS includes conventional clinical thrombosis risk factors, criteria aPL, as well as anti-PS/PT antibodies [39,40], which have shown to have a strong association with thrombosis in APS patients [36-40]. There was a strong correlation between the levels of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies and GAPSS in our study. This finding further supports the importance of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies in the evaluation of APS clinical risks. Although thrombotic occlusion may affect the vessels of all organs and tissues, deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in the legs, stroke caused by thrombotic occlusion of cerebral and coronary arteries, and myocardial infarction are common presentations of the syndrome. APS is also associated with pregnancy morbidity, including early fetal loss, late fetal loss and preterm delivery. Our data show that anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies were a risk factor for thrombosis and the subgroups of vascular events, such as arterial thrombosis (AT), venous thrombosis (VT). When evaluating early vs. late pregnancy morbidity, anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 antibodies were present at significantly higher levels and showed a higher OR (OR 12.12) for late PM compared to early PM (OR 3.43). The clinical relevance of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 antibodies in late PM, but not early PM, might reflect the different pathogenic mechanisms involved in the two phenotypes. Indeed, placental thrombotic infarction has been proposed to be implicated only in complications occurring later in the gestational course. Studies have shown that placentae from pregnancies spontaneously terminated during the first trimester did not present with either placental infarction or spiral artery thrombosis [41]. For the pathogenesis mechanism of early miscarriages, the activation of complement, decidual inflammation, repression of proliferation and invasion of trophoblasts, other than thrombotic, played a more significant role [42]. Taken together, our findings suggest the potential value of the IgG anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 antibodies for the diagnosis of APS and as a risk factor for both thrombotic and obstetric APS. More importantly, anti- $\beta 2$ GPI D1 antibodies can be used for stratification comparing early PM with late PM and thrombosis. Our data support the concept that the pathogenic mechanism of early PM differs from late PM and thrombosis. Anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies detection in APS patients with positive anti- $\beta 2$ GPI antibodies may allow a more accurate classification of thrombotic and obstetric risk. We assessed the levels of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies in relation to the clinical phenotypes of APS in the APS-SH database, but as a result of the retrospective nature of our study, future studies will be needed to determine whether these aPL carriers especially those with high anti- $\beta 2$ GP1 D1 antibody levels will develop APS and the risk of recurrent thrombosis in APS patients with high anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG titers. Prospective studies are also needed to validate the clinical utility of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 antibodies as a useful tool in clinical and therapeutic decision-making for aPL positive individuals and APS patients. Our study further suggests a predictive role of anti- $\beta 2$ GPI-D1 IgG antibodies as a strong risk factor for both thrombotic and obstetric APS (OAPS), especially for late adverse pregnancy outcomes.
