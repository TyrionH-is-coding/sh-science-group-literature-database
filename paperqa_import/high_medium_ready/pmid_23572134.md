# Platelet and endothelial activation in catastrophic and quiescent antiphospholipid syndrome

Agnese Bontadi<sup>1</sup>; Amelia Ruffatti<sup>1</sup>; Emanuela Falcinelli<sup>2</sup>; Silvia Giannini<sup>2</sup>; Alessandro Marturano<sup>2</sup>; Marta Tonello<sup>1</sup>; Ariela Hoxha<sup>1</sup>; Vittorio Pengo<sup>3</sup>; Leonardo Punzi<sup>1</sup>; Stefania Momi<sup>2</sup>; Paolo Gresele<sup>2</sup>

<sup>1</sup>Rheumatology Unit, Department of Medicine, University of Padua, Padua, Italy; <sup>2</sup>Division of Internal and Cardiovascular Medicine, Department of Internal Medicine, University of Perugia, Perugia, Italy; <sup>3</sup>Clinical Cardiology, Thrombosis Centre, University of Padua, Padua, Italy

# Summary

Antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) seem to induce a prothrombotic state by activating endothelium and platelets, but no studies have evaluated systematically the effects of aPL from patients with the antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) in quiescent versus catastrophic phase. Our aims were to evaluate the in vitro effects on platelet activation of anti- $\beta_{2}$ glycoprotein I (anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI) antibodies isolated from APS patient in either quiescent or catastrophic phase and to investigate ex vivo platelet and endothelial activation in patients with quiescent or catastrophic APS. Anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies were isolated from plasma of a pregnant woman in two different stages of APS (quiescent and catastrophic, respectively). They were co-incubated with washed platelets from healthy controls that were then challenged with TRAP-6 (thrombin receptor activating peptide 6) and the expression of P-selectin (P-sel) on platelets was assessed by flow cytometry. Moreover, plasma samples from six patients with quiescent, four with catastrophic APS and 10 controls were assessed for several markers of pla

telet and endothelial activation. The results showed that purified anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies co-incubated with platelets enhanced TRAP-6-induced platelet P-sel expression. Notably, anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies isolated during the catastrophic phase enhanced platelet P-sel expression more than antibodies isolated from the same patient in the quiescent stage of disease. Moreover, APS patients had significantly higher plasma levels of soluble (s) Psel, sCD40 ligand, soluble vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 and monocyte chemoattractant protein 1 than control subjects. In addition, sP-sel and von Willebrand factor activity were significantly higher during catastrophic than in quiescent phase.

# Introduction

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is a systemic, autoimmune disease characterised by thromboembolic manifestations and/or obstetric morbidity (recurrent early abortions and/or fetal loss and/ or premature birth) in association with medium/high titres of antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL), such as anticardiolipin (aCL) and/ or anti- $\beta_{2}$ glycoprotein I (anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI) antibodies and/or lupus anticoagulant (LA) activity (1, 2). A small percentage $(1\%)$ of APS patients develop a life-threatening variant of the disease, the so called catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome (CAPS) (3), which is defined as multiorgan thrombosis over a one-week time affecting at least three organs, systems and/or tissues, with histopathologic confirmation of small vessel occlusion in at least one organ or tissue (4, 5). CAPS is associated with a high mortality rate $(\sim 50\%)$ (6, 7), mostly due to cerebral and cardiac thrombotic involvement, infections and multiorgan failure (8).

APL have probably a pathogenic role in APS, but the mechanisms are still not clarified (9). They are directed against phos

pholipid-binding proteins expressed on, or bound to, the surface of vascular endothelial cells or platelets (10). The main protein recognised by aPL is $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ (11-13), a plasma glycoprotein with five domains, the fifth of which is critical for binding to anionic phospholipids (12). Recently some authors showed that $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ exists in two different conformations: a circular, inactive plasmatic form and an "activated", open conformation bound to cell surfaces. In its active form, a cryptic epitope in the first domain of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ becomes exposed and enables antibodies to bind it and form antibody- $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes (14, 15).

Platelets are considered the key players in the pathogenesis of arterial thrombosis, and it has been demonstrated that platelets are activated by dimeric $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ through the binding to two platelet receptors: GPIba and apolipoprotein E receptor 2' (16-19). Furthermore, it has been reported that thrombosis might be induced via the activating action on platelets of complexes between anti $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies, dimers of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ and homotetramers of platelet factor 4 (PF4). Dimerisation of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ takes place mainly in the presence of anti- $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies which in turn bind to the

<!-- page 2 -->
$\mathrm{PF4} / \beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ complexes and activate platelets (20). Anti- $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies were shown to be able to potentiate thrombin-induced platelet activation in vitro, leading to increased thromboxane A2 (TxA2) production (21), confirming the regulatory role of $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ on platelet aggregation. These findings suggest that dysregulated platelet activation favoured by anti- $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies may contribute to the prothrombotic state in APS.

Several studies have tried to identify the reasons for the increased risk of thrombosis in patients with autoimmune disease and the mechanisms through which autoantibodies make platelets more responsive to activation. While Fc receptor was reported to be not involved in the activation of human platelets by rabbit anticardiolipin antibodies (22), a recent study using platelets from patients with systemic lupus erythematosus showed that the ligation of platelet FcγRIIa by circulating IgG complexes (23) render platelet hypersensitive to thrombin stimulation. The possible role of the FcγRIIa in platelet hyperreactivity thus remain to be defined. Moreover, no studies have evaluated systematically the effects on platelet activation of anti-β₂GPI antibodies from patients with APS in quiescent vs catastrophic phase.

Moreover, aPL may induce a prothrombotic condition also by an activating action on the endothelium (24, 25). Several in vitro studies have demonstrated that anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies promote endothelial activation, with the consequent increased expression of adhesion molecules (25), and in vivo studies in mice have confirmed that the thrombogenic effect of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies is associated with endothelial activation (24).

$\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ is a biologically relevant inhibitor of von Willebrand factor (vWF) activity by blocking vWF-dependent platelet adhesion (26); anti- $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies neutralise this inhibitory function and are associated with increased levels of active vWF, thus enhancing the thrombotic risk in patients with APS (26).

The aims of this study were to evaluate the in vitro effects on platelet activation of anti $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies isolated from APS patient in either quiescent or catastrophic phase and to investigate ex vivo platelet and endothelial activation in patients with quiescent or catastrophic APS.

# Materials and methods

The whole study was approved by the University Hospital Ethics Committee and, in accordance with the principles laid down by the Declaration of Helsinki, the plasma were tested after written informed consent was obtained.

# In vitro studies

# Purification of $\beta_{2}$ GPI antigen

The $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ antigen was purified by human pooled plasmas from healthy blood donors. Perchloric acid (65%) was added to induce the precipitation of 90% of human proteins. The precipitate was then adjusted to a pH of 8.0 with a saturated carbonate sodium solution, and it was dialysed against $0.03\mathrm{MNaCl}$ . A first chroma

tography was performed on a Hi-Trap Heparin column using a Gradi-Frac (GE Healthcare Bio-Sciences AB, Uppsala, Sweden) for binding $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ to heparin. Then the column was washed with 0.03 M NaCl buffer and $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ was eluted with $0.35\mathrm{MNaCl}$ buffer. The eluate containing $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ was dialysed against a $0.15\mathrm{MNaCl}$ solution. A second Hi-Trap Heparin chromatography was performed using as a washing solution $0.15\mathrm{MNaCl}$ and as an elution solution $0.35\mathrm{MNaCl}$ . The collected eluate was dialysed against $0.05\mathrm{M}$ acetate buffer, pH 4.8 and then a third chromatography was made on carboxymethyl-cellulose (CM-cellulose).

After 1 hour (h) incubation, the column was washed with acetate buffer and the fractions that at $280\mathrm{nm}$ displayed higher optical density were collected and dialysed against TRIS buffer $\mathsf{pH}7.4$ The quality of the antigen was evaluated with sodium dodecyl sulphate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE).

# Purification of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies

Anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies were isolated from plasma of a pregnant woman in two different stages of APS (quiescent and catastrophic) by affinity chromatography, according to a previously described protocol (27). Filtered plasma was loaded onto a prepacked polypropylene column (HiTrap NHS-activated, GE Healthcare, Stockholm, Sweden) that had been coupled with purified human $\beta_{2}$ GPI (5 mg of protein per milliliter of polypropylene), according to the manufacturer's instructions. After extensive washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies were eluted from the column using 0.1 mol/l glycine, 0.5 mol/l NaCl, pH 2.8, and immediately buffered with 1 mol/l Tris, pH 8.4. The purity of the resulting anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies IgG preparations was checked by SDS-PAGE, and the protein concentration was measured by optical density at $280~\mathrm{nm}$ . Finally, the antigenic specificity of eluted antibodies was determined using a "home made" enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), according to the Consensus Protocol of the European aPL Forum (28).

# Effects of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies on platelet activation

Citrated-venous whole blood was collected in the morning after a $12\mathrm{h}$ fasting from healthy volunteers who had not taking drugs potentially affecting platelet function in the previous 10 days. It was centrifuged at $150\mathrm{g}$ for 10 minutes (min) to obtain platelet-rich plasma (PRP). Washed platelets were obtained by gel filtration of PRP onto Sepharose 2B (Sigma Chemical, St Louis, MO, USA) columns equilibrated with a calcium-free Tyrode's buffer $(3.1\mathrm{mM}$ HEPES, $4\mathrm{mMNaH2PO4}\cdot \mathrm{H2O},$ $137~\mathrm{mMNaCl},$ $2.6~\mathrm{mM}$ KCl, $1$ mM MgCl2, $5.6~\mathrm{mM}$ dextrose, and $0.1\%$ BSA, pH 7.4), as previously described (29). Gel-filtered platelets $(100,000 / \mu l)$ were incubated with $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ $(20~\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ for $30\mathrm{min}$ at $37^{\circ}C$ and with different concentrations (5 and $25~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ ) of anti- $\beta_{2}\mathrm{GPI}$ antibodies. Platelets were then labelled with saturating concentrations of monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) anti-CD62P (P-sel) fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) and anti-CD41 phycoerythrin (PE) (Immunotech SA, Marseille, France), respectively. After $30\mathrm{min}$ of incubation in the dark, samples were diluted in PBS and analysed in a EPICS XL

<!-- page 3 -->
MCL flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, Miami, FL, USA). The instrument was equipped with an argon laser operating at $488~\mathrm{nm}$ FITC and PE fluorescence were detected using $525\mathrm{-nm}$ and $575\mathrm{-nm}$ band-pass filters. Platelets were identified by their lightscatter characteristics and their positivity for platelet-specific mAb CD41 PE), as previously described (30). Platelet activation was detected by P-sel expression.

The same experiments were conducted in the presence of a platelet agonist, the thrombin receptor activating for peptide-6 (TRAP-6), at a subthreshold concentration $(3\mu \mathrm{M})$ (31).

# Role of Fc receptors in platelet priming induced by anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies

To unravel the role of Fc receptors on anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI-induced platelet activation, washed platelets obtained from FcRγ-/- mice (Jackson Mice, Bar Harbour, ME, USA) were used. FcRγ-/- mice were produced by targeted disruption of the gene encoding for the $\gamma$ subunit of FcRγ, thus lacking FcεRI, FcγRIII and FcγRI (32). Knockout mice were backcrossed to C57BL/6 mice and then heterozygotes were intercrossed to produce mice homozygous for the targeted gene. Genotyping was performed by PCR amplification using genomic DNA isolated from tail snip samples as a template. PCR was carried out using allele-specific probes (20 nmol/l each; reverse: 5'-CTC ACG GCT GGC TAT AGC TGC CTT-3' forward: 5'-ACC CTA CTC TAC TGT CGA CTC AAG-3'; neo: 5'-CTC GTG CTT TAC GGT ATC GCC-3'). In selected experiments, in order to evaluate the specific role of FcγRIIa, washed platelets from FcRγ-/- and wild-type mice were preincubated with an anti-FcγRIIa blocking monoclonal antibody (rat anti mouse CD16/CD32 10 μg/ml, BD Pharmingen, San Jose, CA, USA).

Murine platelets were coincubated with anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies, in the presence or in the absence of the anti-FcγRIIa blocking monoclonal antibody for 30 min at $37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , prior to stimulation with TRAP-4 agonist $800~\mu \mathrm{M}$ . Platelet activation was assessed by P-sel expression, as described (33).

Platelets from C57BL/6 wild-type mice (Charles River, Lecco, Italy) were used as controls.

Moreover, gel-filtered human platelets were co-incubated with anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies and an anti-FcγRIIa blocking monoclonal antibody (mouse anti human CD32 $10~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ , AbD Serotec, Oxford, UK) for $30\mathrm{min}$ at $37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ prior to stimulation with TRAP $3\mu \mathrm{M}$ .

# Ex vivo studies

Blood samples were collected from patients with the catastrophic APS $(n = 4)$ , quiescent APS $(n = 6)$ and from controls $(n = 10)$ . The control group was homogeneous for age and sex with the patients and included five healthy subjects and five patients affected with connective tissue diseases. Plasma, obtained by centrifugation of citrated venous blood at $3,000\mathrm{g}$ for $10\mathrm{min}$ , was immediately frozen and stored at $-80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for later assay.

# Markers of platelet activation

Soluble (s) P-sel was measured using an ELISA kit (R&D Systems, Inc., Minneapolis, MN, USA), as previously described (34). This assay employs the quantitative sandwich immunoassay technique using a monoclonal antibody specific for sP-sel precoated onto a microplate and a polyclonal antibody specific for sP-sel conjugated to horseradish peroxidase. The minimum detectable amount of sP-sel is typically less than $0.5\mathrm{ng / ml}$ .

SCD40L, an inflammatory mediator and a marker of in vivo platelet activation, was measured by an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (R&D Systems), as described (34). The detection limit was $0.005\mathrm{ng / ml}$

# Markers of endothelial activation

Soluble vascular cell adhesion protein 1 (sVCAM-1) and monocyte chemotactic protein-1 (MCP-1) were measured by flow cytometry using the FlowCytomix human kit (Bender MedSystems, Vienna, Austria), that allows the simultaneous measurement of different parameters in the same plasma sample, as previously described (35). The instrument was equipped with an argon laser operating at $488~\mathrm{nm}$ . The two bead populations were differentiated by their size and their internal dye intensity, measured using a 675 nm band pass filter. The PE fluorescence intensity of each bead population, detected using a $575~\mathrm{nm}$ band pass filter, was directly related to the respective antigen concentration in the sample, thus allowing the simultaneous quantification of the two different analytes. Standard curves for each analyte were generated and data were analysed by the FlowCytomixPro 2.4 software.

# VWF factor assays

The ristocetin cofactor activity (vWF:Rco) was assessed using a commercial kit (Helena Biosciences Europe, Sunderland, UK) by evaluating the aggregation of formalin-fixed control platelets upon addition of $1\mathrm{mg / ml}$ of ristocetin in the presence of patient plasma. The level of vWF antigen (vWF:Ag) was quantified by an ELISA test (Asserachrom vWF:Ag Kit, Diagnostica Stago, Asnieres, France). The wells of the microtiter plates were coated with a purified murine anti-vWF monoclonal antibody which recognises a functional epitope of vWF. The minimum detectable amount of vWF is typically $1.6\%$ . The vWF ability to bind collagen were evaluated by ELISA (vWF:CB Kit, Life Diagnostics, Clarkston, GA, USA). During the first incubation step the vWF multimers present in the sample bind to the collagen which is attached to the surface of the microwell plate and in a second reaction, peroxidase conjugated anti-vWF antibodies bind to the captured VWF multimers. The resulting colour intensity, which is proportional to high-molecular-weight VWF multimers present in the sample, was determined photometrically (36).

# Determination of avidity for $\beta_{2}$ GPI

Avidity of IgG anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies purified from the same patient during CAPS and quiescent phase of APS was determined by chaotropic ELISA (37) using the same protein concentration (18 $\mu$ g/ml). Samples were diluted in PBS-Tween containing increasing concentrations of NaCl: 0.15 and $0.5\mathrm{M}$ . Discrimination between anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies with high or low avidity was made arbitrarily by comparing the initial binding at $0.15\mathrm{MNaCl}$ with the binding at $0.5\mathrm{MNaCl}$ . High-avidity anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies were considered to be present when the binding at $0.5\mathrm{MNaCl}$ was more than $70\%$ of the binding at $0.15\mathrm{M}$ . Low-avidity anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies were considered to be present when binding at $0.5\mathrm{MNaCl}$ decreased to $25\%$ or less of the initial binding (37).

Detection of specificity for domain 1 and domain 4/5 of $\beta_{2}$ GPI

Specificity for domain 1 and domain $4/5$ of IgG anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies purified from the same patient during CAPS and quiescent phase of APS was investigated using the same protein concentration $(18~\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ . The assays were performed by ELISA kits (Quanta Lite, INOVA, San Diego, CA, USA) kindly provided by the manufacturer. The $\beta_{2}$ GPI-domain 1 antigen, comprising amino acid 1-64 of $\beta_{2}$ GPI, was produced in insect cells and purified using common HIS Tag purification protocols. The antigen was found to have a purity $95\%$ , determined by SDS-PAGE and densitometry analysis. The $\beta_{2}$ GPI-domain $4/5$ was produced in insect cells as described above for the domain 1 antigen. For comparison IgG purified from normal pooled plasma were also tested at the same protein concentration $(18~\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ .

# Statistical analysis

All results were expressed as means $\pm$ standard error of mean (SEM). In vitro data were analysed using the Student's t-test for unpaired values and ex vivo results using the Mann Whitney U-test and the analysis of variance (ANOVA). All tests were two tailed and a value of $p < 0.05$ was considered as significant. All analyses were performed using the SPSS 13 and the GraphPad Prism 5 for Windows softwares.

# Results

# In vitro effects of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies on platelet activation

Incubation of gel-filtered platelet with anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies did not induce P-sel expression on platelets. However, preincubation of platelets with anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies, at either 5 or $25\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ , potentiated the platelet activation response to subthreshold doses of TRAP-6.

The potentiating activity on thrombin-induced platelet aggregation was dose-dependent with 5 and $25~\mu \mathrm{g / ml}$ of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies isolated from APS patient, differently from control IgG which did not affect activation (▶Figure 1A). Anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies isolated from a patient with CAPS $(5\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ displayed a higher potentiating activity on platelet P-sel expression than anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI $(5\mu \mathrm{g / ml})$ from the same patient, but in a period of quiescent APS $(47\%$ vs $5\%)$ , and higher than control IgG (▶Figure 1B). Similar results were obtained using platelet from wild-type mice and FcRy-/- mice (quiescent antibodies: $12\%$ vs $16\%$ ; CAPS: $26\%$ vs $25\%$ ), implying that the $\gamma$ subunit of immunoglobulin Fc receptors (FcεRI, FcγRIII and FcγRI) is not involved in activation process initiated by anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies.

On the contrary, the incubation of human platelets with the FcγRIIa-blocking mAb suppressed the potentiating effect of anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies, both from quiescent APS and from CAPS, on platelet activation (▶Figure 1C), while it did not modify platelet activation induced by TRAP-6 alone (data not shown). The inhibitory effect of blocking mAb, was more evident when anti $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies from CAPS were used (▶Figure 1C). This result

was confirmed using washed mouse platelets were a mAb blocking FcyRIIa, reversed anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies-mediated platelet activation (▶Figure 1D). Interestingly, here too, the percentage of inhibition with the FcyRIIa-blocking mAb was significantly higher with anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies isolated from CAPS (▶Figure 1C-D).

# Determination of avidity for $\beta_{2}$ GPI

High avidity for $\beta_{2}$ GPI was found for both CAPS $(78.1\%)$ and quiescent $(88.4\%)$ IgG anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies

# Detection of specificity for domain 1 and domain 4/5 of $\beta_{2}$ GPI

The binding to domain 1 of CAPS anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies was significantly lower than that of quiescent anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies $(p < 0.001)$ : $6.2 \pm 0.6$ vs $13.1 \pm 1.1$ units (mean $\pm$ SD from 4 replicates); binding of IgG purified from normal pooled plasma was significantly lower $[3.5 \pm 0.1$ units (mean $\pm$ SD from 4 replicates)] than IgG CAPS anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies $(p = 0.002)$ and IgG quiescent anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies $(p < 0.001)$ . Anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies from CAPS and quiescent APS did not show any specificity for domain $4/5$ of $\beta_{2}$ GPI.

# Discussion

Our data show that anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies isolated during the catastrophic phase induce higher platelet activation than those isolated

during the quiescent phase of APS. This enhanced platelet potentiating activity could be due to an increase in antibody avidity during the active phase of disease (38). Indeed, several observations support the hypothesis that high-avidity anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies are associated with a higher incidence of thrombosis (39, 40). In contrast, our experiments showed that both CAPS and quiescent anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies have high avidity for $\beta_{2}$ GPI. Thus, the different platelet activation might be due to different antibody specificity.

George et al. (41) demonstrated that different anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI monoclonal antibodies cloned from a single primary APS patient with well-documented thrombotic episodes bound different domains of $\beta_{2}$ GPI and exhibited different activating effects on endothelial cells. Similarly, we found that the binding to domain 1 of CAPS anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies was significantly lower than that of quiescent anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies. Therefore, different circulating anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies might be present in different phases of the disease. One explanation of this change in recognition pattern could be a trigger inducing a reshuffle of $\beta_{2}$ GPI epitopes during catastrophic phase of APS and a consequent change in antibody specificity. We fail to show binding to domain 4-5 of $\beta_{2}$ GPI and it is currently impracticable for us to investigate that for domain 2 and 3 peptides.

Similar to other autoimmune disorders, APS patients have circulating IgG complexes (42), which may bind platelet Fcγ receptors, in particular FcγRIIa. It is conceivable that the binding of IgG complexes to platelet FcγRIIa initiates signalling that results in platelets becoming more sensitive to other activating stimuli. Our data are in favour of this hypothesis: we have shown that the blockade of Fc receptors, and in particular FcγRIIa, abolishes anti-β₂GPI antibodies-induced platelet hyperreactivity, suggesting that platelet activation by anti-β₂GPI antibodies is mediated specifically by FcγRIIa. This effect was maximally evident when anti-β₂GPI antibodies from CAPS were used, suggesting that previous negative results (22) may have been due to the use of a different antibody source or a different type of aPL. Consistent with our observation, a recent study has shown that the incubation of platelets with IgG complexes initiates downstream signalling that results in a platelets hyperresponsiveness to thrombin by binding to platelet FcγRIIa (23).

In accordance with previous reports (18, 24), our studies showed that in patients with APS there are signs of in vivo platelet (sCD40L, sP-sel) and endothelial activation (sVCAM-1, MCP-1). Interestingly, plasma levels of sP-sel and vWF:Rco were significantly higher in catastrophic than in quiescent APS, showing that in vivo platelet and endothelial activation is enhanced in CAPS.

The most interest of our study is the evidence of different results in catastrophic and quiescent APS both in vitro and ex vivo studies concerning platelet and endothelial activation. Its limit is the low number of studied cases. If our results are confirmed by other large scale investigations, the importance of platelet and endothelial activation in catastrophic APS would be demonstrated.

# What is known about this topic?

- Antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) seem to induce a prothrombotic state by activating endothelium and platelets.   
- Catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is the most severe clinical subset of APS, characterised by multiorgan thrombosis and associated with a high mortality rate.   
- Currently no studies have evaluated the effects on platelet activation of anti- $\beta_{2}$ glycoprotein I (anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI) from patients with APS in quiescent vs catastrophic phase.

# What does this paper add?

- Anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies isolated during the catastrophic phase potentiate platelet activation more than those isolated during the quiescent phase of APS.   
- The binding to domain 1 of catastrophic anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies was significantly lower than that of quiescent anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies.   
- The FcyRIIa receptor is involved in the platelet-potentiating effects of CAPS anti- $\beta_{2}$ GPI antibodies.   
- Plasma levels of soluble P-selectin and von Willebrand factor activity were significantly higher in catastrophic than in quiescent APS.
