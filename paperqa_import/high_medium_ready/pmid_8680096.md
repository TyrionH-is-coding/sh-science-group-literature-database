# Phospholipid-Dependent Anti- $\beta_{2}$ -Glycoprotein I ( $\beta_{2}$ -GPI) Antibodies and Antiphospholipid Syndrome

Junichi KABURAKI, Masataka KUWANA, Mihoko YAMAMOTO*, Shinichi KAWAI**, Eiji MATSUURA*** and Yasuo IKEDA

A portion of anticardiolipin antibodies is defined as phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I ( $\beta_{2}$ -GPI) antibodies and recognizes the conformationally altered $\beta_{2}$ -GPI which interacts with anionic phospholipids. We studied the clinical significance of IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies in patients with antiphospholipid syndrome (APS). The subjects consisted of 60 APS patients. IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies were detected by ELISA in 32 of the 60 patients $(53\%)$ . Significantly higher incidences of prolonged APTT and lupus anticoagulants were found in patients with these anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies. Moreover, significantly lower incidences of malar rash, serositis, LE cell preparation and anti-Sm antibodies were found in patients with these anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies. It was found that $88\%$ of the patients with these anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies satisfied less than five of the revised criteria items for the classification of SLE. These findings indicate the clinical characteristics of APS patients with IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies.

(Internal Medicine 35:105-110,1996)

Key words: anticardiolipin antibodies, antiphospholipid antibodies, systemic lupus erythematosus

# Introduction

The paradoxical phenomenon of lupus anticoagulants (LA) in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) patients with thrombosis has generated intense interest (1). An association has been found between the presence of antiphospholipid antibodies such as anticardiolipin antibodies (aCL) and LA, and clinical features such as thrombosis, intrauterine fetal loss, and thrombocytopenia. Thus, the concept of antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) has been proposed (2-6). It is well-known that IgG is the predominant isotype related to these clinical manifestations (7-9).

Recent studies have shown that patients with APS can be classified into two subsets: patients with primary APS who do not have any underlying collagen diseases, and patients with secondary APS based on a definite diagnosis of a collagen disease (10-13). However, the differences or overlapping features in patients with primary APS and secondary APS based on SLE have not been clarified in terms of their clinical aspects. On the other hand, the serological heterogeneity of aCL has been documented (14-16). $\beta_{2}$ -glycoprotein I ( $\beta_{2}$ -GPI)-dependent

<!-- page 5 -->
aCL have been defined as a portion of aCL which requires $\beta_{2}$ -GPI as a cofactor for binding to cardiolipin in patients with SLE (16, 17). These $\beta_{2}$ -GPI-dependent aCL react with the complex of $\beta_{2}$ -GPI and cardiolipin, and recognize an epitope appearing on conformationally altered $\beta_{2}$ -GPI (17, 18), or clustered $\beta_{2}$ -GPI at high density (19). The important roles of $\beta_{2}$ -GPI as an antigen have been reviewed by Roubey (20). The controversy regarding whether so-called aCL bind to $\beta_{2}$ -GPI has been eliminated by the subsequent reports (21-23). We, therefore, use the term "phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies" in this study. It is possible that phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies inhibit the function of $\beta_{2}$ -GPI such as the inhibition of prothrombinase activity (24), and that this phenomenon leads to thrombotic events.

We previously reported that IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies are found in sera from patients with SLE as well as from patients with primary APS (25), and that these phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies are associated with thrombosis in patients with SLE and lupus-like disease (26). The clinical significance of these anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies has been elucidated in patients with SLE (27). However, the

<!-- page 2 -->
frequency of IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies was $15\%$ in patients with SLE, whereas the frequency of IgG aCL by conventional enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was $44\%$ in the same patients (27). Thus, some patients with APS have phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies, and others do not.

In this study, we extended our investigation to clarify the clinical significance of IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies in patients with APS.

# Patients and Methods

# Patients

The subjects consisted of 60 Japanese patients with APS (49 females and 11 males: mean age $35.8 \pm 11.7$ years). They were diagnosed as having APS according to the previously published criteria (6). Thrombosis, intrauterine fetal loss and thrombocytopenia were found in 28, 18 and 38 of these patients, respectively. IgG aCL measured by conventional ELISA were positive in sera from all patients.

Forty-two patients satisfied the revised criteria for the classification of SLE established by the American Rheumatism Association (ARA: American College of Rheumatology) (28). Patients with SLE were considered clinically active according to the criteria of Schur and Sandson (29).

Medical records were retrospectively reviewed. Serum samples from each patient were kept at $-20^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ until use.

# Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Sera were examined for IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies by the previously described ELISA (16, 18, 25). Human $\beta_{2}$ -GPI was purified by combined column chromatography (17), and was used as a cofactor in this assay. Purity was confirmed by analytical electrophoresis on SDS-polyacrylamide gel (16). The activity of these antibodies was expressed as units relative to the standard serum. When antibody activity was enhanced by the addition of $\beta_{2}$ -GPI at 3.5 unit/ml or more, the serum was determined to be positive for IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies, as in the previously published article (16). This 3.5 unit/ml represented the mean value plus six times the standard deviation in sera from 283 healthy controls.

The reliability of this ELISA was tested by intraassay, interassay and recovery tests. In the intraassay, the antibody activity was measured in five different lots of the same sample. In the interassay, the antibody activity in the same sample was measured five different times. In the recovery test, different sera of various known titers were used: sample A was added to an equal volume of sample B, and whole antibody activity was measured. Results $(\%)$ were calculated as follows:

whole antibody activity -

IgG aCL were screened by conventional ELISA according

to the previously described method (30). The activity of IgG aCL was expressed as units relative to the standard serum. The cutoff point was 17 unit/ml, which was the mean value plus six times the standard deviation in sera from healthy controls.

# Statistical analysis

The relationship between serum antibodies and clinical manifestations was determined by Fisher's exact test. A probability value of $p < 0.05$ was considered statistically significant.

# Results

# Reliability of ELISA for phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies

The results for the two samples in the intraassay were as follows: means $\pm$ SD, $34.4\pm 3.1$ unit/ml and $15.8\pm 1.4$ unit/ml; CV, $8.9\%$ and $8.7\%$ . The results for the two samples in the interassay were as follows: means $\pm$ SD, $30.0\pm 1.5$ unit/ml and $15.2\pm 1.0$ unit/ml; CV, $5.0\%$ and $6.3\%$ . In the recovery test, the results ranged from $86.4\%$ to $110.0\%$ , with a mean rate of $98.6\%$ .

# Frequency of IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies

IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies were detected in 32 of the 60 patients $(53\%)$ with APS. Sera from all patients with IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies reacted positively for IgG aCL by conventional ELISA.

# Clinical features of APS patients with phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies

These 60 APS patients were divided into two groups: 32 patients with IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies and 28 patients without anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies. There were no significant differences in age, female to male ratio and dosage of prednisolone in the patients with and without phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies (Table 1).

The incidences of thrombosis, intrauterine fetal loss and thrombocytopenia were not significantly different. However, significantly lower incidences of malar rash and serositis were found in patients with phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies than in those without anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies. The incidence of renal disorder was lower in patients with anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies, but the difference was not significant.

Eighteen $(56\%)$ of the 32 patients positive for phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies and 24 $(86\%)$ of the 28 patients negative for anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies were diagnosed as having SLE according to the revised criteria for the classification by the ARA (28). This observation was confirmed by the cumulative incidences of the revised criteria items (Fig. 1). It was found that $88\%$ of the patients with phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies satisfied less than 5 revised criteria items, and that the cumulative incidences for less than 3, 4 and 5 of the revised criteria items were significantly different in patients with and without phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies.

Recent studies have shown that patients with APS are divided into two subsets: those with primary APS who do not have any underlying collagen diseases, and those with secondary APS based on a definite diagnosis such as SLE (10-13). However, differences or overlapping features in patients with primary APS and secondary APS based on SLE have been a source of controversy (31-37). Some investigators have argued that it is difficult to distinguish primary APS from secondary APS based on SLE on the basis of the revised criteria for the classification of SLE by the ARA alone (31, 34, 36, 37). One reason for this problem is that thrombotic events in patients with APS cause serositis, renal disorder, and neurological disorder, which are listed as the revised criteria items. Hematological disorders such as hemolytic anemia and thrombocytopenia, and immunological disorders such as BFP-STS, and low titer of ANA are also found in patients with APS.

On the other hand, it was reported that the 1982 revised criteria for the classification of SLE by the ARA were $96\%$ sensitive and $96\%$ specific for SLE (28). However, as the disease controls were rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma, polymyositis and dermatomyositis in this study, APS may be distributed in both SLE patients and non-SLE patients according to these revised criteria. Thus, another serological marker should be studied to distinguish APS patients from definite SLE patients.

aCL represent one of the serological features for the diagnosis of APS (2-6). IgG is the most frequent isotype of aCL in these patients (7-9). Recent studies have revealed heterogeneous characteristics of the aCL: a portion of aCL does not recognize the cardiolipin structure directly, but binds to the complex of cardiolipin and $\beta_{2}$ -GPI, specifically the conformationally altered $\beta_{2}$ -GPI, as a cofactor, especially in patients with SLE (17, 18). These antibodies also recognize clustered $\beta_{2}$ -GPI at high density on anionic phospholipids (19). These aCL have

been defined as $\beta_{2}$ -GPI-dependent aCL (17), and are referred to as phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies in this study. We have already reported that these antibodies are found in sera from patients with SLE and primary APS (25). The clinical significance of these anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies has been studied in patients with SLE (25-27). Sustained positive reactions for IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies were observed over periods ranging from 4 to 98 months (27). However, the frequency of IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies was lower than that of IgG aCL by conventional ELISA (27). On the basis of these results, we investigated the clinical characteristics of patients with APS who had positive serological reactions for IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies.

Significantly higher incidences of prolonged APTT, LA and percentage of patients fulfilling less than five of the revised criteria items, and significantly lower incidences of malar rash, serositis, LE cell preparation and anti-Sm antibodies were observed in patients with IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies. The revised criteria items in these APS patients who satisfied four items suggested that they had an atypical or mild form of SLE.

The titers of IgG aCL by conventional ELISA were also higher in patients with IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies than in those without these anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies. This result suggests that the association between high titers of IgG aCL and thrombosis reported in previous articles (7, 9) may be due to the $\beta_{2}$ -GPI dependency of so-called aCL.

Thus, the results of the present study suggest that patients with APS who have IgG phospholipid-dependent anti- $\beta_{2}$ -GPI antibodies have unique clinical features, and represent the group of patients with APS, who were described in the original article (3). Recent studies have demonstrated that antiphospholipid antibodies require other cofactors such as prothrombin, protein C, and protein S (38, 39). The clinical significance of IgG anti-annexin V antibodies has been also studied in SLE patients with thrombosis (40). There is a possibility that so-called aCL which do not recognize conformationally altered $\beta_{2}$ -GPI may bind to these other cofactors. Further studies are therefore necessary to determine whether patients with APS can be classified into subsets on the basis of the cofactor dependency of antiphospholipid antibodies.
