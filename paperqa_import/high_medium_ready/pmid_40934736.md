# ABSTRACT

Background: A close link between anti phosphatidyl-serine/prothrombin antibodies (aPS/PT), Lupus Anticoagulant (LA) potency and thrombophilia detected by activated protein C Resistance (aPC-R) in triple-positive antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) patients $(\mathrm{LA} + / \text{anticardiolipin - aCL} + / \alpha \beta 2$ Glycoprotein I-a2GPI+) has been observed. Whether this interdependence is present in patients with isolated LA is not known.

Objectives: This cross-sectional study evaluates aPS/PT and aPC-R in isolated LA patients in relation on how the diagnosis of the coagulation inhibitor was made.

Methods: LA was determined using the diluted Russell Viper Venom Time (dRVVT) and the Silica Clotting Time (SCT). IgG and IgM aCL, aβ2GPI and aPS/PT were measured by ELISA. APC-R was determined by thrombin generation assay.

Results: We studied 32 patients with isolated LA (LA+/aCL-/ $\alpha$ 2GPI-), 53 triple positive patients (LA+/aCL+/a2GPI+) and 24 controls. Total (IgG + IgM) aPS/PT antibody titer was lower and aPC-R weaker in isolated LA compared to triple-positive patients. When isolated LA was diagnosed by both coagulation positive tests (dRVVT and SCT), total aPS/PT titers were higher and aPC-R stronger than when isolated LA was diagnosed by a single positive test (either dRVVT or SCT). Total aPS/PT and aPC-R in patients positive for SCT only (dRVVT-, SCT+), were like those of control subjects.

Conclusions: The relation between LA, aPS/PT and aPC-R is linked to the way the coagulation inhibitor is diagnosed. These data may be useful to clinicians when interpreting the clinical value of isolated LA.

# 1. Introduction

Lupus anticoagulant (LA) is considered the major risk factor for thrombosis in patients with antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) [1]. Diagnosis of LA is made when phospholipid-dependent coagulation tests are prolonged, not normalized in mixing studies, and normalized or near normalized by the addition of excess phospholipids in the test system [2,3]. DRVVT and a sensitive aPTT are recommended and diagnosis of LA is made when one or both tests are positive. LA could be associated with the presence of anticardiolipin (aCL) and aβ2-Glycoprotein I (aβ2GPI) antibodies (LA+/aCL+/aβ2GPI+), the so-called triple positivity) or not associated with positive immunological tests (LA+/aCL-/-

aβ2GPI-, here called isolated LA) [4]. LA is considered the main factor for activated protein C resistance (aPC-R) in patients with aPL [5-8]. However, LA is a tricky coagulation test being often false positive (e.g. in the presence of anticoagulants) or difficult to interpret. Antiphosphatidyl-serine/prothrombin (aPS/PT) are major determinants of LA activity [9] and show aPC-R when spiked into normal plasma [10]. Beside LA, APC-R is also difficult to detect in patients with LA on oral anticoagulants but it is evident in non-anticoagulated patients [8]. Indeed, it is very strong in triple positive carriers (no clinical criteria for APS) [11]. We initially evaluated aPS/PT titer and aPC-R in patients with isolated LA compared to triple positive patients taken as a reference group. We hypothesize that, in patients with isolated LA, the diagnosis of

<!-- page 2 -->
the coagulation inhibitor (single or double positive coagulation tests) is associated with the aPS/PT titer and aPC-R, and therefore possibly with the risk of a first thrombotic event or recurrence. Although this association is clear in triple positive patients, this relation has not yet been investigated in patients with isolated LA. The aim of this study was to evaluate if the aPS/PT titers and the strength of aPC-R-based thrombophilia depends on the diagnostic criteria used in patients with isolated LA.

# 2. Materials and methods

# 2.1. Patients

In this cross-sectional study, plasma samples of non-anticoagulated patients with LA and 24 healthy subjects selected to represent a comparable age and sex distribution were studied. Thirty-two were negative for aCL and aβ2GPI antibodies (isolated LA) and 53 were positive for aCL and aβ2GPI antibodies of same isotype (triple positivity) confirmed on two occasions at least 12 weeks apart. All the patients tested negative for factor V Leiden and no women were taking the contraceptive pill at the time of study. Patients gave their written informed consent to utilize their stored residual plasma in this study.

# 2.2. Lupus anticoagulant, aCL, $\alpha \beta 2GPI$ antibodies

Plasma was obtained by double centrifugation according to current LA guidelines and kept frozen at $-80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ until tested. Tests for the determination of aPL antibodies were performed as previously described [10]. Briefly, LA was determined using the diluted Russell Viper Venom Time (dRVVT, Werfen Group, Milan, Italy) and the Silica Clotting Time (SCT, Werfen Group, Milan, Italy) [10,12]. The cut-off values of coagulation tests calculated using the ratio between dRVVT or SCT in 1:1 mixing studies and that of normal pooled plasma (NPP) were 1.16 and 1.2, respectively. IgG and IgM aCL, aβ2GPI and aPS/PT were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA; QUANTA Lite Werfen Group, Milan, Italy) following the guidance from the Scientific Standardization Committee on lupus anticoagulant/antiphospholipid antibodies of the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis. [13].

# 2.3. Measurement of aPC-R

APC-R was determined by thrombin generation assay in plasma of non-anticoagulated patients, following the recommendations of the International Society of Thrombosis and Haemostasis [14] in the absence and presence of $10\mathrm{-nM}$ activated protein C (aPC) (Diagnostica Stago). Briefly, Thrombin generation was triggered by $4\mathrm{pM}$ tissue factor and measured in platelet-poor plasma with the calibrated automated thrombogram method (Thrombinoscope BV). A comparison of endogenous thrombin potential (ETP) in the presence or absence of aPC enables assessment of the function of aPC in the system [10]. aPC-R is expressed as the percentage inhibition of ETP calculated as follows: ([ETP without aPC - ETP with aPC] $\div$ ETP without aPC) $\times 100$ . Data were normalized assuming that the maximum aPC activity was $100\%$ when using Tris buffer. The lower the value of percentage, the greater the aPC-R. Cutoff value for aPC-R was the approximate 99th percentile of that obtained in 30 healthy subjects (aPC-R, $91.2\%$ ).

# 3. Statistics

Descriptive statistics are reported as appropriate: categorical data are expressed as frequencies and continuous data are reported as mean and standard deviation or median and interquartile range according to data distribution. Mann Whitney test was used to compare groups for variables with non-parametric distribution, $t$ -test for variables with parametric distribution, and chi square test for categorical data. $P <$

0.05 was considered significant. GraphPad Prism (version 8, GraphPad software) was used to perform the analyses.

# 4. Results

Demographic features of the study patients are depicted in Table 1 and were similar in isolated LA and triple positive patients. However, LA potency was weaker and IgG and IgM aPS/PT antibodies were lower in isolated LA compared to triple positive patients.

Fig. 1 shows values of total aPS/PT in patients with isolated LA (blue dots), those in a group of triple positive patients (red dots) and in controls (black dots). Median and interquartile range of total aPS/PT in isolated LA, triple positive and controls were $87~\mathrm{U / mL}$ (IQR, 23-209), $192~\mathrm{U / mL}$ (IQR, 101-473) and $16~\mathrm{U / mL}$ (IQR, 13-18), respectively. Compared to triple-positive patients total $(\mathrm{IgG} + \mathrm{IgM})$ aPS/PT antibodies were significantly lower in isolated LA.

Fig. 2 shows values of aPC-R in patients with isolated LA (blue dots), those in a group of triple positive patients (red dots) and in controls (black dots). In both groups of patients, aPC-R is increased with respect to controls [82.2 %, IQR 59.1-97.2 in isolate LA and 50.1 %, IQR 29.9-70.1 in triple positive patients compared to 96.4 %, IQR 95.4-97.3 in controls $(p = 0.003$ and $p < 0.0001$ , respectively)]. Notably, aPC-R is significantly weaker in patients with isolated LA with respect to triple positive patients.

Diagnosis of LA in isolated LA was made in 16 cases when both dRVVT and SCT were positive, in 10 cases when dRVVT was the only positive test, and in 6 cases when SCT was the only positive test.

As shown in Fig. 3A, when dRVVT and SCT were both positive in isolated LA, total $(\mathrm{IgG} + \mathrm{IgM})$ aPS/PT were significantly higher than in the group of patients with a single positive coagulation test (118 U/mL, IQR 58-310 vs 37 U/mL, IQR 12-165). Values in single positive coagulation test were not different from those of controls (16 U/mL IQR 13-18). Data of single positive coagulation test are detailed in Fig. 3B. When dRVVT was the only positive test, total $(\mathrm{IgG} + \mathrm{IgM})$ aPS/PT (87 U/mL, IQR 16-207) were higher in patients with isolated LA than in controls. On the other hand, when SCT was the only positive test, total aPS/PT values (15 U/mL, IQR 10-56) were not different from those of controls.

# 5. Discussion

This study shows that values of aPS/PT and the degree of thrombophilia measured by aPC-R are significantly lower in patients with isolated LA (LA+/aCL-/aβ2GPI-) compared with those of triple positive patients (LA+/aCL+/aβ2GPI+). These data are in line with previous findings where a close link between aPS/PT, LA potency, and aPC-R has been demonstrated [15,16]. In triple positive patients, both the higher aPS/PT titers and the presence of aβ2GPI antibodies (tetra positive patients) contribute to achieve a robust aPC-R. Proof of this was clearly shown using immunoaffinity purified aPS/PT and aβ2GPI antibodies spiked into normal pooled plasma and tested in thrombin generation assay in the absence and presence of activated Protein C (aPC). Indeed, both antibodies concur to determine aPC-R, which in the case of aβ2GPI is moderate and in the case of aPS/PT is strong [10]. APS/PT are commonly present in triple positive patients, while their presence is less frequent [17] and more commonly of IgM isotype [16] in isolated LA. A strong aPC-R could explain in part why triple (tetra) positive patients are more prone to incident thromboembolic events in the follow up period [18,19]. On the other hand, the milder aPC-R in isolated LA could account for the low rate of thromboembolic events in the follow up period in these patients [19]. LA is considered the main factor for thrombophilia evaluated by aPC-R in patients with aPL [5,6]. LA and aPC-R are both suppressed by adding an excess of phospholipids in the system [20]. However, in clinical practice, LA could be false positive in patients on anticoagulation [21] and this is why in this study we used plasma of non-anticoagulated patients [22]. In addition, LA results could be influenced by the way the detection is made (both coagulation tests positive or only one positive coagulation test). Although tests show a good correlation of coagulation time ratios (coagulation times of 1:1 mixture of patient and normal plasma divided by coagulation time of normal plasma), LA potency is stronger when both coagulation tests are positive [23]. The score for persistent LA positive in the new classification criteria is 5 points thus satisfying the laboratory criteria. The way LA is diagnosed is not mentioned but may be critical as shown in our study and may not always reflect the true risk for thromboembolic events [24].

In this study, titers of aPS/PT and the degree of aPC-R were markedly affected when isolated LA was diagnosed by double or single positive coagulation tests (Figs. 3A and 4A). Total aPS/PT titers were significantly higher and aPC-R was stronger when both coagulation test were positive. In contrast, aPS/PT values and aPC-R were not different from controls in case of SCT being the only positive test. Measurement of aPS/PT in patients with isolated LA is relevant given that: a) they are a measure of aPC-R, and b) they are more frequently found when LA is diagnosed by both coagulation tests [17]. Since aPC-R is a risk factor for thromboembolic events, these data indicate that patients with isolated LA diagnosed by means of a single positive test could be at lower risk of thrombosis. Data from this study are important as they distinguish isolated LA into categories (positive in one or both coagulation tests) with different hypercoagulable states. Further studies are needed to understand whether these laboratory data could modify clinical practice. In conclusion, many years after the description of LA, this in vitro phenomenon lends itself to new considerations. It is important to understand that not all LAs are the same and, in particular, isolated LAs should be interpreted according to the different positivity in the coagulation tests used, and the presence and titer of aPS/PT antibodies.

Limitation of the study: generalization of the results from this study is not possible because a) we do not know if results are reproducible with different commercial kits of dRVVT or to other tests exploring the intrinsic pathway of coagulation and b) the number of the studied patients with isolated LA was low. However, this study generates the hypothesis that the determination of aPS/PT and the criteria used for LA diagnosis could impact on the degree of thrombophilia in LA carriers.
