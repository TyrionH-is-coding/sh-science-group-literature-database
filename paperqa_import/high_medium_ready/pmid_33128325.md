# Abstract

Background: Trial of Rivaroxaban in AntiPhospholipid Syndrome was a prospective randomized, open-label, noninferiority study conducted in 14 centers in Italy. Rivaroxaban was compared with warfarin for the prevention of thromboembolic events, major bleeding, and vascular death in high-risk, triple-positive patients with antiphospholipid syndrome.

Objective: The aim of this paper is to report the events during the 2-year follow-up after the study closure.

Methods: On January 28, 2018, the trial was prematurely stopped by adjudication and safety committee for an excess of events in the rivaroxaban group. Randomized

patients were advised on trial results and those randomized to rivaroxaban were solicited to switch to warfarin. All 14 participating centers were asked and accepted to follow their patients for clinical events. This report describes the rate of events that occurred between January 28, 2018, and January 28, 2020.

Results: Of 120 randomized patients, 115 were available for follow-up. Outcome events were two in six (33.3%) patients who remained on direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) and six in 109 (5.7%) patients on warfarin (hazard ratio [HR] 6.9; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.4-34.5, $P = .018$ ). The two patients on DOACs (one taking dabigatran and one taking rivaroxaban) suffered from thromboembolic events, whereas of the six patients with composite outcomes on warfarin, three had thromboembolic events (HR for thrombosis 13.3; 95% CI 2.2-79.9, $P = .005$ ).

Conclusion: These data further support the use of warfarin in high-risk patients with antiphospholipid syndrome.

# 1 | INTRODUCTION

Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) are progressively replacing warfarin in the management of venous and arterial thromboembolism. Nevertheless, in some clinical conditions, namely mechanical heart valve prostheses, thrombosis in the left ventricle and antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), warfarin is considered the drug of choice. In the setting of APS, randomized controlled trials, case series, and a meta-analysis have suggested that DOACs should not be used in this clinical condition, at least in high-risk triple-positive patients. Whether DOACs could be used in patients with a low-risk antiphospholipid antibody profile or a single episode of venous thromboembolism should be tested in appropriate clinical studies. The Trial of Rivaroxaban in AntiPhospholipid Syndrome (TRAPS) trial, a randomized open-label trial of rivaroxaban vs warfarin (international normalized ratio [INR] 2.0-3.0) in high-risk triple-positive APS patients, was interrupted before completion for a high incidence of arterial thrombosis in the rivaroxaban group. The aim of the present study is to report events in the TRAPS participants in the 2-year follow-up after the study closure.

# 2 METHODS

At the time of closure, the TRAPS trial steering committee immediately alerted the 14 participating centers that patients receiving rivaroxaban required transitioning to warfarin. A written recommendation was provided to coadminister warfarin and rivaroxaban for a minimum of 4 days to minimize the risk of thromboembolism. Investigators were invited to continue the follow-up of all randomized patients by visits every 6 months or more frequently if deemed necessary. # 3 | RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

All 14 participating centers accepted participation in the follow-up study. Of the 120 patients randomized in the TRAPS trial, 115 were available for follow-up, whereas two patients died (one on rivaroxaban and one on warfarin), two were lost to follow-up (both on warfarin), and one permanently discontinued anticoagulant treatment (Figure 1). Eleven patients stopped their assigned therapy during the TRAPS trial but were back on oral anticoagulant treatment in the follow-up period. After trial closure, most patients $(n = 109)$ were switched to warfarin at a target INR of 2.5. Six patients did not accept the switch to warfarin despite recommendations: five continued rivaroxaban, whereas one patient switched treatment from rivaroxaban to dabigatran. No event occurred during the transition from rivaroxaban to warfarin. Overall, eight events were reported during the 2-year follow-up period (Table 1). Of these, two occurred in the six patients $(33.3\%)$ treated with DOACs, whereas six occurred in the 109 patients $(5.5\%)$ on warfarin. On Cox regression, the risk of cumulative events was significantly higher in the DOACs group (HR 6.9; $95\%$ CI 1.4-34.5, $P = .018$ ). The risk of thrombotic events only, was also higher among the DOACs patients (HR 13.3; $95\%$ CI 2.2-79.9, $P = .005$ ).

Among DOAC-treated patients, one event was a proximal deep vein thrombosis (occurred on dabigatran $150\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily) and one an ischemic stroke (occurred on rivaroxaban $20\mathrm{mg}$ each day). The patient on dabigatran had a history of both venous and arterial thrombosis and autoimmune disease (lupus-like). She was also taking low-dose aspirin $(100\mathrm{mg}$ each day) on top of anticoagulants at the time of the event. The patient on rivaroxaban had a history of stroke, an associated autoimmune disease, and was taking statins at the time of event. Cerebral imaging (magnetic resonance) was positive for cerebral ischemia consistent with the patient symptoms. Of the remaining four patients on rivaroxaban, one developed a severe thrombocytopenia with a fall in hemoglobin level of $>2\mathrm{g/L}$ but without signs of overt bleeding and was not classified as a major bleeding event. The follow-up of the remaining three patients was unremarkable.

Three patients on warfarin had thromboembolic events, two were arterial and one was venous thrombosis (Table 1). One arterial event (stent occlusion of an iliac artery) occurred in a patient with a history of venous thromboembolism and peripheral artery disease. The other arterial event (transient ischemic accident) occurred in a patient with history of stroke. No patients with recurrent thrombosis during additional follow-up after study closure had the primary endpoint during the TRAPS trial initial follow-up.

Three patients got pregnant during the 2-year follow up: treatment with LMWH plus low-dose aspirin resulted in a successful pregnancy without complications. A 23-year-old male on rivaroxaban during the TRAPS trial and immediately switched to warfarin at study closure, underwent surgical pulmonary thromboendarterectomy for chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension 3 months after the trial closure. This study was conducted in the 14 centers participating in the TRAPS trial. They were reference centers in Italy for the treatment of APS and accumulated expertise in managing DOACs and ability in maintaining patients treated with warfarin at optimal time in therapeutic range. Results show a higher rate of outcome events after the TRAPS closure in patients taking DOACs compared with those taking warfarin. Although the number of patients with events is low to draw conclusions, it is remarkable that two of six on DOACs developed a thrombotic event. These data support the accumulating evidence that warfarin should be the drug of choice in high-risk patients with APS. $^{3,6,15,16}$ In this follow-up study, thromboembolic events occurred not only in two patients, one on rivaroxaban and one in dabigatran, but also in patients on warfarin, as expected in these high-risk patients. $^{17}$

It should be underlined that different antiphospholipid (aPL) laboratory profiles are not equivalent in predicting future thromboembolic events and that their clinical relevance is quite different. Triple positivity (in most cases tetra positivity $^{18}$ ) is always confirmed after

12 weeks, whereas single test positivity is confirmed after 12 weeks in $<50\%$ of cases. $^{19}$ All 14 participating centers confirmed the presence of triple-positive aPL profiles during the 2-year follow-up after TRAPS closure. Triple-positive individuals are at high risk of recurrence of events and are prone to develop chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension. $^{20,21}$ Moreover, all 14 catastrophic APS seen in the Padua hospital were triple-positive patients. $^{22}$ In addition to an aPL profile, the patient thrombotic burden might also be relevant in predicting future thromboembolic events. $^{23}$ Although some data suggest that the rate of recurrent thrombosis is lower in APS patients with isolated venous manifestations than in overall APS patients including high-risk patients, $^{24}$ studies on APS patients with a homogeneous aPL profile are warranted to answer to patients preference to switch from warfarin to DOACs. In our view, this should be considered with caution only in patients with a non-triple-positive aPL profile. $^{25}$
