# Caveolin-1 as a Marker of Endothelial Damage in Primary Antiphospholipid Syndrome Nephropathy

## Abstract

Rationale & Objective: Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) as seen in antiphospholipid syndrome nephropathy (APSN) is associated with poor kidney outcomes. Caveolin-1 (Cav-1) has emerged as a potential marker of endothelial damage and microvascular injury.

Study Design: This retrospective study investigates the diagnostic potential value of Cav-1 immunohistochemistry in APSN-related TMA.

Setting & Participants: Patients were retrieved from the Piedmont and Aosta Valley Rare Disease Registry, from which a total of 771 APS cases had been recorded.

Exposure: Only case with biopsy-proven renal involvement were included.

Outcomes: Cases were classified into three histopathological clusters: TMA (APSN-TMA), hyperplastic vasculopathy, and subendothelial edema. Immunohistochemical staining for Cav-1 was performed and graded based on expression in peritubular and glomerular capillaries.

Analytical Approach: Clinical and laboratory data, including antiphospholipid antibody profiles, were correlated with Cav-1 expression.

Results: Out of 771 patients with APS, a total of 11 patients with primary APS and renal involvement were retrieved (1.43%). APSN-TMA was identified in 7 (63.3%) cases, hyperplastic vasculopathy in 2 (18.1%), and subendothelial edema in 2 (18.1%). Cav-1 expression was significantly higher in APSN-TMA cases in both glomerular ( P < 0.001) and peritubular ( P < 0.05) capillaries compared with other clusters, with 5 of 7 APSN-TMA cases exhibiting diffuse (grade III) staining. Triple antiphospholipid antibody positive testing was more prevalent in APSN-TMA cases, reinforcing their distinct pathophysiological profile.

Limitations: The small sample size warrants validation in larger cohorts, and Cav-1 expression, although associated with APSN, might not be disease-specific.

Conclusions: APSN-TMA is a rare manifestation of a rare disease. Cav-1 is strongly associated with APSN-TMA and may serve as a novel marker for its diagnosis and stratification. Given the poor renal prognosis of APSN-TMA, identifying affected patients is crucial for optimizing management strategies.

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is an autoimmune condition that can cause blood clots in many organs, including the kidneys. In some patients, a type of severe small-vessel injury called thrombotic microangiopathy develops, leading to poor kidney outcomes. Diagnosing this form of kidney damage can be challenging. In our study, we examined kidney tissue from people with APS using a special stain for a protein called Caveolin-1, which is found in damaged blood vessel cells. We found that high Caveolin-1 levels were strongly linked to the most aggressive form of APS-related kidney injury. This marker could help doctors identify high-risk patients earlier, improve treatment decisions, and bring more clarity to the complex and varied ways APS can affect the kidneys.

## Full Text

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is primarily characterized by thrombosis affecting both the venous and arterial systems across vessels of all calibers and various organs. 1 , 2 Although less common, renal involvement in patients with detectable antiphospholipid antibodies (aPLs) significantly affects morbidity and mortality. Its clinical and histologic presentations are heterogeneous, with varying prevalence reported in different studies. 3 , 4 , 5

The term antiphospholipid nephropathy (APSN) has been used to describe a diverse group of aPL-related renal lesions with potentially distinct prognostic significance and clinical manifestations. 4 Our group has previously demonstrated that different patterns of renal lesions exist, each with distinct prognoses and treatment responses, emphasizing the importance of kidney biopsy when aPL-related renal involvement is suspected. 6 Specifically, our findings support the existence of 3 types of aPL-associated renal involvement. The first pattern is characterized by a higher prevalence of glomerular capillary and arteriolar thrombi, along with fragmented red blood cells in the subendothelial space. The second pattern is defined by a greater frequency of fibromyointimal proliferative lesions, resembling hyperplastic vasculopathy. The third is marked by increased subendothelial edema affecting both glomerular capillaries and arterioles.

Renal histopathological lesions indicative of thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), as observed in the first group, are associated with poorer kidney outcomes and systemic thrombotic events, aligning with the definition of APSN. Although this classification is consistent with current APS criteria, additional diagnostic approaches could further refine disease characterization and guide treatment decisions, ultimately improving patient management.

Caveolin-1 (Cav-1) is a structural protein widely expressed in terminally differentiated cells, including endothelial cells, epithelial cells, and fibroblasts. Its prognostic significance has been demonstrated in various pathological conditions. 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 In stage I lung adenocarcinoma, Cav-1 expression in cancer-associated fibroblasts and cancer cells was identified as a key predictor of disease recurrence and poor outcomes, showing a strong correlation with shorter overall and disease-free survival, vascular invasion, and advanced cancer stage. 8 , 14

The potential role of Cav-1 has also been explored in kidney pathology. Immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis of Cav-1 expression in kidney biopsies has been linked to antibody-mediated injury, with positive expression correlating with microvascular inflammation and antibody-mediated rejection. More recently, pathological studies have highlighted Cav-1 immunohistochemistry as a potential marker of endothelial damage. 15 , 16

This study aimed to determine whether Cav-1 can serve as an additional IHC marker for diagnosing primary APSN and to assess its impact on renal prognosis.

## Methods

This retrospective study analyzes a series of kidney biopsies from patients with confirmed renal involvement in primary APS (PAPS).

Patients were retrieved as having PAPS from the Piedmont and Aosta Valley Rare Disease Registry, as part of the National Registry of Rare Diseases, by the coordinating Center of Piedmont and Aosta Valley Network for Rare Diseases, S. Giovanni Bosco Hospital, Turin, Italy. The Registry is based on a continuously implemented dataset, including socio-demographic and disease data, as detailed elsewhere. 17 A total of 771 patients with a definite diagnosis of APS has been included in the Rare Disease Register of Piedmont and Aosta Valley over the last 35 years. 17

Cases diagnosed through indication biopsy between September 2015 and February 2025 were retrieved and classified according to our previously established cluster system. They were divided into 3 groups: (1) TMA, as seen in APSN; (2) hyperplastic vasculopathy; and (3) subendothelial edema. To minimize confounding factors, samples with concurrent kidney pathologies, such as diabetic nephropathy, were excluded.

Demographic, clinical, and pathologic data were collected from patients’ clinical records and original diagnostic reports, then entered into a pseudonymized database. Kidney samples were obtained via incisional transcutaneous biopsies, processed, and stained following standard protocols. The biopsies were independently reviewed by 2 pathologists (A.B. and P.C.), and any discrepancies were resolved through collective discussion to reach a consensus classification.

For the purposes of this study, APSN-related TMA was defined histologically by the presence of at least 2 of the following features: endothelial cell enlargement/swelling, subendothelial or intimal edema, fragmented red blood cells within capillary loops or arteriolar walls, and fibrin thrombi in glomerular or peritubular capillaries. Cases classified as “subendothelial edema” showed this finding in glomerular and/or arteriolar walls but lacked other defining TMA features (such as red blood cell fragmentation or fibrin thrombi) and were therefore considered a distinct, non-TMA pattern in accordance with our previously established cluster classification. 6

As previously described, Cav-1 IHC staining was performed using the N-20 clone (Santa Cruz Biotechnology), applying a grading system analogous to C4d scoring. 17 Cav-1 expression was evaluated separately in peritubular and glomerular capillaries. Cases were classified as grade 0 (negative) when no Cav-1 expression was detected, grade I (minimal) for <10% positivity, grade II (focal) for 10%-50% positivity, and grade III (diffuse) for >50% positivity in peritubular and glomerular capillaries.

Six patients with biopsy-proven glomerulonephritis (3 membranous nephropathy and 3 focal segmental glomerular sclerosis) with positive aPL were considered as controls.

Numeric data with normal distribution were compared using independent samples t tests; numeric data with abnormal distribution of ranked data were compared using Mann–Whitney U tests. Categorical data were compared using χ 2 tests or Fisher exact tests. All statistical analyses were performed with the SPSS statistical package version 29.0 (SPSS Inc). Missing data were included as an additional category in the analysis. P values less than 0.05 were considered significant in 2-sided tests.

This study was conducted according to the Helsinki Declaration and the Piedmont and Aosta Valley (Northwest Italy) legislation for Rare Diseases (N. 1577/UC/SAN of 11.10.2005 based on Regional Government Act 23 April 2007 dealing with Rare Diseases; article. 1: 796 paragraph Z Law number 296 of 2006. Number 5-5740). Each patient provided written consent to participate.

## Discussion

Our findings suggest that Cav-1 staining is strongly associated with APSN-TMA, positioning it as a potential additional marker for its identification. The inclusion of APSN in the new classification criteria for APS highlights the growing recognition of its clinical significance. 18 However, this also underscores the need for improved diagnostic tools to enhance accuracy and better stratify patients with APSN-related TMA. 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 , 23

In our cohort, greater Cav-1 immunoreactivity in both glomerular ( P < 0.001) and peritubular capillaries ( P < 0.05) was particularly evident in APSN-TMA cases compared with those showing hyperplastic vasculopathy or isolated subendothelial edema. Although the study was not powered to detect statistically significant differences in long-term clinical outcomes across staining strata, the co-segregation of high Cav-1 expression with the APSN-TMA phenotype, previously associated with worse kidney prognosis and higher prevalence of triple aPL positivity, suggests that Cav-1 grade may reflect the burden of microvascular injury. 6 This raises the possibility that Cav-1 staining could serve not only as a diagnostic adjunct but also as a prognostic indicator, a hypothesis that merits validation in larger, longitudinal cohorts. In fact, the importance of this association lies in the poor kidney prognosis observed in patients classified within the APSN-TMA cluster. 6 Our previous data demonstrated that this subgroup exhibited a higher frequency of thrombotic APS, consistent with an increased prevalence of triple aPL positivity and elevated adjusted Global Antiphospholipid Syndrome Scores (>12) compared with other clusters. These findings suggest that APSN-TMA may represent the most clinically relevant phenotype within APSN, reinforcing its prognostic implications. In addition, these patterns, first identified by our group through cluster analysis, have been acknowledged in subsequent literature and are currently being evaluated by the American College of Rheumatology/European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology APS Classification Criteria Renal Pathology Subcommittee as part of efforts to standardize definitions of aPL-related nephropathy for future diagnostic and classification updates. 6 , 24

Furthermore, recognizing APSN-TMA as a distinct subgroup may have therapeutic implications. Given its aggressive nature and poor kidney outcomes, patients in this cluster could be considered for additional therapeutic strategies beyond conventional anticoagulation. 25 The ongoing BLAST trial, investigating the role of belimumab in APS, may provide insights into targeted therapeutic options for these high-risk patients. 26

Despite these promising findings, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The sample size of our study is relatively small, necessitating further validation in larger cohorts to confirm the specificity and clinical utility of Cav-1 as a diagnostic marker. Additionally, although Cav-1 expression was strongly associated with the APSN-TMA cluster in our cohort, it is not unique to APSN. Cav-1 immunoreactivity has been reported in other conditions characterized by endothelial injury, including antibody-mediated rejection in kidney transplants, and it shows a similar staining distribution. 16 Given its role as a general marker of microvascular endothelial damage, Cav-1 is likely to stain positively in other forms of TMA not associated with APSN. Our findings therefore suggest that its value lies less in distinguishing APSN from non–APSN-TMA, and more in identifying the APSN-TMA phenotype, which in our series was associated with a poorer kidney prognosis and a higher prevalence of triple aPL positivity compared with other APSN subtypes. This phenotypic precision may be particularly valuable in the heterogeneous field of aPL-related kidney injuries, in which improved subclassification could guide prognostication and therapeutic decision making. Future studies comparing Cav-1 staining patterns in APSN-TMA and other TMA etiologies are needed to further define its diagnostic specificity and prognostic value. In addition, to date, whereas Cav-1 immunoreactivity in renal microvasculature tracks endothelial injury in TMA and antibody-mediated rejection, published studies have not correlated Cav-1 staining with circulating or urinary Cav-1 in kidney disease cohorts. Our findings therefore motivate a paired-biopsy study measuring plasma and urinary (including urinary extracellular vesicles) Cav-1 alongside renal Cav-1 IHC to determine whether biofluid Cav-1 can noninvasively identify the APSN-TMA phenotype and its association with triple aPL positivity and prognosis.

Nevertheless, the distinction between APSN-TMA and other forms of TMA can still be reliably achieved through comprehensive clinicopathological correlations.

In conclusion, our findings support Cav-1 as a promising marker for APSN-TMA, contributing to both diagnostic accuracy and prognostic stratification. Further research is warranted to fully establish its role in clinical practice and to determine whether patients within this cluster may benefit from emerging targeted therapies.
