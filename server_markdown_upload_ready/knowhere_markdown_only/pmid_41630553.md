# Antiphosphatidylserine/prothrombin antibodies define a high-risk antiphospholipid syndrome profile associated with organ damage

https://doi.org/10.1515/cclm-2025-1565

Received November 23, 2025; accepted January 26, 2026

published online February 3, 2026

# Abstract

Objectives: Antiphosphatidylserine/prothrombin (aPS/PT) antibodies are promising non-criteria aPLs associated with antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) manifestations. Their influence on long-term outcomes and damage remains uncertain. To assess aPS/PT antibodies association with accrual damage and severe APS disease phenotypes, focusing on their potential for risk stratification.

Methods: This single-centre cohort study involved 163 patients who fulfilled the 2023 ACR/EULAR APS criteria. IgG/ IgM aPS/PT, aCL, and anti- $\beta 2$ GPI antibodies were measured by commercial ELISA. Lupus anticoagulant (LA) was detected in accordance with the ISTH-SSC recommendation. Clinical data covered macrovascular, microvascular, obstetric, and hematologic manifestations. Damage was assessed using DIAPS.

Results: aPS/PT IgG and IgM were positive in $46.0\%$ and $65.6\%$ , respectively. aPS/PT IgG positivity was linked to microvascular involvement (58.7% vs. 34.1%, p=0.002), obstetric APS (57.4% vs. 24.6%, p<0.0001), valvular disease (17.3% vs. 4.6%, p=0.008), and thrombocytopenia (37.3% vs. 12.5%, p<0.0001). aPS/PT IgM positivity was also associated with microvascular APS (p=0.006), obstetric morbidity (p=0.02), and thrombocytopenia (p=0.01). In multivariable analysis, quadruple aPL positivity (LA + aCL + anti-β2GPI + aPS/PT) was

independently linked to damage (OR 4.3, $95\%$ CI 1.3-14.5; $\mathsf{p} = 0.02$ ), with no other factors remaining significant.

Conclusions: Both IgG and IgM aPS/PT antibodies are associated with severe APS features, including microvascular and obstetric issues, as well as overall organ damage. Adding aPS/PT to standard aPL tests finds a subgroup of quadruple-positive APS patients at higher risk of damage. These results could help guide personalized APS management.

Keywords: antiphospholipid syndrome; antiphosphatidylserine/prothrombin antibodies; lupus anticoagulant; damage; risk stratification; APS criteria

# Introduction

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is an acquired autoimmune disorder characterised by recurrent thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity, in the persistent presence of antiphospholipid antibodies (aPLs) [1]. The current laboratory classification criteria for APS include lupus anticoagulant (LAC), anticardiolipin (aCL), and anti- $\beta2$ -glycoprotein I (anti- $\beta2$ GPI) antibodies [2]. However, a significant subset of patients with typical APS symptoms remains seronegative, highlighting the need for additional biomarkers to improve diagnosis and risk assessment [2, 3]. In this context, antiphosphatidylserine/prothrombin (aPS/PT) antibodies have emerged as clinically relevant "non-criteria" aPLs [4-6]. Studies show that aPS/PT positivity correlates with LA activity and is linked to thrombotic and obstetric APS manifestations [7-11]. Beyond their diagnostic role, aPS/PT antibodies seem to identify a subset of patients with more severe APS. Their presence has been associated with recurrent thrombosis, microvascular involvement, and thrombocytopenia [12], which are known features related to accrual damage [13]. These findings suggest that aPS/PT antibodies may act as independent predictors of disease progression, reflecting a more aggressive and sustained thromboinflammatory state.

Recent updates to the 2023 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for APS underscore the importance of risk stratification based on both laboratory and clinical domains [2].

<!-- page 2 -->
Within this framework, identifying high-risk antibody profiles – such as triple aPL positivity or the coexistence of aPS/PT antibodies – may facilitate earlier recognition of patients at greater risk of relapse and organ damage, thereby supporting personalised management strategies.

Despite increasing evidence, the role of aPS/PT antibodies in the long-term evolution of APS remains poorly understood - their potential contribution to cumulative vascular injury and damage over time warrants systematic investigation.

Therefore, this study aims to investigate the association of aPS/PT antibodies with accrual damage, recurrent thrombosis, and severe disease phenotypes. By combining serological data with clinical and damage indices, this study seeks to establish whether aPS/PT antibodies are reliable biomarkers for risk stratification and long-term outcome prediction in antiphospholipid syndrome.

# Materials and methods

# Study design and population

We conducted a single-center, cross-sectional study. Plasma samples from 163 APS patients fulfilling 2023 ACR/EULAR classification criteria [2] attending the Thrombotic and Bleeding Disorder clinic and Rheumatology Unit at the Department of Medicine, University of Padua, were collected and stored at $-80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ until tested. Patients who tested positive for (aPS/PT) antibodies were confirmed 12 weeks apart.

# Data collection, outcomes, and definitions

Data on demographics, comorbidities, and clinical events were sourced from institutional registries and compiled into an ad hoc electronic database. All charts recorded in the database were reviewed carefully by two authors independently (AH, GG). All reported variables were collected from every subject included in this study. The recorded data included cardiovascular risk factors, autoimmune comorbidities, and congenital thrombophilia. APS manifestations were categorised as macrovascular (venous/arterial), microvascular, obstetric, valvular, and hematologic.

The primary objective of the study was to evaluate the association between aPS/PT antibodies and accrual damage, recurrent thrombosis, and severe disease phenotypes. The secondary aim was to assess the correlation between aPS/PT titres and LAC-normalised ratio, accrual damage, APS Damage Index score (DIAPS), recurrent thrombo-embolism (rTE) event, and clinical classification (CC score) weight.

Moreover, we investigate the impact of aPS/PT antibodies in relation to aCL and anti-2β2GPI in LAC-positive patients.

- Definitions: congenital thrombophilia was considered the protein C deficiency, protein S deficiency, antithrombin deficiency, factor V Leiden, and prothrombin G20210A mutations.   
- Accrual damage is defined as organ/tissue impairment present for at least six months or causing permanent loss and is presented as a categorical variable. Cumulative organ damage was quantified using the DIAPS score [14].   
- The rTE event was defined by either venous or arterial thrombosis, identified through a combination of compatible clinical features and imaging results [15], or microvascular thrombosis, confirmed via histology, suitable imaging, or clinical judgment based on the 2023 ACR/EULAR APS classification criteria [2].   
- The CC score is the sum of the classification-criterion weight points.

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, and all participants provided informed consent. The Institutional Review Board for observational studies and the University Hospital of Padua Audit Committee approved the study design (Protocol Number: 6894/AO/2013).

# Antibody detection

The IgG/IgM aPS/PT antibodies were measured using a commercial, validated [16] ELISA kit (QUANTA Lite®, Inova Diagnostics, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA), following the manufacturer's instructions. The cut-off values, established from 100 sex- and age-matched healthy blood donors in accordance with the ISTH-APS SSC recommendations for the solid-phase assay [17, 18], were 43 and $39~\mathrm{U / mL}$ for IgG and IgM isotypes, respectively. The IgG/IgM aCL and anti- $\beta 2$ GPI antibodies were assessed using a commercial ELISA (Orgentec®, Sebia), according to the manufacturer's instructions.

LAC was detected using a three-step procedure (screening, mixing, confirmation) following ISTH-APS SSC guidelines [18, 19], which employed dRVVT and aPTT-based assays. Cut-off values for positivity were as per manufacturer instructions.

<!-- page 3 -->
The antiphospholipid antibody profile was defined as single-, double-, or triple-positive, as previously described [20]. Briefly, single aPL positivity referred to LAC or IgG/IgM aCL or IgG/IgM anti- $\beta 2$ GPI; double positivity referred to IgG/IgM aCL plus IgG/IgM anti- $\beta 2$ GPI or IgG/IgM aCL plus LAC or IgG/IgM anti- $\beta 2$ GPI plus LAC; triple positivity referred to IgG/

IgM aCL plus IgG/IgM anti- $\beta 2$ GPI plus LAC and tetra positivity referred to IgG/IgM aCL plus IgG/IgM anti- $\beta 2$ GPI plus LAC plus IgG/IgM aPS/PT.

# Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using STATA version 17 software (StataCorp, 2021, Stata Statistical Software: Release 17. College Station, TX: StataCorp LLC). GraphPad Prism version 10.4.1 was utilised to create graphs. Data distribution (normal or not) was assessed through graphical representation and subsequently verified using the Shapiro-Wilk normality test. Data were then presented as the mean (standard deviation) for normally distributed variables and as the median (interquartile range - IQR) for non-normally distributed variables. Categorical variables were expressed as counts (percentages). Chi-square $(\chi^2)$ was used to compare categorical variables, while the $t$ -test or the Mann-Whitney test and the Kruskal-Wallis test for independent samples were employed to compare continuous data. Chi-square p-values were adjusted for multiple comparisons using stringent Bonferroni correction. The degree of the linear association between aPS/PT and anti- $\beta 2GPI$ antibodies and LAC-normalised ratio, as well as that between aPS/PT antibodies and DIAPS score and CC conventional aPL antibodies, was quantified using the Spearman $\rho$ test. Multivariable logistic regression identified independent predictors of accrual damage and severe APS phenotypes (rTE and higher CC score), adjusting for significant univariate factors $(p < 0.10)$ . Venn diagrams were created to represent the distribution of aPL antibodies in LAC-positive patients. A p-value $< 0.05$ was regarded as significant.

# Results

# Study cohort characteristics

One hundred 63 APS patients were enrolled in the study; 119 $(73.0\%)$ were female, and 160 $(98.2\%)$ were Caucasian. The patients' characteristics are outlined in Table 1. The median disease duration was 150 months (IQR, 115-178). More than half of the patients (86; $53.1\%$ ) had at least one cardiovascular risk factor. However, except for obesity, which was found to be significantly associated with aPS/PT IgM $(p = 0.036)$ , there was no association between any of the cardiovascular risk factors and aPS/PT IgG and IgM. One-third of the study cohort had autoimmune diseases, including 32 $(50.7\%)$ with undifferentiated connective tissue disease, 8 $(12.7\%)$ with autoimmune thyroiditis, 5 $(7.9\%)$ with systemic

lupus erythematosus (SLE), 5 $(7.9\%)$ with idiopathic thrombocytopenia, 5 $(7.9\%)$ with primary biliary cholangitis, 2 $(3.2\%)$ with psoriasis, 2 $(3.2\%)$ with myasthenia gravis, 2 $(3.2\%)$ with celiac disease, 1 $(1.6\%)$ with rheumatoid arthritis, and 1 $(7.9\%)$ with recurrent polychondritis. No correlation was observed between aPS/PT IgG and aPS/PT IgM isotypes and autoimmune diseases, respectively. Four out of five $(80\%)$ patients with SLE tested positive for either aPS/PT IgG or IgM antibodies; all exhibited accrual damage. IgG aPS/PT antibodies were detected in 75 $(46.0\%)$ of the APS patients, IgG aCL in 134 $(82.2\%)$ , and IgG anti-β2GPI in 132 $(80.9\%)$ , IgM aPS/PT in 107 $(65.6\%)$ , IgM aCL in 67 $(41.1\%)$ , IgM anti-β2GPI in 73 $(44.8\%)$ , and LA in 139 $(85.3\%)$ .

# Discussion

In this cross-sectional cohort of patients fulfilling the 2023 ACR/EULAR APS classification criteria, we found that aPS/PT antibodies - especially the IgG isotype - identify APS patients with more severe clinical features and significantly higher accrual damage. Our results support the growing body of evidence indicating that aPS/PT is a meaningful "non-criterion" aPL and extend previous findings by showing its association not only with thrombotic and obstetric issues but also with irreversible organ damage, as measured by the DIAPS score.

The prevalence of aPS/PT positivity in our cohort aligns with earlier reports, which suggest that these antibodies commonly coexist with conventional aPLs and are strongly associated with lupus anticoagulant activity [7-12, 21, 22]. Several studies have also demonstrated that aPS/PT antibodies have comparable or even stronger associations with thrombotic events than aCL or anti- $\beta 2$ GPI, particularly in LA-positive individuals [7, 9, 10]. Consistent with these findings, we found that aPS/PT IgG and IgM were significantly connected to microvascular APS, thrombocytopenia, and severe obstetric morbidity - phenotypes previously identified as markers of severe disease and poor long-term outcomes [13].

The strong association between aPS/PT antibodies and microvascular involvement is consistent with earlier findings from our group [10] and Delarue et al. [12], who also identified aPS/PT positivity as a characteristic of more aggressive APS subsets.

Our study adds two important contributions to the literature. First, we demonstrate a robust association between aPS/PT antibodies and accrual damage using the DIAPS index, with nearly $90\%$ of patients presenting with cumulative injury testing positive for either IgG or IgM aPS/PT antibodies. Although previous studies have shown that microvascular disease, thrombocytopenia, and recurrent thrombosis contribute to long-term damage in APS [13, 14], none have specifically linked this progression with aPS/PT antibodies. Our results, therefore, support the hypothesis that these antibodies contribute to persistent thromboinflammation, which is capable of driving irreversible vascular and organ injury over time.

Second, we demonstrated that tetra-positivity – defined as $\mathrm{LA} + \mathrm{aCL} + \mathrm{anti - }\beta 2\mathrm{GPI} + \mathrm{aPS / PT}$ – confers the highest risk of accrual damage among all serological profiles. Although triple positivity is traditionally regarded as the strongest laboratory predictor of adverse outcomes [23, 24], our multivariable model showed that triple positivity alone did

not independently predict damage once aPS/PT was included. These findings align with recent recommendations from ISTH SSC subcommittees, which suggest that aPS/PT testing adds both diagnostic and prognostic value in thrombotic and obstetric APS [8, 9]. Identifying a tetrapositive subgroup may thus represent an evolution beyond the classical triple-positive paradigm and could help refine risk stratification algorithms such as GAPSS.

Recently, Donath et al. [25] demonstrated in a large cohort, including patients with SLE, primary antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), overlap SLE-APS, and non-APS controls, that IgM and IgG anti-PS/PT antibody testing may assist in identifying APS and APS-like conditions, particularly in diagnostically challenging scenarios. This facilitates timely intervention in patients with thrombotic manifestations, underscoring the significance of aPS/PT antibodies in the diagnosis of APS. Conversely, our findings elucidate the implications of anti-PS/PT positivity over time, specifically indicating a higher likelihood of severe disease progression and irreversible organ damage. Collectively, these two studies support a continuum model whereby anti-PS/PT antibodies function both as diagnostic enhancers and as prognostic stratifiers. Such dual functionality reinforces the rationale for integrating anti-PS/PT testing into

<!-- page 8 -->
diagnostic algorithms for APS as well as into longitudinal risk assessment frameworks.

The pathogenic mechanisms linking aPS/PT antibodies with severe APS manifestations may involve enhanced binding of prothrombin to phosphatidylserine on activated platelets and endothelial cells, resulting in increased thrombin generation and potentiation of the coagulation cascade [1, 26, 27]. This procoagulant milieu, combined with endothelial activation and complement involvement, may bridge acute thrombotic events with chronic vascular injury, thereby explaining the strong correlation between aPS/PT titers, microvascular disease, and accrual damage.

Our study also highlights the relevance of aPS/PT evaluation in LA-positive patients. Among LAC-positive individuals, those with concomitant aPS/PT positivity presented higher DIAPS and CC scores than those positive for aCL or anti- $\beta 2\mathrm{GPI}$ alone. This suggests that aPS/PT antibodies may capture an immunothrombotic endotype within the LA-positive spectrum, supporting the ISTH SSC guidance that aPS/PT testing is particularly informative in this subgroup [8, 9, 19].

We acknowledge that our study has limitations. Its cross-sectional design prevents establishing causality, and long-term follow-up is needed to determine whether aPS/PT antibodies predict future damage progression. Additionally, while the monocentric nature ensures consistency in methodology, it may restrict generalisability. Nevertheless, the well-characterised cohort and standardised laboratory procedures strengthen our conclusions.

Overall, our findings reaffirm the clinical importance of aPS/PT antibodies and provide novel evidence linking them to accrual damage. These results suggest that incorporating aPS/PT testing into routine APS assessment could be beneficial, and that tetra-positivity may serve as an initial marker for increased risk. Incorporating aPS/PT antibodies into ongoing risk evaluation frameworks could assist clinicians in identifying patients who may be at higher risk for irreversible organ injury, thus supporting more personalised monitoring and management strategies.

These findings support the integration of aPS/PT testing into APS risk assessment models, which may help improve the identification of high-risk patients and inform management decisions.
